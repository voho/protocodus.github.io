# Ashline

An original 2D real-time strategy game for the browser. Lead an expedition into the ashlands, harvest minerals, build and defend a base, and defeat the opposing command center.

Tephra is a frontier world whose forests died in the Burn, a cascade of rift eruptions that cracked open veins of luminous shard crystal. Under the Charter, a sector belongs to whoever keeps a working Command nexus there; the Ashline is the moving front between claims. You command **Expedition 07** of the Frontier Compact, human settlers and the double-crested Vael, against AI Unity, the Charter's survey network that kept running after the colonial authority withdrew and decided the shards were its own. Crimson forces are always the rival claimant: Red Ledger claim-jumpers in a mirror match against Organics, severed mainframes against Unity.

Choose **Organics** or **AI Unity** independently for your force and its rival. Organics combines human infantry and alien launcher teams with rounded, clunky industrial machinery: bulbous cast turrets, old truck cabins, cylindrical tanks and arched factory roofs. AI Unity fields autonomous combat robots, mobile walkers, skimmers, and intricate assembly facilities. Each race has its own complete nine-unit roster and nine functional buildings; ownership colors remain ivory/cobalt for your force and crimson for the opponent. Their economies and technology paths are comparable, with differences in durability, firepower, mobility, and infrastructure. Older operations load as Organics. See [the recorded AI-versus-AI balance trials](docs/BALANCE.md) for matchup results and reproduction commands.

**Nexus construction vehicles** (Organics) and **Mainframe constructors** (AI Unity) cost 1,800 credits and train at a foundry. Move one to an explored area, select **Deploy nexus**, and choose a clear 3 × 3 footprint within four tiles. Deployment consumes the vehicle with no extra charge, transferring its damage to a new nexus that takes 40 base seconds to complete. Once complete, it grants population capacity and a new construction area for refineries, defenses, and production. AI commanders scout deposits, transport constructors, and establish new mining bases. Losing every nexus and construction vehicle ends the operation.

Control groups are exclusive: Shift/Ctrl + 1–5 assigns the selected entities to that group, replacing its old members and removing the selected units from prior groups. Press 1–5 to recall. A numbered badge beside each assigned friendly unit shows its group; assignments persist in saves.

Both races construct **walls**. Choose the wall card, then drag across the ground to preview a continuous line and its credit cost; release to build. Diagonal drags make connected stair steps, up to 48 segments per order. Placement stops at the first obstruction or unaffordable segment and reports what was actually built. Walls block paths, extend from an existing base or adjacent wall segments, and can be attacked, repaired, or sold. Leave a gate for haulers and deployment bays. Armed attackers can break an enemy wall that obstructs pursuit; siege fire can reach targets beyond it. Escape or the visible Cancel button exits the wall tool.

Build a **Signal laboratory** to research three two-stage branches. Pulse accelerators → Composite field armor improve infantry damage and health; Stabilized armaments → Adaptive drivetrains improve vehicle damage and speed; Efficient power routing → Advanced ballistics reduce electricity demand and unlock advanced weapons. Research applies to deployed and future units, persists in saves, costs credits, and occupies one laboratory per project. Multiple laboratories can work on different projects at once.

Select a structure and choose **Upgrade** to install local improvements: Accelerated operations makes production, research, and refinery work 25% faster; Efficient converters cuts that building’s power consumption by 25%; a foundry’s Advanced assembly bay unlocks **Pike strikers** once Advanced ballistics is researched. These fast six-wheel vehicles carry twin autocannons that counter infantry. Foundries also build **Field engineers**, unarmed tracked support vehicles that automatically repair nearby vehicles and buildings within four tiles, consuming credits and requiring power.

Surplus generation gives construction, training, research, upgrades, and mineral processing a modest speed boost with diminishing returns: `1 + 0.25 × surplus / (demand + 100 + surplus)`, approaching +25%. Repairs, weapons, and unloading retain their normal full-power rates.

**Grid capacitors** store 1,200 power-seconds each, charging from surplus generation at up to 30 per second. During a shortage, reserve power keeps defenses and industry online until storage runs out. Brownouts then disable defenses and scale training, research, upgrades, unloading, processing, and repairs with available power; emergency construction retains a 20% minimum so reactors can recover the grid. The HUD shows current supply/demand, operating percentage, remaining reserve time, and storage. Building lights dim and show restrained amber outage indicators. The AI scouts, chooses counters from observed forces, develops technology, expands toward seen deposits, rebuilds power, escorts armor with engineers, and regroups damaged or outmatched formations.

**Rival commanders** follow one of five doctrines. Pick one in the briefing or leave it on **Random**, which draws a doctrine from the sector seed, so the same seed always meets the same commander. The choice applies to every skirmish mode; a campaign operation ignores it and can set its own doctrine through `createGame`'s `aiProfiles` option (the current operations meet Balanced). AI Unity commanders carry machine designations in place of the Organics names. A Random commander is named only when Signals intercepts one of its columns in view; loading a save never reveals it in the briefing.

| Doctrine | Commander (Organics / AI Unity) | Plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Balanced | Warden Kestrel "Watchfire" / Arbiter WK-9 "Lattice" | A steady economy, a powered perimeter and staged combined-arms waves |
| Ironclad | Marshal Orsa Vantreck "Anvil" / Bastion mind OR-4 "Keel" | Two early foundries, armor and siege guns behind walled sentry lines, then one late, heavy push |

A commander knows only what its forces see and what it remembers. Its rover sweeps the sector, then re-checks the enemy main, remembered shard fields and stale sightings from just outside known tower reach; a damaged rover repairs at a nexus and a lost one is replaced. It keeps recruiting while it saves for an expansion, sites and deploys each new nexus clear of lane gates, fords and passes, holds credits back for priority structures, research and the striker assembly bay, adds foundries when they fall behind its doctrine's mix, and weighs that mix against the forces it has seen: strikers and tanks answer infantry, rocket teams answer armor, siege crawlers answer towers. Waves leave the rally on the doctrine's cadence, take the next remembered objective from wherever they stand, weigh each contact against visible enemies and remembered towers, fall back to a forward regroup point when outmatched and merge with reinforcements there instead of pausing the whole army. Siege crawlers park beyond a remembered tower's reach while a rover or a flare spots it. Shelled from beyond its vision, a commander reads only its struck units and the impacts it can see, confirms the fire on a second look, lights the bearing with a rover or flare, sends fast units along it and moves its rally back. It alternates rail sentries and rocket towers until it has seen a real force, then leans toward the counter to what it saw, fans them across the approach and fortifies its outposts; walls go up in front of towers only where every spawn bay, hauler lane and exit stays open. Its units use their abilities: Overdrive to close or escape, Dig in while holding, Long shot at armor just out of reach, Barrage on remembered towers or visible clusters and field patches for badly damaged vehicles and structures. Haulers attacked anywhere get defenders. Scripted operations can direct a commander to attack a point, hold a zone without raiding, stay at its current bases or field waves of a fixed size, and a commander that loses every nexus fights on with the forces it has; if a construction vehicle survives, it redeploys it at the nearest known shard field clear of recent threats and keeps training from surviving barracks and foundries meanwhile.

New skirmishes default to the **192 × 144 Frontier** battlefield (27,648 tiles), with **144 × 112 Standard** and **224 × 168 Vast** sectors available in the briefing. Choose **Volcanic rift** for lava shores and broken ridges, **Basalt basin** for broad open basalt plains and scattered mesas, **Shattered highlands** for defended passes and valuable flanking expansions, or one of the four curved-lane sectors described below. Each profile changes the actual terrain and resource layout. Layered, domain-warped value noise shapes the relief: gapped ridge walls and mesas of impassable rock, sunken basalt basins, a broken plateau ring around each base whose gates the three guaranteed routes cut, open bowls around every mineral field, and scattered boulder outcrops. The relief is point-symmetric about the map centre, so both bases face the same ground, and every pocket of 30 or more tiles is breached back to the main sector. Rock renders as a raised plate with a lit rim and a shaded foot; basalt as a darker, cooler plate with joint seams; haul ruts only wear into open ground. Seeded crater bowls sit beside approaches and flanking positions: units inside take 15% less direct-fire damage, while rockets and siege explosions ignore the cover. Craters remain passable but cannot support construction; they preserve starting clearings, mineral bowls, and all guaranteed routes. Construct production buildings and defenses, recruit specialized units, and face an AI that develops its economy and adapts its attacks. **Cadet** opposition thinks every 3.5 seconds, builds and trains at 55% of full speed, raids late (a Balanced Cadet first attacks at five minutes, a Swarm Cadet at three, an Ironclad Cadet after eight and a half) with columns of at most eight whatever its doctrine, never fields siege crawlers, researches nothing, and neither regroups its waves nor adapts its army or uses abilities. **Commander** thinks every 2 seconds at 75% speed, adapts its army, uses abilities and falls back from losing fights; Ironclad and Siegebreaker commanders also focus their fire, Swarm commanders hunt haulers and Ironclad commanders wall their sentries. **Veteran** thinks every 1.2 seconds at full speed with the fastest attack cadence, focus-fires with every doctrine, hunts haulers unless it is Ironclad or Siegebreaker, and walls its sentries unless it is Swarm or Siegebreaker.

The four further profiles replace the three sinusoid approaches with seeded, curved lanes: the centre lane is an S-curve through the exact map centre and the two flanks mirror each other, so both commanders face the same shapes. Where a lane crosses an obstacle it narrows to a gate cut exactly to its guaranteed width.

- **Ember channels** chain compact lava pools between rock dikes across the whole sector on each side. Every lane crosses on a narrow basalt ford between rock banks, so each crossing is a chokepoint.
- **Impact steppe** is open ash. Two staggered rows of crater bowls cross every lane on each side, forming ready-made firing lines with crater cover.
- **Deadwood barrens** grow dense dead groves, strongest in belts across the lanes. Each tree keeps open ground around it, so infantry and vehicles can thread a grove but cannot build in it. Rock shoulders narrow the centre lane into a walled pass on each side.
- **Caldera crown** walls the map centre with raised rock around a sunken basalt floor. Gates open where the centre lane passes and toward both flanks. A red seam lies at the exact centre, the same travel distance from both nexuses.

On these profiles, expansions are placed by travel distance: a blue natural close to home, a blue third further out, a red seam where both bases' travel distances agree, then mirrored reserves. Every field keeps a clear site where a nexus can deploy.

Every map names its strategic sites from a seeded word list, for example *Cinder Gate* or *Vael Seam*: the centre or crown, contested seams, each side's natural and third expansions, outposts, passes, fords, gates and crater lines. The names come from their own seeded stream, never the combat stream, and are stored in saves.

Lava pools are recessed into rugged basalt basins, with shaded inner banks and a subtle lit near rim above broad red/orange molten surfaces, swirled amber folds, sparse dark crust, and brief bubbles. The molten texture slowly flows beneath the fixed shoreline and its inner shadows, pausing with the simulation. Pools block movement and construction; units and the AI route around them. Seeded pools preserve starting clearings, mineral access, and the map’s guaranteed routes. Fog hides undiscovered pools and stops live animation outside current vision. Pools persist through save/load, and older saves remain compatible.

Individual dead trees scatter randomly across open ground: bare hardwoods, charred conifers, twisted trees, bleached snags, hollow stumps, and fallen trunks. Their roots block movement and construction while base clearings and guaranteed routes stay open. Varied silhouettes and sizes enrich the ashlands, with soft shadows and branches that fade when they would obscure visible units. Mineral fields form loose, irregular patches with small outlying deposits. Safe mint starter fields preserve the same budget across profiles; blue minerals mark expansion reserves. Exposed red deposits contain twice the credits and yield twice as quickly while mining, with the same 200-credit hauler capacity. Terrain, mineral types, and reserves are mirrored exactly about the map centre, and all deposits connect to the starting bases through open ground. Placement is seeded and remains consistent after loading a save. Start a new operation for the scattered layout; existing saves retain their terrain and deposits.

Barracks train **Rocket infantry** for mobile anti-armor support. Their slow-firing shoulder launchers hit vehicles hard (1.8× against heavy armor) and deal a small amount of splash damage; rifle squads and recon rovers counter them, and tanks only deal half damage to infantry. **Rocket towers** provide longer-range missile defense with a wider blast radius, require a barracks and reactor, and stop firing without sufficient power. Rockets travel before exploding. The AI recruits rocket infantry and builds rocket towers as part of its defenses.

Units earn three ranks through combat: rank 1 at **5 kills**, rank 2 at **10 kills**, and rank 3 at **15 kills**. Each rank adds 20% of base damage and maximum HP, for totals of **+20%, +40%, and +60%**, but only 5% of base movement speed (+5%, +10%, +15%), so veterans keep pace with their formation. Destroyed walls count toward the team's kill total but never toward a unit's rank. Promotion adds the gained maximum HP to current health, preserving damage already taken. Three chevrons below each visible unit show its earned ranks; the selected-unit panel shows kills toward the next threshold and the current bonus. A surviving unit receives credit for its own kills, including splash and rocket impacts. Ranks and kills persist in saves; existing units without a kill count start unranked.

Every completed refinery includes one free hauler. Haulers automatically collect and deliver minerals, and resume harvesting after moving or stopping. Armed units guard by default: they fire at visible enemies in weapon range while holding their position. Moving or stopping returns them to guard when the order finishes. New units never deploy into a sealed pocket between buildings; a producer whose exits are walled in waits until ground opens.

Damage to your forces raises a throttled **under attack** warning with an orange ping on the tactical map, destroyed units are reported, losing the last hauler prompts a replacement, and low power warns once and marks the power readout while defenses stay offline. Shells and tracers fired from concealed positions are drawn and heard where they land, never where they came from.

Battlefield effects are drawn by the renderer only and never touch the simulation. Units glide between the fixed 50 ms ticks: positions and headings blend from the previous tick, enemies only when both their previous and current cells were in sight, and jumps longer than two tiles snap; selection and clicks use the same drawn pose, a paused frame holds still, and resuming never steps a unit back. Shots, rockets and shells finish their flight on screen, and a rocket's blast waits for the rocket to arrive. Artillery damage lands in the simulation when a shell is fired, but the picture follows the shell: the units it hits flash, and the ones it destroys stay in place until it lands and then explode. Explosions you can see open with a brief ground flash and shockwave ring, throw debris with shadows, and shake the camera slightly by size and distance (off under the system's reduced-motion setting, or with `view.screenShake = false`). A destroyed vehicle or structure you saw leaves a charred wreck that fades into the ground; structures add staggered secondary bursts and a short smoke column, and one lost during construction leaves only the part that was built. Hits flash a light silhouette over the sprite, and vehicles below 25% health burn. Sparse ash drifts in three layers with gentle gusts and embers lift off lava, both only over ground in current vision; explored lava casts a faint, static warm light on its banks. Operation zones show a ring, a label and, for active objectives, a pulsing beacon on the battlefield and tactical map. A zone shows through fog only once a revealed objective uses it, the operation marks it lit, or it is the operation's deploy zone (Convoy's claim site); any other zone (a hidden objective's area, a garrison or spawn point) appears once its centre is explored. Named sites appear once explored. Ability effects mark Flare light and its reveal radius (own team only), Dig in sandbags, Overdrive and Afterburner exhaust, Barrage target reticles with incoming-shell markers, Field patch sparkles and the extended Long shot range ring.

Units use [Craig Reynolds’ Boids rules](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/boids/) for local herd movement: **separation** avoids crowding nearby bodies, **alignment** follows nearby allies’ average heading, and **cohesion** draws traveling allies together. Group orders guide the flock toward compact destinations around a shared rally point. Only nearby allies traveling toward compatible destinations align and cohere; opposing traffic keeps its own orders. Comfortable spacing has a deadband so an orderly flock does not continually squeeze together and spread apart. Attraction fades near the destination while collision clearance stays active. Each simulation tick reads a shared snapshot of neighbors from a spatial grid, preserving deterministic save continuation.

Units anticipate crossing traffic, slow behind a leader, and route around nearby bodies. A blocked unit can back out of a crowd; allies still assembling a preserved formation can step aside and return after it passes. Idle military guards remain planted. Friendly spacing only softens between a specific blocked pair when static geometry prevents a bypass in a tight lane. Rocks, lava, buildings, and enemy collision boundaries remain solid. This also keeps haulers moving through shared refinery approaches.

Clear routes follow direct lines at any angle. Obstacle routes discard unnecessary grid waypoints. Units steer and travel at the same time, moving along their current body heading without sliding sideways. They slow for sharp turns and brake when a corner requires more clearance. Stationary units stop their walking and track animations. Every movement checks the swept path against solid ground. The same routing serves manual orders, rallies, harvesting, scouting, and combat pursuit.

Group move and attack-move orders gather scattered units into one compact army around the click. Units keep roughly their left/right and front/back ordering while closing the original gaps. They travel and turn at their own speeds, using local detours, speed matching and yielding to clear traffic. Distinct parking positions leave room for late arrivals to pass between settled allies. Obstructed, clipped or unreachable rally points use nearby available ground on each unit’s side of the obstacle. Repeating the same order for the same selection keeps its reserved positions stable; a different selection can regroup. Selected units show their own destination brackets and subtle route segments through visible ground.

To preserve a particular formation, hold the right mouse button at its destination and drag around that point. A live preview rotates the selected layout through a full 360 degrees; release to move into that layout and face the chosen direction. The layout is captured when the gesture begins, so moving units cannot change the preview underneath it. Preserved formations share travel and turning limits while avoiding obstacles and finish assembling before becoming idle. A quick right-click continues to gather a compact flock, with each unit becoming idle as it arrives.

Military units default to the **Guard** idle stance: they stay on their exact spot and fire at the closest visible enemy within firing range. Choose **Defend** in the selection panel to let an idle unit retaliate against an enemy that fires on it. It may chase that visible attacker, but stays within one firing-range radius of its original position and returns there when retaliation ends. Move, attack, attack-move, and explore orders take priority over Defend; its saved preference resumes only when the unit is idle, from its new position. Selected military units show their firing range as a subtle translucent circle. Stances and ongoing retaliation survive saving and loading.

Select units and toggle **Explore** (X) to automatically scout reachable unexplored areas. Armed explorers stop to fire at nearby visible enemies and resume exploring afterward. Haulers can explore too; they resume harvesting when exploration ends. Toggle Explore again, stop, or issue a manual move, attack, or harvest order to end auto-explore. When no reachable unexplored areas remain, armed units resume their chosen idle stance.

Every barracks, vehicle foundry, and refinery has its own rally point. Select it and right click a destination, or choose **Rally** (R) and click/tap. New units reserve separate positions around that rally point; combat units attack move there, while haulers relocate and resume automatic harvesting. Each building trains one unit at a time, independently of the others. Recruiting with a compatible producer selected adds to that producer’s queue; otherwise recruitment chooses the available producer with the least remaining work. Card badges show total queued items, with a progress bar for each active producer, and the production list identifies each working building.

Buildings show their actual activity. Production bays contain the current queued unit as it assembles or trains; empty queues leave a dim, empty bay with its machinery stopped. Refineries process delivered minerals with a filling hopper, moving conveyor, and exhaust. Haulers visibly carry empty, partial, or full loads and drain their cargo during unloading. Command scanning, reactor fans, and sentry scanning/recoil remain distinct. Animations pause with the simulation and respect fog and power.

Select a building to **Repair** or **Sell** it. At full power, repair restores 2% of maximum health per second, spends credits only for actual healing, waits when funds run out, and stops at full health; click again to stop early. The panel shows the actual power-adjusted rate and cost. Restoring an entire health bar costs half the building price. Construction must finish before repair starts. Selling immediately returns half the building’s and installed upgrades’ value scaled by remaining health, plus all paid unit queues, active research, and unfinished upgrades; the button shows the exact refund. Destruction loses those investments. A refinery’s deployed included hauler keeps its cargo and resumes work, with its value excluded from the building refund. The command nexus can be repaired but cannot be sold.

The pause menu saves one operation to this browser’s local storage and loads it again, including the exact simulation, fog knowledge, production queues, rally points, active repairs, camera, economy, and AI. The briefing offers **Load saved operation** on the next visit. Loading pauses the restored operation until you resume. A loaded skirmish shows its settings in the setup form; a loaded campaign operation leaves that form alone, and its **Retry** and **Next operation** keep the opposition it was launched at. Existing 72 × 56 and 144 × 112 saves remain playable with their stored terrain, mineral positions, and depletion unchanged; older deposits retain their mint material. Start a new skirmish to choose a larger battlefield and terrain profile. Saving again replaces that slot; clearing browser site data removes it.

Every sound effect is synthesized in code; no samples or recordings are used. Organics weapons have gritty powder reports and mechanical clanks, while AI Unity fires pulse, rail and FM tones, each with several variants and slight pitch variation. Artillery shells land audibly at their target after their flight, and the kills a shell makes are heard on landing, with the blast the battlefield shows then. Rockets burst on impact, and losses scale with what was destroyed: infantry falls with a thud, Unity robots break apart, vehicles burst and buildings collapse. Combat is placed against the camera: sounds pan with their screen position, and beyond the view they fade and grow muffled. You hear only what your forces can see. A hidden shooter is heard only at its visible impact point, hidden launchers and guns stay silent, and an artillery landing is checked against your vision again when it arrives. Alerts and the interface stay centred.

Event stingers mark attacks on your forces, losses, completed research and upgrades, promotions won in sight, power loss and recovery, objectives, incoming waves and supply drops, ability use, first contact with a visible rival unit and signals intercepts. A scripted transmission is voiced when the objective tracker puts it on screen, so queued lines are heard in turn, and each speaker of the operation keeps one voice. Alerts and voices briefly duck the battle and the soundtrack. Your units answer selections and orders with short wordless radio lines, one reply per command, and the comms line shows the same reply as text. Human crews speak through squelched two-formant syllables, the Vael launcher teams in a lower growl, and Unity machines in bit-crushed chirps. Selecting the same squad again and again draws an annoyed reply. Ambient beds follow the camera: wind rises as you zoom out, visible lava rumbles and bubbles, and busy friendly refineries and production bays hum. The soundtrack stays muffled and quieter in calm play and opens up as visible combat intensifies. It fades out when you pause and under the victory or defeat stinger.

Sound begins after a user gesture. The pause menu's settings area holds the independent **Effects** and **Music** toggles and the **Master**, **Music**, **Effects**, **Voices** and **Ambient** volume sliders. This browser remembers the mix (`ashline.audio.v1`), apart from the interface settings. The effect bank is rendered in a background worker after the first gesture, starting with interface and weapon sounds, so enabling audio never stalls the game. The locally hosted synthwave loop **Space Adventure** by **MintoDog** is CC0; its creator’s license and source are recorded in [audio credits](assets/audio/CREDITS.md).

Each completed nexus adds **200 unit slots**, up to **2,000 units per side**, including haulers, engineers, and construction vehicles. Losing a nexus lowers capacity without removing existing units; recruitment waits until there is room again. Paid production queues reserve population slots so parallel factories cannot exceed the limit. Buildings and walls do not consume unit capacity.

## Campaign, modes and records

The briefing has two tabs. **Skirmish** is the classic setup, with the rival commander and a **Mode** choice; **Campaign** plays **Operation Ashline**, eight scripted operations with Expedition 07:

| # | Operation | Goal | Cleared for the first time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Landfall | Tutorial without an enemy commander: walk to a marker, build a barracks, train rifles, deliver shards, clear a Unity picket | Reactor, refinery, barracks, rifles, rovers, haulers |
| 2 | Hold the Line | Survive eight minutes of escalating raids from hidden map edges and raise two Rail sentries | Walls, Rail sentries, Grid capacitors, Vael rocket infantry |
| 3 | Signal in the Ash | Destroy a Logic archive inside a fortified outpost; killing its Resonance spire browns out the Lance nodes | Laboratory, foundry, tanks, engineers, four research projects |
| 4 | Convoy to Cinder Gap | Escort the construction vehicle and bring a nexus online inside the claim site; the vehicle deploys only there, and a Unity counterattack strikes while the nexus builds | — |
| 5 | Red Ledger | Against crimson Organics claim-jumpers and a full commander: bring three nexuses online and deliver red seam crystal | Construction vehicles, grid research, building upgrades |
| 6 | Dead Signal | Commando strike with no base: three spires, then the relay mainframe. Its 400 credits only buy the engineer's repairs. It fails when the last armed unit falls. Ranked survivors become veterans | Nothing: the strike team is all you have |
| 7 | Hold the Relay | Hold the lit central relay for four minutes in total before Unity does, or void its claim; the commander plays one opposition level above your setting | Everything else |
| 8 | Severance | Destroy every Unity mainframe, including two outlying cores; Dead Signal veterans deploy with their ranks, and keeping them alive is a secondary objective only when some deploy | — |

Each operation has a fixed sector, seed and par time. Its briefing lists the story, visible objectives, newly cleared equipment and your best result; **Remix sector** plays it on a freshly generated map, and its zones follow the map layout so any seed works. Campaign operations start paused on their briefing: press **Begin operation**. Commanders, launcher chiefs, Charter auditors and Unity itself speak through transmissions, which queue and play in order when several arrive together; objectives reveal as the operation unfolds, and secondary and classified objectives reward a thorough commander. Scripted waves scale with the chosen opposition (Cadet 70%, Commander 100%, Veteran 130%). Construction and recruitment list only what the operation clears.

Skirmish modes: **Annihilation** (destroy every hostile nexus and construction vehicle), **Relay control** (the map-centre relay is lit for both sides; hold it for five minutes in total, or void the rival claim; the rival wins by holding it first) and **Last Light** (no rival base: endless, growing waves arrive from map edges you cannot see, at most 150 attackers alive at once; score is seconds survived × kills ÷ 10, with a local best; during play it counts only kills you saw, and the final score counts every kill).

The objective tracker at the top left shows progress, timers, credits and completion ticks, centres the camera on a zone objective's marker with its ⌖ button, starts collapsed to one line on phones and short landscape screens (where it also stays above the tactical map), and in Annihilation offers optional **Commander's goals** drawn from your own match statistics. Kill counts shown or acted on during play (commander's goals, kill objectives, kill-triggered transmissions, the Last Light score) include only kills you saw; one made out of sight, such as by splash or a barrage on remembered ground, is confirmed when the operation ends. Destroy objectives count only losses you saw: a group of structures placed with the operation shows its total (for example 1 / 3 spires), while a group that includes units shows only how many you destroyed in sight, so the tracker never counts forces under fog. Loss limits count each limited category against its own limit. The pause menu repeats the current objectives and the fail conditions, and offers **Retry operation** during a campaign operation. When an operation ends, the debrief shows time, units trained and lost, structures built and lost, credits mined and spent, research, peak army and kills beside the rival's figures, plus a score and letter grade. Campaign medals: **bronze** for victory, **silver** when every secondary objective is also complete, **gold** for silver within par. **Next operation**, **Retry** and **Remix sector** follow a campaign debrief. The **Field archive**, in the briefing and the pause menu, describes Tephra, its claimants and every unit and structure of both races.

Progress (unlocks, medals, best times, veterans, Last Light best) is stored in this browser under `ashline.campaign.v1`, and career totals under `ashline.career.v1`. Both are optional: without storage the game still plays, and clearing site data resets them.

## Run locally

From the repository root:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Open <http://localhost:8000/fun/ashline/>. No build step or installation is required.

Previews that permit local JavaScript modules can also open `index.html` directly: when workers are blocked, map generation falls back to the main thread after painting the loading screen. Use the local server in browsers that block modules from `file:` URLs.

Selections containing any military unit automatically exclude haulers, including drag selection, Shift selection, and control groups. Select haulers on their own to command them together.

Messages stack in a short log: up to four lines at the top left on larger screens (beside the objective tracker when one is shown, or below it on tablets); on phones, up to three just above the selection panel, or only the newest while the console is open. Scripted transmissions and objective news (new, complete, failed) belong to the objective tracker instead; a folded tracker flashes its header when an objective changes. Incoming waves and supply drops stay in the log. Each line fades on its own, repeats collapse into one line with a count, and a full log drops routine news before warnings. Screen readers hear each burst once through a polite live region, with the same repeat counts and a short summary when a burst runs long; the same region reads each transmission as the tracker shows it, and objective news. Lines about a place end in a ⌖ mark: click the mark, or press Backspace, to center on it, while the rest of the line lets clicks through to the battlefield; the tactical map pings each recent alert, which only ever marks your own units and structures or a mission zone. The topbar **Idle** and **Bays** buttons count armed units waiting more than 12 tiles from your structures, idle engineers and construction vehicles, empty production bays and laboratories with research to start. The selection panel's ability button shows the ability name, its recharge sweep with the seconds left in place of its icon, and why it cannot fire; ground targeting follows the selection, so choosing units with another ability (or none) leaves it; a mixed selection uses the largest group that shares an ability. Hovering a console card shows its base damage per second (damage ÷ firing interval), range, health, speed, armor class and the armor classes it is strong or weak against; hovering the battlefield names visible units and structures with their health and rank. Selected structures report their actual power-adjusted training, research, processing and charging rates.

Every unit has a callsign derived from the sector seed and its id, so it survives saving without being stored: Organics rifle squads answer to words and numbers such as *Larkspur 8*, Vael launcher teams carry a given name and a clan, vehicle crews nickname their machines, and AI Unity numbers its machines by cohort and serial. Selecting, ordering, readying and promoting your units brings a short reply on the comms line above the tactical map (on phones at the top left below the objective tracker and idle buttons; on short landscape screens at the top right). The line repeats the unit's spoken reply, so a click gets one answer in voice and text; the second click of a double click does not answer again, readiness waits for a quiet radio, and replies never come from enemies, and a crew only boasts about a promotion when you saw the kill. Promotion and veteran-loss messages name the unit. A promotion earned on a kill you did not see is not announced when it happens: the message waits, without a location, until you next select that unit (a unit selected at the time must be selected again), and it is dropped if you reload first. Such a kill likewise joins the unit's kill count in the selection panel only when you next select it; the unit's chevrons still update at once, because the simulation credits the kill. When five or more armed rivals you can currently see close on one of your structures, signals intercept names the commander leading them (in Red Ledger, Captain Dace Mor) and points at the threatened structure.

The briefing chooses the rival commander (Random, or one of the AI doctrines with its commander and approach) and the skirmish mode. The pause menu's settings area also toggles edge scrolling, screen shake and card tooltips; these and the game speed persist in this browser under `ashline.settings.v1`, kept apart from the sound mix.

On touchscreens, tap to select, then tap a destination or target. Drag empty ground to pan; pinch to zoom. The production console starts collapsed and closes after choosing a structure so its placement stays visible.

## Testing

Run every check from this directory with one command:

```sh
node tests/run-checks.mjs            # simulation checks and node:test suites
node tests/run-checks.mjs --browser  # also every Playwright browser check
```

The runner discovers the scripts in `tests/`: plain checks run as `node tests/<name>.mjs`, `*.test.mjs` suites run with `node --test`, and scripts that drive Playwright are browser checks. Node checks run in parallel lanes, one per CPU by default (`--jobs N`); browser checks run afterwards, up to three at a time depending on the CPU count (`--browser-jobs N`), and `--browser-only` skips the node checks. With `--browser` it serves the repository root on a free local port and sets `ASHLINE_URL`, so no separate server is needed; an `ASHLINE_URL` you set yourself is used instead. It uses `ASHLINE_PLAYWRIGHT` when set, otherwise the `playwright` package, `NODE_PATH` or the global npm root, and runs Google Chrome when it is installed, otherwise Playwright's Chromium (`ASHLINE_BROWSER` overrides the channel).

Each check writes a log, and browser checks write screenshots to their own subfolder of `screenshots/` beside the logs (a new folder in the system temporary directory unless `--logs DIR` is given). Failures print their output, or the failing node:test blocks, as they finish. A closing table lists every check with its status, duration, subtest count and log (`summary.json` in the log folder holds the same results for scripts), and the command exits non-zero if any check fails or times out. Other words select checks by name: `node tests/run-checks.mjs --browser faction map` runs only the checks whose names contain them. `--list` prints what was discovered, `--timeout S` stops a check after S seconds (default 900), and `--retries N` re-runs failures one at a time and reports checks that passed only on a retry. The measurement tools `performance-benchmark`, `race-balance` and `render-benchmark` run only with `--tools`, last, one at a time and without a time limit; see [performance](docs/PERFORMANCE.md) and [balance](docs/BALANCE.md) for their options.

## Implementation

See [performance measurements and reproduction commands](docs/PERFORMANCE.md) for simulation, rendering, and canvas-memory comparisons. Fixed 50 ms simulation steps yield between expensive ticks to keep input and rendering responsive; under sustained overload, effective game speed can fall below the requested slider setting. Returning to setup, replacing a world, or failing deployment releases the old terrain and fog surfaces immediately.

AI perimeter defense assigns nearby troops according to visible threats, armor effectiveness, and powered defenses in actual firing range. It preserves distant raids, stops chasing intruders that leave the perimeter, and withdraws outmatched defenders locally. Critically damaged military units seek a nearby nexus for repairs; threatened haulers can shelter at nearby refineries. These decisions use current vision and preserve deterministic save continuation. Each think builds one index of the commander's own forces and the enemies it currently sees, instead of scanning every entity per question; the two commanders of an AI-versus-AI match think half an interval apart, and orders a unit already holds are not issued again. Wave, raid, scouting and fire-response plans are plain saved state (`ai.waves`, `ai.harass`, `ai.intel`, `ai.shelled`), validated on load.

The setup menu is a static DOM screen. Changing seed, faction, size, or terrain updates the form without creating a world. Deploy and Load open a progress screen before art preparation; `world-worker.js` normally generates new simulation state away from the UI thread, with a main-thread fallback when workers are unavailable or blocked. Both paths use the same deterministic generator. The renderer bakes terrain in chunks that yield for painting through `loading.js`. Progress follows completed assets and terrain stages. The game loop starts only after preparation and stops in the pause and setup menus. Oxanium supplies the space-inspired heading face; its self-hosted font and license are in [assets/fonts/CREDITS.md](assets/fonts/CREDITS.md).

The game runs directly from static files. `sim.js` owns the deterministic simulation: entities, orders, movement, combat, economy, power, fog and victory. `terrain.js` generates the seeded, point-symmetric maps from local hash-seeded streams, so map generation never shifts the shared simulation random stream; `sim.js` re-exports its map sizes, profiles and layout. Per-profile generator knobs live in `PROFILE_RELIEF`. `mapRoutes(state)` returns the guaranteed routes as polylines with a per-point radius (the classic sinusoids or the curved lanes), which the renderer's haul roads follow. `mapReport(state)` measures a sector's fairness: travel distance between the bases, open lanes and their narrowest gates, the widest bottleneck, buildable ground near each base, and each expansion's distance from both bases and whether a nexus can deploy there. A curved-lane map that breaks the report's bounds is regenerated under a salted seed (`seed:attempt-n`, at most four attempts), so generation stays deterministic. `state.sites` holds the named sites as plain `{id, kind, x, y, r, name, side?}` data. The generation worker transfers the typed grids to the page instead of copying them. `ai.js` plays the opposing commander through the same public commands as the player. It reads the enemy only through current vision, impacts that land in its sight, its own fog memory and the map's structural starting positions; expansion sites are judged on explored ground by terrain, by its own and remembered structures and, under fog, by where the sector generated ore, and kept clear of the chokepoints named in `state.sites`, never by the shared blocked grid or live ore amounts, which also reflect unseen buildings and mining. It defines the commander doctrines that `createGame` accepts through its `aiProfiles` option (`'random'` resolves from the seed and team), and `doctrineOptions` and `aiCommander` describe them for the interface. `render.js` draws the battlefield with Canvas 2D: `main.js` hands it a pose snapshot before every tick and the scheduler's leftover time before every frame, so bodies, effects and animation blend between ticks without changing the simulation. `main.js` connects pointer/keyboard/touch input to the compact DOM command console. `hud-data.js` derives the console's event routes, card statistics, idle forces and stored settings, and `character.js` supplies callsigns, barks and intercepts; both only read the game, never the shared random stream. `save.js` validates and restores the versioned local save. No runtime packages or network services are required.

Sound is split into three modules. `soundbank.js` holds pure synthesis recipes that tests can render in Node. `audio.js` is the Web Audio engine. `soundscape.js` connects the game to the engine. The engine renders the bank lazily in `sound-worker.js`, falling back to short main-thread slices when module workers are unavailable. It routes voices through interface, voice, weapon, impact, alert, ambient and music buses into a glue compressor and a brickwall limiter. Its voice manager never drops alerts or result stingers and keeps an interface reserve. Unit lines are monophonic, and weapons and impacts are capped, with the quietest, most finished voice stolen first. Cues issued while the context resumes, or before their buffer is ready, wait briefly instead of being lost. `soundscape.js` scans new effects after every simulation step, so one-tick shots are never missed at high speed, then merges identical cues by area once per drawn frame. It only reads the state and gates every sound about another team on the player's current vision. It is the only source of event sounds: the message log is silent, events that echo a click (placements, sales, project starts, cancellations) leave the sound to that click's own cue, and scripted dialogue is voiced through `transmission(line)` when the objective tracker shows the line, from an explicit voice table for the operation's speakers. Blasts and shell landings play when the renderer draws them (`renderer.blastTime` against `renderer.drawClock`), so a shell's kills are heard as it lands. A promotion stinger follows the log's rule and sounds only for a kill the player saw. Unit replies to commands (`acknowledge`) and to their own readiness or ability use come from the soundscape, and the HUD shows each on the comms line.

Simulation events carry a stable `kind` beside their message text, and events about a team's own units or structures include their position, so the interface and audio can react without parsing copy or revealing anything under fog. Each team also keeps match statistics: units trained and lost, structures built and lost, unit and structure kills (and how many of each the team did not see happen), mined and spent credits, damage dealt and taken, peak armed-unit count and completed research. Starting forces are not counted as trained, refinery haulers delivered later are; cancelled research and cancelled training return their spending, while sales count as salvage. Saves from before these records still load and continue; their statistics stay unavailable. `cancelTraining` removes any queued unit, including the one in training, for a full refund.

`abilities.js` gives each combat role one active ability, with the same mechanics under each race's name: **Dig in / Brace protocol** (rifles take 35% less damage for 10 s while holding still; a move, attack or explore order ends it, while Stop and stance changes keep it), **Long shot / Extended lock** (rockets gain 3 tiles of range for 8 s), **Flare / Sensor probe** (a scout lights a 7-tile radius within 14 tiles for 12 s, for its own team only), **Overdrive / Overclock** (tanks move 40% faster for 6 s), **Afterburner / Sprint** (strikers move 60% faster for 4 s), **Barrage / Arc barrage** (siege crawlers fire four scattered shells at explored ground within range + 2, 0.7 s apart, each at 70% damage with splash) and **Field patch / Nano-patch** (an engineer restores 25% of the most damaged friendly vehicle or structure within 4 tiles, paid at the engineer repair rate). Cooldowns run from 30 to 45 s. Timers are absolute simulation times on the unit, flare reveals are saved team-owned fog sources, and barrage scatter draws from the shared simulation stream only while a salvo fires.

`mission.js` runs scripted operations defined as data in `campaign.js`. `createGame(seed, difficulty, {mission})` applies the operation's races, AI teams, credits and opening forces, resolves its zones against the map layout so any seed works, and runs its setup. Four times a second the engine evaluates objectives (destroy or protect tagged entities, annihilate, survive, hold or reach a zone, deploy a nexus in a zone, deliver credits, research, build, train, kills), fires triggers (dialogue, reinforcements and hostile waves, objective reveals, supplies, commander directives) and settles victory or defeat; losing the nexus, every unit (or every armed unit, with `armed: true`), a tagged asset or the time limit can fail an operation. `deployZone` confines the player's nexus deployment to a zone, so an operation's only construction vehicle cannot be spent where the claim would not count. A delivery quota revealed during an operation counts only deliveries made after it appears. A protect objective whose group never deployed does not apply: it is not listed and does not count toward medals. Tagged losses on ground the player sees are recorded as witnessed (`noteTagLost`, called as an entity dies), and destroy-objective progress and `tagsLeft` triggers use only those, while completion remains the mission's ruling on the real state. Operations can restrict which structures, units, research and upgrades the player may use. Triggers fire once, or repeat with `when.every` (seconds; `time` sets the first fire, `limit` and `until` end it) for escalating waves whose unit entries grow per wave; they can also wait for another trigger (`after`), a kill count, a number of tagged entities left or an objective still open (`objectiveActive`, so a hint never trails its step), rally idle units, and arrive at `fogEdge`, a far map edge the opposing side does not currently see. A `directive` action changes only the keys it names. Lit zones are revealed to both sides, so a contested relay never depends on hidden units; a `rivalHold` fail rule ends the operation when the rival holds one long enough. A wave's alert points at its arrival zone only when the player may already see that zone, by the same rule that draws zones; other arrivals carry no position. Scripted spawns never exceed the population cap, and every authoring bound matches save validation, so a definition that starts can always be saved and loaded. Mission progress is plain saved state; skirmishes carry none and keep the Charter rule: a side without a nexus or construction vehicle loses. `campaign-ui.js` runs the briefing tabs, the skirmish mode select, launches, pre-start pause, end-menu actions, progress and career storage and the Field archive; `objectives-hud.js` draws the tracker and queues transmissions, calling its `transmit` hook as each line comes on screen; `debrief.js` scores a finished operation from the saved state and team statistics, reporting rival figures only after the end.

Units and buildings use generated military sprites with smooth contours, broad armor panels, substantial weapons and restrained mechanical detail designed for gameplay size. Unit frames are baked at 64–128 pixels into small lossless WebP atlases before shipping; building frames are prepared at 80–208 pixels during loading. These sizes serve high-density displays while keeping existing battlefield sizes. Filtered preparation and sprite drawing keep roofs, turns, zoom, portraits, and production previews smooth. Friendly units and buildings have ivory armor with cobalt panels and blue square insignia; enemies have broad crimson armor and red diamond insignia. These colors and shapes carry through production previews, cargo/idle states, and the minimap. `assets.js` slices the prepared unit atlases without resizing or recoloring friendly frames, derives enemy colors and shadows, normalizes building sprites, and supplies the textured terrain. The battlefield fills the viewport; a collapsible production console and contextual selection controls preserve space for play. Asset provenance and the complete built-in image-generation prompt set are in [assets/generated/ASSETS.md](assets/generated/ASSETS.md).

Every unit uses **eight separately generated direction views**, spaced 45° apart, for both factions. Infantry also has eight walking poses. While turning and moving, it displays the closest view to its continuous simulation heading. Each sheet shows the same unit from a fixed shallow overhead camera, including distinct front and rear surfaces. The renderer uses those views directly, preserves whole silhouettes and shared scales, and keeps shadows pointing lower-right. Attached effects follow the selected direction, and hauler cargo fills each view's hopper through a bounded cache. The camera check covers source integrity, nearest-direction selection, full turns, animation registration, transparency, and desktop/mobile previews at normal and minimum zoom.

Units and buildings cast soft silhouette shadows from a fixed upper-left light. Shadows draw on the ground before objects, grow with building construction, and respect fog of war. Prepared shadow images are cached with the sprites.

### Rebuilding unit atlases

Keep original directional art in `assets/generated/directions/`. The game downloads only `assets/prepared/units/`: 18 lossless sheets totaling 1.30 MB instead of 16.16 MB. Each cell uses the existing renderer's prepared resolution and normalization, preserving physical size, anchors, native maximum zoom, and portrait detail. The manifest stores original draw scales and hopper polygons in prepared pixel coordinates. `source-bounds.json` records extraction bounds for source-art QA and is not loaded during play.

After changing a unit master, source normalization, friendly paint, or hopper bounds, rebuild with an existing Playwright/Chrome installation and `cwebp` from libwebp. Run from `fun/ashline/`; the tool starts its own temporary local server. `ASHLINE_BROWSER` and `CWEBP` can override the browser channel and encoder executable.

```sh
ASHLINE_PLAYWRIGHT=/path/to/playwright/index.mjs node tools/prepare-unit-atlases.mjs
ASHLINE_PLAYWRIGHT=/path/to/playwright/index.mjs node tools/prepare-unit-atlases.mjs --check
```

The check regenerates into a temporary directory and compares all outputs without modifying them. Commit the updated sheets and metadata together. `tests/resource-check.mjs` verifies the download/pixel budgets, exact consumption of baked frames, unchanged maximum zoom, and successful loading with source masters blocked. Unit masters remain available for future art changes but are never fetched during game startup or play.
