# Meteora models

Every file here is generated by the Blender scripts in
`fun/meteora/tools/blender/`. Do not edit the GLBs, `js/anchors.js` or
`js/anchors/*.js` by hand: change the scripts and rebuild. The original
rocks and ships are described first; the campaign's ships, stations and
capital ships follow under [Campaign models](#campaign-models).

```
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --factory-startup \
  --python fun/meteora/tools/blender/build_all.py
```

`build_all.py` runs `build_asteroids.py`, then `build_ships.py` (which also
writes `js/anchors.js`), then `render_previews.py` (one 512² Eevee still per
model in `tools/blender/previews/`). The whole build takes about 3 minutes
on an Apple-silicon Mac. Each script also runs on its own, and the build
scripts accept model names after `--` to rebuild only those. The scripts are
deterministic (fixed seeds), so a rebuild reproduces the files. Eevee needs
a GPU context, so the preview step must run where Blender can reach Metal
(not inside a sandbox that hides the GPU).

Textures are baked in Cycles (CPU) from procedural node materials and
embedded as WebP (`EXT_texture_webp`, listed in `extensionsRequired`; three's
`GLTFLoader` reads it natively). No Draco, no meshopt. Every textured
material has base colour, a tangent-space normal map (tangents exported),
and metallic-roughness (G = roughness, B = metallic); the ships also carry
an emissive map.

### Asteroids

Each rock is sculpted on a subdivided icosphere (6 levels for rocks 1–2, 7
for 3–5): an overall body (ellipsoid, contact-binary lobes with boulders, a
bent potato, a jointed block), domain-warped fBm, planar fracture facets,
bedding strata on the block, and craters of several scales with raised
rims. `lod0` is decimated from a lightly smoothed copy of the sculpt (so
the collapse cannot fold over fine spikes; any fold left is collapsed) and
`lod1` from `lod0`; `lod1`'s UVs are re-projected island by island onto
`lod0`'s layout, so both nodes share one material. Normals, colour (carbonaceous grey-brown, paler
regolith in pits and crater floors, greyer fresh scarps, sparse glints) and
roughness are baked from the sculpt onto `lod0`, with micro-craters and
cracks added as bump detail. Both nodes are centred on the origin; `lod0`'s
largest coordinate equals the nominal radius (1.5, 4, 12, 40, 125 m).

| `asteroid_3` | elongated, bent potato with one deep crater |
| `asteroid_4` | blocky, jointed, layered with ledges |
| `asteroid_5` | battered, heavily cratered |

### Ships

The parts are bmesh primitives — a lofted fuselage, lathed engines, nozzles
and pods, airfoil wings and fins, slabs and boxes — joined into one `hull`
mesh. Normals are baked from a bevelled copy (rounded edges, recessed panel
lines); colour, roughness, metallic and emission are baked from the hull's
paint materials (tri-planar panel lines, AO grime, aft streaks, chipped
edges).

- `fighter.glb`: 14.4 m from nose to nozzle exits (15.1 m with the nose
  probe), 11.3 m span. Off-white `#d9d6cf` and graphite `#2b2d31` with an
  orange `#c8642a` accent; dark tinted glass canopy as the separate
  `glass` node. Detailed to be seen close in the hangar: split flaps,
  ailerons and rudders with hinge gaps; cheek intakes with ducts, fans and
  splitter plates; sixteen-petal nozzles over a turbine face and liner,
  heat-sink rings; gimballed wingtip guns with slotted shrouds and muzzle
  brakes; missiles on swept pylons; three-nozzle RCS clusters; a canopy
  frame, sensor turret, antennas and beacons. The paint adds rivet rows,
  hatches, the "01" of Lantern One, Lantern Wing and Watch markings,
  stencils and warnings (from a decal image the bake reads), soot and wear.
- `interceptor.glb`: 11.2 m long. Gunmetal `#4a4f55` and oxblood `#5e1a1d`
  with hazard stripes; red emissive sensor eye.

## Axis rule

The scripts model in Blender space: nose along +Y, top along +Z, right
along +X. The glTF exporter's +Y-up conversion maps Blender `(x, y, z)` to
glTF `(x, z, -y)`, so a ship arrives nose along three.js −Z, top +Y,
right +X (the game's ship frame) with no runtime correction.

## Anchors

Anchors are empties parented to `hull` and exported as glTF nodes.
`build_ships.py` converts each one's world transform with the rule above
and writes them to `js/anchors.js` (`ANCHORS.fighter`,
`ANCHORS.interceptor`), in metres in glTF space.

| node | meaning | direction |
|---|---|---|
| `nozzle_L`, `nozzle_R` (fighter), `nozzle_C` (interceptor) | centre of the nozzle exit | `dir` = exhaust: the empty's Blender local −Y, glTF ≈ +Z |
| `muzzle_L`, `muzzle_R` | gun barrel tips (fighter: wingtip pods; interceptor: under the wing roots) | — |
| `rcs_01` … `rcs_16` | attitude-thruster ports, just outside each thruster block | `dir` = puff: the empty's Blender local +Z, out of the hull; the port pushes the ship along −`dir` |
| `cam_cockpit` | nose-camera eye point (`cockpit` in `anchors.js`) | — |

`radius` in `anchors.js` is the largest distance of any hull or glass vertex
from the origin (fighter 7.65 m, interceptor 5.75 m).

Both ships carry 16 RCS ports: nose up, down, left and right; tail up,
down, left and right; four wingtip ports (up and down on each side); two
forward-facing nose ports for reverse thrust; and two aft-facing tail ports.
Every linear direction and every rotation direction has at least two ports
that can push it, which the models check verifies.

## Campaign models

The campaign's craft come from six group scripts, each building its own
models with the shared helpers in `common.py` and `build_ships.py`, and each
writing one anchors module per model to `js/anchors/<model>.js`
(`js/hardpoints.js` merges them with `js/anchors.js` for the game). Every
group has a check in `tests/` and notes in `tools/blender/notes/` with the
details: shapes and paint, node trees, anchors, and known weaknesses.

| group | script | models | check | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Watch craft | `build_craft.py` | `swift`, `anvil`, `drone` (Tender), `torpedo`, `shuttle` (Skiff) | `models-craft-check.mjs` | `craft.md` |
| Harrow | `build_harrow.py` | `maul`, `sentry` (Eye), `seed`, `anchor` (fold anchor) | `models-harrow-check.mjs` | `harrow.md` |
| Mother | `build_mother.py` | `mother` | `models-mother-check.mjs` | `mother.md` |
| Perpetua | `build_perpetua.py` | `perpetua` | `models-perpetua-check.mjs` | `perpetua.md` |
| Civilian | `build_civil.py` | `freighter` (Mule), `station` (Varlaam), `colony` (Long Mercy) | `models-civil-check.mjs` | `civil.md` |
| Fleet | `build_fleet.py` | `mite`, `talon`, `warden`, `halberd` | `models-fleet-check.mjs` | `fleet.md` |

`build_all.py` runs them all after the original two scripts. Each script
also takes model names after `--` to rebuild only those, and
`METEORA_FAST=1` builds the geometry with small textures for quick checks.

### Anchor conventions

The original ships' anchors (nozzles, muzzles, RCS ports, the cockpit eye)
carry over. The campaign adds, all as empties exported in glTF space:

| anchor | meaning |
|---|---|
| `col_*` | collision spheres (`colliders: [{pos, r}]`); a large ship is the union of its spheres |
| `sub_<kind>[_n]` | subsystems (`subsystems: [{name, kind, pos, r}]`): reactor, shield generator, engine, bridge, hangar, bay, beam, drive, emitter, comms, dock |
| `turret_NN` + `turret_NN_gun` | turret mesh nodes: the base yaws about its local +Y, the gun pitches about its local X, barrels along −Z (`turrets: [{name, pos, up, fwd, size}]`) |
| `bay_N` | launch bays with their launch direction (`bays`) |
| `launcher_N` | missile and torpedo launch points |
| `dock` | where a Tender clamps on (craft) or the docking ring (station) |
| `light_N` | navigation lights and strobes with colour and blink period |

Nodes the game animates are listed in each module's `nodes`: the
*Perpetua*'s habitat `ring`, the *Long Mercy*'s `drive`, the Mother's
`reactor`, `eye` and `shield_0`/`shield_1`, the Eye's `eye`, the fold
anchor's `emitter`, the Tender's `arm_L` and `arm_R`, and the station's
`rock`. Emissive maps are baked at normal strength; the game multiplies
ship emission by three (three and a half on large ships).

## The hangar bay

`hangar.glb` (`tools/blender/build_hangar.py`) is the flight deck behind
the hangar screen: an 80 × 116 m bay with ribbed walls, catwalks and light
strips, girders and light panels overhead, a gantry crane, crates, fuel and
a torpedo rack, and the launch door open on a lit tube and the stars. Its
surfaces wear small tiling textures — deck plates (8 m), wall panels (8 m),
crate sides (2 m) and hazard stripes (1 m) — baked from procedural
materials on a swatch and laid on in world units, so they stay sharp at any
distance; the turntable pad has a texture of its own with the Watch's star
and a lit rim. Two nodes: `bay` and `turntable` (which turns with the
ship). Every face is one-sided, facing into the room or out of its solid.
About 3,800 triangles and 520 KB; `tests/models-hangar-check.mjs` keeps it
so.
