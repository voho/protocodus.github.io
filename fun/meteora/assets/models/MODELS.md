# Meteora models

Every file here is generated by the Blender scripts in
`fun/meteora/tools/blender/`. Do not edit the GLBs or `js/anchors.js` by
hand: change the scripts and rebuild.

```
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --factory-startup \
  --python fun/meteora/tools/blender/build_all.py
```

`build_all.py` runs `build_asteroids.py`, then `build_ships.py` (which also
writes `js/anchors.js`), then `render_previews.py` (one 512² Eevee still per
model in `tools/blender/previews/`). The whole build takes about 3 minutes
on an Apple-silicon Mac. Each script also runs on its own, and the build
scripts accept model names after `--` to rebuild only those. The scripts are
deterministic (fixed seeds), so a rebuild reproduces the files. Eevee needs
a GPU context, so the preview step must run where Blender can reach Metal
(not inside a sandbox that hides the GPU).

Textures are baked in Cycles (CPU) from procedural node materials and
embedded as WebP (`EXT_texture_webp`, listed in `extensionsRequired`; three's
`GLTFLoader` reads it natively). No Draco, no meshopt. Every textured
material has base colour, a tangent-space normal map (tangents exported),
and metallic-roughness (G = roughness, B = metallic); the ships also carry
an emissive map.

### Asteroids

Each rock is sculpted on a subdivided icosphere (6 levels for rocks 1–2, 7
for 3–5): an overall body (ellipsoid, contact-binary lobes with boulders, a
bent potato, a jointed block), domain-warped fBm, planar fracture facets,
bedding strata on the block, and craters of several scales with raised
rims. `lod0` is decimated from a lightly smoothed copy of the sculpt (so
the collapse cannot fold over fine spikes; any fold left is collapsed) and
`lod1` from `lod0`; `lod1`'s UVs are re-projected island by island onto
`lod0`'s layout, so both nodes share one material. Normals, colour (carbonaceous grey-brown, paler
regolith in pits and crater floors, greyer fresh scarps, sparse glints) and
roughness are baked from the sculpt onto `lod0`, with micro-craters and
cracks added as bump detail. Both nodes are centred on the origin; `lod0`'s
largest coordinate equals the nominal radius (1.5, 4, 12, 40, 125 m).

### Ships

The parts are bmesh primitives — a lofted fuselage, lathed engines, nozzles
and pods, airfoil wings and fins, slabs and boxes — joined into one `hull`
mesh. Normals are baked from a bevelled copy (rounded edges, recessed panel
lines); colour, roughness, metallic and emission are baked from the hull's
paint materials (tri-planar panel lines, AO grime, aft streaks, chipped
edges).

- `fighter.glb`: 14.4 m from nose to nozzle exits (15.1 m with the nose
  probe), 11.3 m span. Off-white `#d9d6cf` and graphite `#2b2d31` with an
  orange `#c8642a` accent; dark tinted glass canopy as the separate
  `glass` node.
- `interceptor.glb`: 11.2 m long. Gunmetal `#4a4f55` and oxblood `#5e1a1d`
  with hazard stripes; red emissive sensor eye.

## Axis rule

The scripts model in Blender space: nose along +Y, top along +Z, right
along +X. The glTF exporter's +Y-up conversion maps Blender `(x, y, z)` to
glTF `(x, z, -y)`, so a ship arrives nose along three.js −Z, top +Y,
right +X (the game's ship frame) with no runtime correction.

## Anchors

Anchors are empties parented to `hull` and exported as glTF nodes.
`build_ships.py` converts each one's world transform with the rule above
and writes them to `js/anchors.js` (`ANCHORS.fighter`,
`ANCHORS.interceptor`), in metres in glTF space.

| node | meaning | direction |
|---|---|---|
| `nozzle_L`, `nozzle_R` (fighter), `nozzle_C` (interceptor) | centre of the nozzle exit | `dir` = exhaust: the empty's Blender local −Y, glTF ≈ +Z |
| `muzzle_L`, `muzzle_R` | gun barrel tips (fighter: wingtip pods; interceptor: under the wing roots) | — |
| `rcs_01` … `rcs_16` | attitude-thruster ports, just outside each thruster block | `dir` = puff: the empty's Blender local +Z, out of the hull; the port pushes the ship along −`dir` |
| `cam_cockpit` | nose-camera eye point (`cockpit` in `anchors.js`) | — |

`radius` in `anchors.js` is the largest distance of any hull or glass vertex
from the origin (fighter 7.65 m, interceptor 5.75 m).

Both ships carry 16 RCS ports: nose up, down, left and right; tail up,
down, left and right; four wingtip ports (up and down on each side); two
forward-facing nose ports for reverse thrust; and two aft-facing tail ports.
Every linear direction and every rotation direction has at least two ports
that can push it, which the models check verifies.
