# Meteora

A space-combat campaign in the spirit of FreeSpace, played in the browser.
Ten missions in and around an asteroid belt at a red dwarf: escort
convoys, scout a sleeping enemy, break seeded asteroids, hold a station
while it is evacuated, guard the old carrier through an eclipse, cut a
line of haulers in open space, crack a capital ship's reactor in a fleet
assault and, at the end, keep a colony ark alive through the last battle
until it can jump. Between them, a special operation: pull survivors out
of a convoy falling into a black hole. Flight training first, if you like.
[Play it](index.html) — no installation, no account.

## The story

Three hundred years ago the generation ship *Perpetua* left Earth with
forty thousand sleepers. Ahead of her flew the Vanguard: self-building
fabricator ships, sent to make a home ready. At the red dwarf Pindus there
was no home and no Vanguard, only a belt of dark, metal-rich stone, and
the settlers carved their towns into the largest rocks and named them
after the monasteries of Meteora: Varlaam, Roussanou, Stefanos.

For a hundred and ninety years the belt lived quietly, guarded by the Belt
Watch: thirty pilots in refitted escort craft. Then the ore tug *Saint
Amaro* stopped answering in Sector Seven. Her last transmission was a
single frame of video: a red eye, opening in the dark.

You are Lantern One. Saint and Wren fly on your wing, Commander Doukas
gives the orders from the *Perpetua*, and Dr Okonkwo tries to understand
what the machines the miners call the Harrow want. The story is uncovered
a piece at a time: every mission adds evidence to the **case file** —
letters round the red eye's lens, hull plate rolled to an old Earth gauge,
a registry cast into a reactor, instructions written in the *Perpetua*'s
own machine-script, bombers that flinch at her beacon — until Mission 7
says outright what it all means, and Mission 10 makes use of it. Three
clues are found only by those who look for them (the bonus objectives
that find them say so in the debrief). Each briefing after the first
opens with the narrator's "previously"; the codex keeps the whole file.

## Playing

From the main menu: **Campaign** (choose a difficulty, read the prologue,
then pick a mission), **Flight training** (Saint takes you through
steering, speed, the afterburner, rolling and sliding, targeting, guns and
a locked missile, one step at a time, then five weaving drones to pass;
nothing shoots back and it counts for nothing in the campaign),
**Survival** (endless waves, best score per hull), **Codex** (the story
so far, the case file, the craft, the weapons, your service record and
commendations, and the controls) and **Settings**.

Every mission starts with a **briefing** — the situation in stages over a
tactical map, the objectives and bonus objectives, and a recommended
loadout with the reason for it. The **hangar** is where you choose a hull,
a gun, the ordnance in each bank and a reactor; it rates the choice against
what the mission needs and says so if you depart from the recommendation.
The flight opens on a short establishing shot under the mission's title
(any key or mouse movement hands you the stick at once) and closes on
another once the mission is decided. The big moments get a cutaway: the
camera cuts to the Mother's eye opening, the gunships arriving, a Mother
going up, the *Perpetua*'s last order and the drive ring opening, while
the fight slows down meanwhile (Settings can turn cutaways off). After the mission the **debrief** shows the outcome, the medal, every
objective, your kills and accuracy, what was unlocked, any commendation
earned and your rank, then offers the next mission (or a retry), a replay,
a change of loadout or the menu. A won mission also lists the new evidence
it added to the case file, and any clue left behind.

Progress, medals and the best score of each mission are kept in the
browser.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Mouse | Steer and aim: the ship turns toward the crosshair |
| Left click | Guns |
| Right click | Secondary weapon (lock first) |
| W / S | Speed up / slow down |
| A / D | Strafe left / right |
| Q / E | Roll left / right |
| Tab | Afterburner (hold) |
| Z | Match the target's speed |
| C | Chase / cockpit view |
| T | Next hostile |
| Y | Next friendly or objective |
| B | Nearest incoming torpedo |
| V | Next subsystem of the target |
| N | Next nav point |
| G | Switch secondary bank |
| X | Level the shields |
| R | Call a repair drone (hull below half) |
| 1 / 2 / 3 | Wing: attack my target / cover me / form up |
| Esc | Pause |

A gamepad works too, in flight and in the menus (standard Xbox layout):
the left stick aims, the right stick rolls and sets the speed, RT and LT
fire the guns and the secondary, A burns the afterburner, B matches speed,
X and Y pick the next hostile and the nearest torpedo, the bumpers switch
bank and subsystem, the stick clicks level the shields and call a Tender,
the d-pad gives the wing its orders (← picks the next nav point), View
switches the camera and Menu pauses. In the menus the d-pad moves between
the buttons, A presses and B goes back.

Settings hold the mouse sensitivity, the field of view, the steering
style — **Direct** (the crosshair drifts back to the centre when the mouse
rests, so the ship settles by itself) or **Stick** (the crosshair stays
where you leave it) — inverted pitch, the effects, music and radio-voice
volumes, the render quality (how sharp the adaptive resolution may go),
the bloom, the cutaways and the keys: click an action, press the key you
want for it (a key already in use swaps over), and the guides everywhere
print your keys.

## The campaign

| # | Mission | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Red Eye | Patrol to the *Saint Amaro*'s last fix, scan the wreck, meet what cut her open. Teaches the controls. |
| 2 | Ore Run | Escort three ore freighters home; a rockslide stops the convoy under fire. |
| 3 | Deep Signal | Recon: scan three signals without the Harrow's Eyes seeing you, then run. |
| 4 | Seedfall | Destroy eight seeded asteroids before they hatch; three start falling on Varlaam. |
| 5 | Hold Varlaam | Hold the station while six shuttles evacuate it; bombers go for the reactor. |
| 6 | The Long Night | Guard the *Perpetua* for six minutes against torpedo bombers, in the gas giant's shadow. |
| 7 | Cut the Line | Stop the Harrow's ore haulers before they reach the fold point — in open space, no rock to hide behind. |
| 8 | Heart of the Mother | A fleet assault: bring down a Mother's shields past her corvettes, crack her reactor, get clear. |
| 9 | The Narrows | The last convoy out of Stefanos — and not every ship in it is ours. |
| 10 | Long Mercy | The last battle: every ship the belt has left against everything the Vanguard sends, until the ark can jump. |
| ✦ | The Well | *Special operation, open once Mission 8 is won.* A convoy dropped on a black hole: match each falling hulk and latch a rescue beacon before it reaches the horizon — and do not go below your point of no return. |

Special operations sit outside the ten: the campaign does not wait for
them and they unlock nothing, but they earn medals, commendations and
evidence for the case file, and the story notices when they were flown.

Every place looks its own: its own sun, its own gas giant (or none), its
own nebulae, Milky Way, light and dust. Sector Seven is home; the
Roussanou lane runs under the banded giant in a golden light; the
Kalavryta Deep is dark and choked with dust; Varlaam sits under a cold
blue ice giant, full in Mission 4 and a thin crescent three days later;
the long night is flown under an eclipse, the giant a black disc ringed
with fire; the Roussanou drag is open space under a violet nebula; the
Deep Anchorage is a quarry of big rocks under a dead star's red shell;
the Narrows run beneath a rust giant filling a fifth of the sky; and the
belt's edge is the last rock behind you and nothing ahead but the Milky
Way.

**The Well** is a black hole, and it is drawn and flown as one. Every pixel
of its sky is a ray traced back from the camera through the hole's curved
space (the Schwarzschild orbit equation, stepped per pixel): rays that fall
in make the black shadow, rays that graze the photon sphere the bright ring
round it, and the rest bend, so the stars smear into arcs and the
accretion disk's far side shows over and under the shadow. The disk's gas
orbits at a good part of the speed of light, so the side coming at you is
Doppler-brightened and bluer, the other side dimmer and redder, and all of
it reddened by the hole's gravity. Everything that moves is pulled by the
Paczyński–Wiita law, which grows without bound at the horizon as general
relativity's does: hulks with a little sideways speed swing in on long arcs
and last longer, your flight computer holds you against the pull until the
pull outgrows the engines, and below your point of no return (on the gauge
over the speed panel) full thrust no longer climbs. The hole's size is
drawn true to the physics of light; its pull is scaled so a hulk takes
minutes, not milliseconds, to fall.

Each mission has primary objectives, bonus objectives (which raise the
medal from bronze to silver to gold) and a score from kills, streaks,
objectives, time and difficulty. Wins unlock the rest of the armoury in
story order, and sixteen commendations reward particular feats; the
score adds up to a rank, from Cadet to Wing Commander.

## Ships and loadouts

| Hull | Role | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Swift | Interceptor | Fastest and smallest on any sensor; fragile (90 shield, 60 hull); one bank. |
| Kestrel | Fighter | The all-rounder: 140 shield, 120 hull, two banks, one of them heavy. |
| Anvil | Bomber | Slow and armoured (220 shield, 240 hull); a large heavy bank and a tail gun. |

- **Guns:** Pulse (balanced), Needle (fast light bolts, easy to land on
  fighters, weak on armour), Ion (strips shields and scrambles their
  recharge, barely scratches hull), Breaker (slow, heavy, armour-piercing).
- **Ordnance:** Hornet (homing missile, locks on anything), Swarm pod (four
  rockets at once, for fighters and bombers), Lance torpedo (heavy bank
  only; locks on large ships and their subsystems, can be shot down).
- **Reactors:** Hearth (standard), Forge (faster guns and a longer burn,
  weaker shields, louder), Aegis (much stronger shields, slower guns), Hush
  (less than half the signature, quieter still with the guns cold).

The choice matters: armoured targets shrug off Pulse and Needle, a Swift
cannot carry torpedoes, the recon mission is about signature, a bomber
screen is about speed, and the tugs of mission 7 keep their shields up
under anything but Ion. The hangar's assessment says what is missing
before you launch.

The hangar is the Perpetua's flight deck: the hull you are fitting out
hovers over a pad under a gantry, lit by the deck's lamps and casting its
shadow, the launch door open on the stars behind it, and the camera shows
it off, the whole ship from above — three-quarter from the front, the
profile, the engines, high over the back, the other side — each shot held
and eased into the next. Drag to look round it yourself, scroll to come
closer; left alone, the tour picks up again. Out of flight only the music and the buttons are
heard. The bay is a model of its own (`assets/models/hangar.glb`), a
scene of its own with its own lights (`js/hangar.js`), and the only thing
in the game that casts a real shadow.

## Difficulty

| | Recruit | Pilot | Veteran | Ace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Harrow damage | 40 % | 65 % | 85 % | 110 % |
| Harrow numbers | 70 % | 85 % | 100 % | 120 % |
| Repair drones | 3 | 2 | 1 | 1 |

The Harrow also aim and fire slower or faster, your shields recharge
faster or slower, friendly ships are tougher or frailer and the Eyes see
less or more. Some missions add their own touches on the harder settings:
tighter timers, more guns finished on the Mother, older seeds.

## In the cockpit

- **Shields** have four quadrants — front, rear, left, right. A hit drains
  the quadrant it lands on; they recharge after a quiet spell and slowly
  even out. X levels them at once (every five seconds at most).
- **Hull** does not recharge. Below half, R calls a **Tender**: a repair
  drone folds in, docks — slow down to let it — patches the hull and
  restocks the banks, then leaves. Below 30 % the HUD warns HULL CRITICAL
  and an alarm sounds.
- **Wing orders:** Saint and Wren attack your target (1), cover you (2) or
  form up (3). Between the script's lines they call their kills, shout when
  they are hit, and warn you of bombers and torpedoes.
- **Large ships** are built of collision spheres, carry subsystems
  (generators, engines, reactors, bays, beam emitters) and turrets. V
  cycles a target's subsystems; torpedoes lock on the selected one. Turrets
  traverse at their own pace and lead with what they last saw, so fast,
  jinking flight beats them and straight lines do not.
- **Torpedoes** can be shot down, and stopping the Harrow's is your job:
  only flak guns fire at them, a ship's main guns do not. B targets the
  nearest; four Pulse hits or one Breaker bolt kill one.
- **Eyes** (sentries) see a ship's signature: size, reactor, speed,
  afterburner and recent gunfire. A rock between you and an Eye hides you.
- **Targets:** with nothing targeted the nearest hostile is taken by
  itself; T cycles from it. The **target panel** at the bottom of the
  screen shows the target as you see it from where you are — which side
  it turns to you, which way it rolls — whatever the range, with its
  class, range, closing speed, shield, hull and the subsystem picked out.
- The HUD shows the target box with hull, shield and subsystem, the lead
  marker, nav markers, a radar, objectives and timers, the radio, the
  escorts' hulls, hit markers, damage direction and streak callouts.

## Flight

The physics has no drag and no speed limit: a ship has a position, a
velocity, an orientation and a spin, and only thrusters change them.
Flight assist sits on top, using those same thrusters to hold the speed you
set. Turning the nose at speed leaves the old velocity sideways for a
moment — the ship slides — and the velocity marker on the HUD shows where
you are really going. The afterburner is a sprint on an energy store that
refills after a pause.

Collisions have mass and spin. Ramming a rock costs hull by the closing
speed, capped per impact; pebbles do a quarter of that, and wingmates
bumping each other only scrape. A rock is hit where you see it: each of
the five rock models is measured into a map of its surface in every
direction (`tools/rock-shapes.mjs`), and ships, shots, sight lines and the
camera meet that shape, turned as far as the rock has tumbled — not a
sphere, which would let a ship sink into a landmark's crags or bounce off
the air beside a hollow.

## Sound and music

Every sound is synthesised at load from oscillators and noise — there are
no audio files — and placed in 3D (Web Audio's HRTF panner, best on
headphones). Engines glide in pitch with speed, other ships bend through
doppler as they pass, hits sound by what they strike, and the cockpit adds
lock tones, warnings, the radio and the Tender's welding.

The soundtrack is six pieces made for the game, crossfaded with the mood
and lifted into combat when the Harrow close in:

| piece | plays |
|---|---|
| The Sea of Distant Suns | the menus and briefings |
| Beneath Uncharted Skies | patrols, the flight home |
| The Weight of Faraway Suns | stealth, tension, defeat |
| Apex of the Armada | combat and the big fights |
| Grandeur of the Fallen | victory, and the Long Mercy's last stand |
| Gravity's Final Breath | The Well, all the way down |

They stream from `assets/music/` (only what plays is downloaded; a
mission's pieces are fetched while it loads) and duck under the radio.

The radio is recorded: every line the game can say — some four hundred,
the scripted ones and those built from names and numbers — has a voice of
its speaker's own, and plays through the comms: the telephone band, a lift
where speech is clearest, a little overdrive, a hard compressor and a faint
hiss, so it sounds like a fighter's radio and stays clear over a fight
(the music and the effects step back while anyone speaks). The Harrow's
signal is ring-modulated and slowed. Commander Doukas reads each briefing
and a narrator the prologue and the debriefs, clean. A mission's lines are
fetched while it loads.

The lines are gathered by `tools/voices.mjs` (it flies every mission
headless, at every difficulty, and keeps whatever reaches the radio, then
adds the briefings, recaps, debriefs, prologue and the wing's chatter) and
recorded by `tools/render_voices.py` with Kokoro, an open speech model
(Apache-2.0), into `assets/voice/`. A line without a recording — a new
one not yet recorded — falls back to the browser's speech synthesis.

## Models

Every model is built by Blender scripts in `tools/blender/`, one per group,
and committed as `.glb` with baked WebP textures:

| Group | Script | Models |
|---|---|---|
| Watch craft | `build_craft.py` | Swift, Anvil, Tender drone, torpedo, Skiff |
| Harrow | `build_harrow.py` | Maul, Eye, seed, fold anchor |
| Mother | `build_mother.py` | the Mother |
| Perpetua | `build_perpetua.py` | the *Perpetua* |
| Civilian | `build_civil.py` | Varlaam station, Mule freighter, the *Long Mercy* |
| Fleet | `build_fleet.py` | Halberd gunship, Mite, Talon, Warden |

The Kestrel, the Thorn and the asteroids come from the original
`build_ships.py` and `build_asteroids.py`; after rebuilding the asteroids,
measure their shapes again for the collisions with
`node tools/rock-shapes.mjs`. Each script also writes the
model's anchors to `js/anchors/<model>.js`: muzzles, nozzles, attitude
thrusters, collision spheres, subsystems, turrets, launch bays, lights and
the nodes the game animates. `assets/models/MODELS.md` lists budgets and
conventions and `tools/blender/notes/` has each group's notes. To rebuild
one group, or everything with `build_all.py` (about half an hour, most of
it texture bakes; the previews want a GPU, so run it outside a sandbox):

```sh
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --factory-startup \
  --python fun/meteora/tools/blender/build_craft.py
```

## Code, checks and balance

The simulation — flight, the belt, weapons, AI pilots, turrets, combat,
detection, the Tender, missions and the campaign — imports nothing from
three.js and runs in Node at a fixed 120 Hz. Missions are generator
scripts (`js/missions/m01.js` … `m10.js`, the special operation `m11.js`
and `training.js`) over a small mission API, so each one can be played
headless. Where each mission
happens — sky, light and dust — is in `js/environments.js`; the case file
and the "previously" lines are in `js/story.js`. Run the checks from `fun/meteora`:

```sh
for f in tests/*-check.mjs; do node "$f" || break; done
```

`missions-check.mjs` flies every mission to victory at every difficulty
with a cheating autopilot, throws random inputs at every hull, and checks
objectives, unlocks, saves and scoring. `tools/balance.mjs` has a fair bot
— it uses only the player's controls, aims with a slightly shaky cursor on
the lead marker and calls the Tender when hurt — play each mission several
times per difficulty and report win rates, time, hull lost and bonuses:

```sh
node tools/balance.mjs m05 --runs 3 --diff pilot,ace
```

With `--idle` nobody flies: the player cannot be hurt and the Harrow
ignore it. Every mission should then fail from Pilot up — one that still
wins does not need the player, which is a design bug the tool catches.

`?mission=m04` launches a mission directly, `?debug` shows frame time,
draw calls and the simulation state, and `?bench` runs the performance
flight. With `?debug`, the console can hand the controls to the same bot
and watch a whole mission in the real game loop, faster:

```js
const { createBot } = await import('./tests/bot.mjs');
meteora.pilot = createBot(7); meteora.speed = 3;
```

## Performance

- The belt is culled per frame (cells, then rocks) and drawn in one call
  per rock size and detail level, nearest cell first, so the depth test
  rejects hidden rocks before they are shaded. Rocks switch to a far mesh
  at 100 of their own radii.
- Large ships keep their detail with a tri-planar detail layer instead of
  bigger textures; glow sprites are judged against the camera and dropped
  when they would cover the screen.
- Nothing is built mid-flight. Under the loading screen (`js/warmup.js`)
  one of every model the mission flies, in every paint, has its shaders
  compiled in parallel (with a half-float target bound, so they are the
  ones the HDR frame asks for), its maps uploaded between frames and its
  buffers drawn once, out of sight. A mission's `models` must list every
  model it spawns; `tests/missions-check.mjs` checks it.
- The target window's renderer is a second WebGL context, so its hulls
  wear copies of their maps no larger than 512 pixels (shrunk one a frame
  while the briefing is read) and it is warmed the same way: the first
  look at a new kind of ship costs nothing.
- A copy of a material keeps the hull detail's shader hook
  (`cloneMaterial`), so a turret going dark uses the shader it had.
- The pixel ratio adapts to frame time in flight, between 0.6 and 1.5.
  The loading screen and the menus, busy getting things ready, are not
  judged, so a mission starts as sharp as the last one ended and is not
  resized while it is flown to climb back.
- Where the GPU can render into half-float targets, the scene is drawn in
  HDR and tone-mapped at the end, and a five-level bloom lets flames,
  bolts, fire and the Harrow's red lights bleed: about 1.4 ms of GPU time
  at 1440 × 900 on an Apple-silicon Mac. Settings turn the bloom off;
  `?ldr` forces the old 8-bit path.
- The belt's spatial hash is a flat grid of typed arrays, so the AI's
  rock-avoidance sweeps walk index ranges.
- The sound bank is synthesised during loading in short slices between
  frames.
