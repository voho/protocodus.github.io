# Hedgerow — design (proof of concept)

Status: written 2026-10-19 from the request in conversation; the request was
detailed enough to build from, so the choices below marked *builder's call*
were made without a round of questions and are open to change.

## 1. Intent

A realistic-looking but arcade Second World War third-person shooter for the
Protocodus games page. The player is one soldier advancing through a set of
hand-built missions — different environments, terrain and weather — whose goal
is always to push through the enemy to the end of the level.

What the user asked for, in substance:

- third-person shooter, WW2, "realistic-looking but arcade";
- 3D models made in Blender;
- missions with a variety of environments, terrains and weather;
- every level pre-designed and hand-crafted: one clear linear way through enemy
  guards, but not boring — alternative routes exist;
- trees, buildings, boulders, hills, rivers, bridges, enemy stations, trenches,
  bunkers;
- **this POC: one level — evening, rain, entering a small village and then the
  forest behind it.**

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals

- One complete, playable mission from start to "mission complete", with a
  title screen, briefing, death/checkpoint loop and an end screen with stats.
- It should *feel* like a WW2 shooter at dusk in the rain: low light, wet
  reflective surfaces, rain streaks and splashes, thunder and lightning,
  fires in the street, tracers, muzzle flashes lighting the walls.
- Arcade feel: responsive over-the-shoulder aiming, readable enemies,
  forgiving health (regeneration + checkpoints), grenades, a score.
- Level structure that proves the "linear with alternatives" idea: at every
  major defended point there are at least two ways through.
- Engine pieces (character, weapons, AI, collisions, level description format,
  weather) reusable for further missions.

Non-goals (for the POC)

- More than one mission, a campaign map, saving beyond the browser's local
  best score.
- Vehicles the player drives, squad mates, multiplayer.
- Touch or gamepad controls (mouse + keyboard only).
- Enterable house interiors (except the ruined house, which is open).

Anti-goals

- Cartoon look. Stylisation must stay on the realistic side: muted palette,
  real-world scale, believable architecture and uniforms.
- Gore. Hits read with a dark puff and a fall, nothing more.
- A corridor. If a defended point has only one way past it, the level failed.
- Running below ~45 fps on a recent laptop.

## 3. How it is made

| piece | decision |
|---|---|
| platform | browser, `/fun/hedgerow/`, like the other Protocodus games |
| renderer | three.js r185 (already vendored in `/assets/vendor/three/`), WebGL2 |
| models | scripted, deterministic headless Blender 5.3 builds in `tools/blender/` exporting GLB (Meteora/Alpen convention) |
| textures | tileable detail textures generated by a Python script (`tools/textures.py`), shaded and made wet at runtime |
| characters | one rigged soldier with an Allied and a German outfit, animation clips keyed in Blender; upper/lower body split in the engine |
| physics | own lightweight solver: heightfield terrain + box/cylinder colliders authored in the models' glTF extras |
| audio | procedural Web Audio (gunfire, rain, thunder, footsteps, explosions) — *builder's call* |
| health | regenerating health + checkpoints — *builder's call* (arcade) |

## 4. The mission: "Saint-Clair, dusk"

Northern France, 1944, evening, steady rain, thunder in the distance. The
player advances south → north (world +Z).

1. **Sunken lane** — start between high hedgerows, a wrecked jeep, short
   control hints. The lane opens towards the village.
2. **Village entrance** — checkpoint with a barrier, a sandbagged MG nest
   covering the road. Alternatives: the orchard and haystacks on the left
   behind a low stone wall, or the drainage ditch on the right.
3. **Main street** — two-storey stone houses with lit windows, a burning
   truck in the street, enemies behind crates and sandbags.
   Alternatives: back gardens behind the west row (walls, fences, a shed),
   or through the ruined house on the east side.
4. **Village square** — fountain, church with a sniper in the belfry,
   enemy squad. Alternatives as above converge here.
5. **River** — a stone bridge guarded by an MG nest and barbed wire.
   Alternatives: wade the shallow ford downstream (west) or cross on a fallen
   tree upstream (east).
6. **Forest edge and trench line** — zig-zag trenches across the slope with
   riflemen; flanked from either end.
7. **Bunker** — a concrete pillbox with an MG covering the forest track;
   approach from the side or behind, or grenade the slit.
8. **Clearing** — a flak gun and a radio tent; destroy the gun with a
   charge, then reach the rally point. Mission complete.

## 5. Controls

Mouse look (pointer lock), WASD move, Shift sprint, C / Ctrl crouch,
Space vault/jump, LMB fire, RMB aim, R reload, 1 / 2 / wheel weapon,
G grenade, E use (place charge), Esc pause.

## 6. Left to the builder

Exact enemy counts and placement, tuning (damage, accuracy, ranges), names,
HUD look, the number of model variants, and the score formula.

## 7. Realism pass (2026-10-20)

Asked in conversation: "focus on realism — make each model and level 10x
more realistic and detailed". What that became:

* every model re-built with about three times the geometry detail and baked
  PBR textures from a procedural surface library with weathering
  (`tools/blender/bake.py`, `surfaces.py`, `docs/models-brief-v2.md`);
* new architecture (corner house, half-timbered house, café, burnt shell,
  churchyard), street dressing (rubble, beams, bicycles, wall lamps, ivy,
  benches, a Morris column), vehicles and guns (Kübelwagen, BMW with sidecar,
  PaK 40, burnt half-track), casualty traces, camouflage nets, a dugout;
* alpha-card foliage: spruce, fir, Scots pine, oak, beech, birch, bocage on
  earth banks, brambles, nettles, tall grass, weeds, ferns, leaf litter;
* 1024² ground textures (churned mud with clods and ruts, meadow, forest
  litter, granite setts);
* the renderer: rain lit by nearby lights, halos, low mist, decals, contact
  shadows under every prop, foliage wind, distant barrage flashes timed with
  the rumble, burning farms beyond the hedges.

Cost: about 47 MB of models (Meteora ships 31 MB).

## 8. Second realism round and feel (2026-10-20)

Asked in conversation, in this order: fix the soldier's upper body that
"rotates"; one more realism round on all models; terrain with small bulges
and irregularities; more and bigger trees and something in the distance;
a reload animation with automatic reload or R; physics so bodies fall
realistically.

* The rotation was the aim pitch accumulating: three's mixer skips bones whose
  animated value did not change, so held poses kept the previous frame's
  pitch. The clip's spine pose is restored every frame before the pitch.
* Terrain: fine rolling unevenness, ~900 mounds and ~70 shell craters with
  thrown-up rims and churned mud, kept off roads, paths, trenches and plots.
* Trees: twice the forest, larger scales, mature 20–28 m specimens (scaled
  stand-ins until the nature set provides them). Distance: a ~1 km ring of
  bocage fields and woods with ~13,000 far-LOD trees, burning farms beyond
  the hedges, the next village on a hill; the haze thinned (exp² density
  0.0048) so the land reads as silhouettes out to ~400 m.
* Reloads for player and enemy as described in the README; ragdoll deaths.
