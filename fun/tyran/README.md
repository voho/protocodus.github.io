# Tyran

An original browser arcade shooter inspired by the vertical scrolling tradition of Tyrian and the swarming choreography of Galaga. Available at `/fun/tyran/`, linked from the home page and Fun section. Static ES modules with a batched WebGL2 flight renderer and Canvas 2D fallback; no build step, third-party runtime, account, or backend.

## Play

Serve the repository root and open the game:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8773 --bind 127.0.0.1
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8773/fun/tyran/`.

- **Single player:** WASD to move; **Space** fires your primary gun, **Q** fires secondary plasma, **E** detonates a nova charge.
- **Pause:** Escape, P or Tab. **Sound:** V, also in the pause menu.
- **Title screen:** a console menu. ↑ ↓ (or W/S) move the highlight, ← → (or A/D) change the sector, or the difficulty while that row is highlighted, **Enter** confirms the highlighted row, **C** resumes a saved campaign, **F** practices the selected sector and **H** opens the flight manual. Tab, Enter and Space still work on every control.
- **Weapons:** your ship carries one primary gun (Pulse Array, Scatter Cannon or Lance Driver) and the Plasma Mortar. Each fire key uses its own weapon directly; there is no weapon-switch key. The six-tier Ion armament upgrade improves every gun and drone.
- **Touch:** drag the left control to steer, hold Pulse or Plasma on the right to fire and tap Nova to detonate.

## How a sector plays

Each sector is a script of waves (`waves.js`), followed by its guardian:

Ten authored strategies (`tactics.js`) give the environments different wave orders and fleet mixes: jungle chevrons, armored snow ranks, desert firing lanes, island pincers, asteroid escorts, disciplined Mars columns, fast volcanic strikes, neon crossings, alien orbits and citadel diamonds. Each wave resolves its own armor/speed tradeoff, approach paths, mirrors, spacing, reinforcement timing and firing cadence. Returning circuits remix the order and formations deterministically. Every new campaign or practice flight also draws a **campaign salt**: the opening sector keeps its teaching order, while later sectors reorder their waves, swap formation pairs, pick different flight paths and schedule different encounters. A retry or a saved flight keeps its salt, so a sector replays the same choices, and salt zero is the authored reference the QA suites replay. Tactical armor stays within 85–120% and movement within 84–120% of the sector's normal hull values. No additional artwork or audio loads during flight.

- **Swarm (hive) waves.** Squadrons fly in along looping Catmull-Rom paths in conga lines — wide rows arrive as two mirrored lines — and settle into a breathing hive below the HUD. Ships then peel off to dive at the pilot, fire from the top of their loop and on the way down, and wrap unseen to the top to rejoin their slot. Commanders take escorts from the row beneath them, dives grow more frequent as the swarm thins, bottom-row ships take occasional potshots, and needles home in as kamikazes. A ship destroyed mid-dive is worth double. After a timeout the remaining swarm makes one last pass and leaves.
- **Strike squadrons** sweep across on zigzag, cross, arc, snake, U-turn and acrobatic paths while firing; reapers fly fast groups of three. Chevrons at the screen edge announce queued side entries about a second in advance.
- **Gunships** descend to firing stations. Lancers lock a firing line, paint it as a pulsing dashed line, then fire a short heavy beam along it; bulwarks fire rings.
- **Heavy cruiser (midboss).** A reinforced dreadnought alternates rings and aimed fans while escorts cross; it drops a power core and a wing drone.
- **Captor.** A harbinger hovers above the pilot and projects a tractor cone. It steals one wing drone, parades it upside down beneath itself and turns its guns on the pilot; shooting the captor down rescues the drone (+1,500). With no drone to steal, the beam drains shields and fire energy and hauls the ship upward. It escapes with its prize after a while.
- **Tactical formations** combine two different groups drawn from eleven shapes: the vee, wall, orbit, escort and pincer, plus a spinning diamond around a heavy centre, a snaking column, a breathing crescent, a counter-rotating ring that pulses, a spear with a heavy tip that holds its lane and then charges, and a twin helix whose inner pair and outer ring turn against each other. Turning shapes widen for heavy hulls and grow into tall ellipses in narrow arenas. They arrive from above, left or right, establish their firing lane, then depart. Side entrants hold fire until they have entered; narrow arenas fit the formation to their available space. Members fire one rippled salvo along the line, at the harmonic mean of their own intervals so the group keeps its total fire rate. Survivors close ranks: rings and orbits spread evenly around the anchor and broken lines re-centre on it. Escorts, diamonds and spears fly behind a heavier leader; shooting it down breaks the group for a bonus, sending half the survivors into a panic dive (worth double, as any diver) while the rest scatter and stop firing.
- **Specialist roles** ride existing waves once the campaign has taught the basics (`roles.js`). An **elite** may lead the first strike squadron: 60% more armor, faster volleys and double prizes, marked by a gold hexagon. **Shielded** gunships carry a barrier that must be broken before the hull takes damage and recharges after three quiet seconds; aces (25%), veteran elites from sector 6 and heavy cruisers from sector 3 (20% each) carry lighter barriers. **Splitter** corsair rows release three diving needles when destroyed.
- **Encounters** are short scripted surprises drawn from the campaign salt (`encounters.js`): one to three per sector on distinct, non-adjacent waves, never on cruiser or captor waves, and only calm opportunities in the first sector. A **convoy** of unarmed haulers drops salvage under armed escort; a **bonus flight** of unarmed scouts pays a double squadron bonus for a full wipe; an **ambush** climbs from below the pilot and cannot fire until it has passed; a **meteor shower** tumbles rocks down the lane; a **minelayer** seeds drifting mines that detonate on contact and fizzle after fourteen seconds; a named **ace** brings reinforced armor and drops a power core, a nova charge (or repairs) and salvage; **phantoms** cloak on a cycle and ignore shots while hidden; **bombers** drop fused bombs that burst into rings; **medics** repair nearby hulls. Each encounter waits until the arena holds at most 28 live hulls, so difficulty never changes actor counts, and hazards never slow the terrain. The instrument strip announces each one and the flight report tallies aces downed, haulers plundered and hazards cleared.

Destroying every ship of a squadron before any escapes pays a squadron bonus; every third wiped squadron drops a power core. Enraged guardians launch pairs of diving interceptors. Guardians fight in one of three styles by environment, beside an aimed fan: the original rotating ring (Emerald Frontier, Azure Archipelago, Inferno Foundry, Obsidian Citadel), a twin-arm spiral (Polar Silence, Shattered Orbit, Neon Afterlife) or a downward curtain with a sweeping gap (Sunken Empire, Red Horizon, Luminous Garden). A curtain sends every round down, so it carries two fewer rounds, flies slower and pairs with a narrower aimed fan.

**Objectives, ranks and checkpoints** (`objectives.js`). Every sector sets two objectives drawn from the campaign salt: wipe squadrons, shoot down divers, demolish buildings, down formation leaders, hunt a scheduled ace, or clear the sector with set accuracy, an untouched hull or no ships lost. The opening sector draws gentle goals and a sector never sets more than one flight-long goal. Progress goals pay credits and score the moment they are met; flight-long goals are judged at the clear. Objectives (half the points), accuracy, ships lost and hull hits grade the sector S to D, adding 50%, 30%, 15%, 5% or nothing to the clear bonus. The pause screen lists live progress; the shop shows the rank, both results and the next sector's goals. Reaching the middle wave marks a checkpoint, announced with that wave's briefing; when the last ship falls, **Retry from checkpoint** restarts there with the sector clock, terrain position, flight report and already-paid objectives restored. Between waves a battered pilot gets up to 2.4 seconds more rest (below 60% hull) so shields can recover.

After the guardians of sectors 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9, a **challenging stage** flies 40 ships through acrobatic figure-eight, orbit and loop paths without firing or colliding. The stage reports hits out of 40 and pays credits and score per hit, with a large perfect bonus. After the result's 2.6-second hold, the entire screen and all audio fade together over 1.5 seconds. The challenge track continues through the fade, followed by a brief silent black frame before the upgrade shop opens. The transition freezes flight, preserves the player's mute preference and saves the completed round immediately; resuming that save goes straight to the shop.

**In-flight progression.** Power cores widen the primary through five levels (pulse stream, scatter fan, piercing lances); outer bolts are slightly weaker so width is not pure multiplication, and only the central bolts strike ground scenery so wide volleys do not farm salvage. A hit that gets through the shield knocks one core loose; it drifts away and can be caught again after about a second. Up to two wing drones fly in formation, echo every primary volley and soak up hostile rounds. Nova charges clear all hostile fire (each cancelled round scores), strike every visible ship, interrupt captor beams and grant a brief shield; they need a fresh key press. Each sector launches with at least two. Reserve ships continue the sector after a short delay with a three-second launch shield, a cleared launch lane, two fewer power levels and one fewer drone. Score milestones (30,000, then every 120,000) award extra ships up to five in reserve. Retrying a failed sector continues with at least two reserve ships.

Heavy kills briefly freeze the simulation (hitstop); the freeze holds actor positions still and resumes without a catch-up burst. Large hulls add expanding shockwaves, stronger light and staggered secondary blasts. A brief, size-scaled camera response adds shake, defocus, smooth desaturation and two narrow signal slips; the HUD stays sharp. Camera effects honor reduced motion and the impact-motion setting, and all particle/cascade budgets remain bounded. On final defeat, the impact plays briefly before a 1.5-second fade to black; the debrief appears only after the fade, and retry restores the normal view. All notices, including sector titles, guardians, waves and pickups, stay in the reserved instrument strip outside the arena. A master limiter and a shared voice slot for simultaneous explosions keep novas and mass kills from clipping. The shop prepares the next sector's terrain, scenery and fleet liveries during idle time, so launching it builds nothing. The soundtrack uses five user-provided Suno tracks: three rotate between sectors, with separate music for challenges and guardians. The shop shows a Galaga-style flight report: shots, hits and hit ratio, squadrons wiped, dive kills, rescues and the challenging stage result.

Explosions leave no flying fragments, ship wrecks, rubble or destroyed-building craters in any environment. Fire, smoke, sparks, shockwaves and camera impact remain. Destroyed buildings expose the original terrain and stay absent after scrolling, resize and save/load. Debris atlas generation, GPU uploads, surface checks and persistent wreck drawing have been removed; the unused crater atlas is no longer downloaded.

All successful primary, plasma and nova volleys divert reactor power from shields for 0.75 seconds: recharge runs at just 10% of the capacitor rate while weapons draw power and 125% after they settle (1/s versus 12.5/s without upgrades). Releasing fire creates a deliberate dodge-and-recover window; the normal post-hit delay still applies, and neither mode restores hull. Dry triggers do not consume shield power. The existing shield label shows ↓ while firing and ↑ during faster recovery, with no extra HUD row. Weapon-load timing survives saves and resets with a fresh ship or sector.

**Shields against weapons.** Each weapon class meets an enemy barrier differently: Pulse (and drones) strips it at 1.5×, Plasma overloads it at 1.25×, Scatter pellets glance off at 0.65× and Lance needles push 40% of every hit straight through to the hull. Overflow past a breaking barrier returns to raw hull damage; novas and chains stay neutral. Barrier hits show a bright flare for strong fire, dull sparks for resisted fire and a hot exit spark for piercing needles; a breaking barrier shatters. The pilot's shield stops energy rounds and beams completely, but kinetic impacts (rams, meteors, mines and bombs) push 30% of their damage through it. When a hit collapses the shield, it vents a pulse that clears hostile rounds within 140 units and then reboots 0.5 seconds later than after an ordinary hit; the HUD and a chime report it coming back online.

Primary fire is unlimited. Secondary plasma deals 58 base damage per orb, adds splash damage and costs 20 fire energy. Its orbs gently steer toward live, visible enemies ahead within 650 world units, balancing proximity against hull size: larger ships win at similar distance, while much closer fighters take priority. Guidance turns at most 0.65 radians (about 37 degrees) per second without changing projectile speed or lifetime, and reassesses targets as they move or are destroyed. Your ship has a 100-energy reserve that regenerates at 18/s after one second without a secondary shot. Depletion locks secondary fire until 40 energy has recovered, then a held secondary key resumes automatically. Primary fire remains available while energy recovers; holding both keys favors secondary while charged and primary during recovery. A shared shot cooldown prevents rapid key alternation from firing extra volleys. Fusion capacitor upgrades increase energy regeneration by 3/s per tier and reduce its delay by 0.1 seconds per tier, alongside their existing shield benefits. Rapid fire consumes secondary energy faster. New sectors refill the reserve.

During flight, delivered keyboard events are canceled in capture phase and stopped before other page handlers; this includes modifier combinations while firing. Escape or P pauses, and Tab pauses for keyboard navigation of the controls. Menus retain normal Tab, Enter and Space behavior, and the title screen adds direct keys for sector, difficulty, launch, resume, practice and the manual. Supported fullscreen browsers also request Keyboard Lock for gameplay keys and release it on pause, focus loss or fullscreen exit. Browser permission denial falls back to ordinary event capture. A webpage cannot block OS-reserved shortcuts that the browser never receives.

Choose **New campaign** to start in sector one and keep flying through ten repeating environments: jungle, snow, desert, tropical islands, asteroid belt, Mars, volcanic foundry, neon city, alien garden, and void citadel. Enemy class mixes vary by sector, with all nine normal classes represented across the circuit and a guardian with three attack phases ending each sector. Each sector has its own fleet family and hull designs: ten classes across ten families make 100 enemy variants. World cards preview each environment; **Practice sector** starts from that world with a fresh ship and leaves your saved campaign untouched.

**Difficulty.** Choose Easy, Medium, Hard or Real before a new campaign or practice flight. Easy uses the original base combat multipliers with the current sector strategies. Higher settings increase enemy armor, damage, projectile speed, firing pace and dive pressure; rewards, player equipment, attack warnings, wave and encounter sizes and the 78-hostile-projectile ceiling stay the same. The flight HUD and saved-campaign summary show the active setting. Resuming, retrying and advancing sectors preserve it; old saves default to Easy. The menu remembers the choice for new flights separately from the saved campaign.

**Endless campaign.** Every cleared sector opens the shop. After sector 10, sector 11 returns to the jungle for cycle 2; after sector 20, cycle 3 begins. Equipment, credits, score, power and supplies carry forward, and each cycle uses a fresh deterministic terrain seed. The HUD, shop route and saved campaign show the absolute sector and cycle. Later circuits retain eight to ten waves and bounded tactical variation, while enemy armor, shot/beam damage and rewards grow with `log2(cycle + 1)` (zero-based cycle): 22%, 12% and 30% respectively per logarithmic step. Nova strength follows armor scaling. Challenges repeat after odd-numbered sectors. Actor counts, mission duration and projectile pace do not grow without limit.

Destroy ships and buildings for credits, collect repair and salvage pickups, and purchase six tiers each of armament, fire rate, fire power, shields, hull, and recharge between sectors. The shop also sells the Scatter Cannon (1,100) and Lance Driver (1,600) — bought once, then equipped freely — plus wing drones, nova charges and reserve ships (each more expensive than the last); power, drones and nova charges carry into the next sector. The shop shows campaign progress, your ship's capacities, and each upgrade's current and next values before purchase. Pulse Array delivers fast, precise twin bolts; Plasma Mortar fires slower, heavier orbs with splash damage against clustered targets. Both fire keys share a cooldown. Enemy formations fly coordinated vee, wall, orbit, escort and pincer patterns. Guardians seal their armor between attack cycles; glowing weak points open during safe firing windows and can be broken for bonus damage. Chaining a double kill, multi kill or rampage overcharges damage and blast radius for a few seconds, then resets if the timer expires or the pilot is hit. Your ship launches each new sector with full hull, shields and fire energy. Retry keeps current equipment and accumulated credits/score. Hostile rounds hit harder in later sectors so upgraded hulls still respect incoming fire; salvage per kill grows more gently than score.

Fire rate and Fire power are separate permanent shop upgrades, each capped at six ranks. Each rank adds 2% of the base firing rate or shot damage to the existing Ion armament multiplier, without changing spread, blast radius, energy cost or weapon patterns. Starting prices are 540 and 600 credits, increasing by 1.55× per rank. With both new upgrades maxed, their combined sustained damage gain is at most 25.44% over the same ship without them (about 18.74% with maxed Ion armament). Fractional firing cooldowns make each fire-rate rank effective while preserving the original unupgraded cadence. Existing saves start both new ranks at zero.

Buildings offer targets and rewards. Ground turrets have been removed; trees, rocks, vegetation and ground vehicles stay intact under direct fire, nearby blasts and novas. Only buildings take ground damage or create explosions and salvage. Marked supply buildings release one pickup when destroyed, including through a nearby explosion. Their locations and contents follow the level seed. Repair and credit supplies join two temporary bonuses:

- **Rapid fire:** fire 65% faster for ten seconds, with the current weapon's projectile damage unchanged.
- **Immortality:** take no hull or shield damage for ten seconds.

Picking up the same bonus refreshes its ten-second duration; different bonuses can run together. Ship effects and the HUD countdown show what is active. Pausing freezes both timers, campaign autosaves preserve them, and a new sector starts without temporary bonuses.

## Automatic campaign progress

- **New campaign** begins at sector one and replaces the previous campaign. **Resume campaign** returns to your saved flight or shop; there are no manual save/load controls.
- Progress saves automatically every five seconds of flight, when pausing or leaving, at each sector launch/clear, and after shop purchases, across every cycle. Stage, credits, score, equipment, fire energy, recharge delay and depleted state are included.
- A saved flight also restores ships, health, enemies, formations, specialist role state (barriers, cloaks, mine budgets, fused bombs), the campaign salt and its encounter schedule, projectiles, boss state, level hash, building damage stages, temporary bonuses and remaining blast momentum, plus the wave script position, hive, conga-line queues, dives, squadron bonuses, captor and captive drone, lancer beams, power, drones, nova charges, reserve ships, the challenging stage and the flight report. Saves from before the wave script resume with a fresh script and the default loadout. It opens paused; press **Resume flight** when ready. A shop save reopens the shop before the next launch.
- Progress stays in this browser's local storage. The game reports unavailable storage or unreadable saves and keeps the current flight playable. If the browser closes unexpectedly, at most the last five seconds of flight since a successful autosave may be lost. Practice flights never overwrite campaign progress.

The single storage key is `tyran-campaign`. Existing `tyran-save-v2:auto` and `tyran-save-v2:manual` saves remain compatible: the newest valid one is adopted when resuming, unless a campaign already exists. Earlier version-one checkpoints migrate into the shop before their next sector. Former sector-ten victory saves reopen the shop with equipment and credits intact, ready to continue into sector 11. Older co-op saves continue as single-player, retaining the first surviving ship (player one when both survived), progress and equipment. Enemy and boss weak-point health scale down to the solo balance with their damage fraction preserved. Projectile ownership migrates once. Retired ground turrets and their in-flight rounds are discarded when restoring older saves. Saves without fire-energy fields start with a full reserve. Saved data is validated and bounded before restoring; formation references are rebuilt without replaying reward or transition events. Legacy weapon metadata remains compatible; retired projectiles already in flight finish with their original behavior. All future fire uses the dedicated pulse/plasma channels. The `sceneryVersion` marker distinguishes current structure health from earlier saves. Restoring older damage preserves the percentage of health remaining against the new armor totals, so a lightly damaged old building remains lightly damaged.

## Visuals and audio

Runtime artwork uses 19 detailed sprite atlases: six base sheets for terrain, scenery, ships and effects, three destruction sheets and ten sector fleet families. The asset library keeps only the lossless WebP atlases used by the game; duplicate PNGs and retired landscape paintings have been removed. The WebPs retain identical pixels and transparency to the original PNGs. Weathered metal, fine foliage, mineral relief and textured fire retain the existing biome palettes. The renderer decodes the atlases before preparing flight and caches palette grading and silhouette shadows. Generated assets and their provenance are recorded in `assets/sprites/prompts.json`; common fleet and procedural artwork provide fallbacks when a specialized atlas is unavailable. The map uses generated material tiles and connected shoreline/cliff masks.

Audio preflight fetches all 23 files before launch, even when sound is muted: eighteen CC0 effects from Kenney and five user-provided songs generated with Suno AI. Short effects decode with an OfflineAudioContext before flight; complete compressed songs become reusable local Blob URLs with five music players prepared from complete cached files before launch. One shared in-memory cache serves every sector and retry, while optional versioned Cache Storage avoids repeat downloads on later visits. Playback makes no audio network requests. The title image and all fonts also finish loading before launch; the site icon is embedded directly so the browser cannot request it late. A shared 15-second startup deadline bounds failed downloads, storage and decoding; unavailable assets settle to synthesized fallbacks for the session without in-flight retries. Playback starts only after a sound-enabled user gesture. Kenney's laser, explosion, impact and pickup effects use subtle pitch variation, short weapon tails and bounded simultaneous voices. Tracks `1.mp3`, `2.mp3` and `3.mp3` rotate between sectors; `4.mp3` accompanies guardians and `5.mp3` accompanies challenges. Track changes select an already prepared player through the same master volume and limiter, without assigning a new media source or loading another file. Normal playback decodes the cached compressed music as it runs, avoiding the memory cost of keeping whole songs in PCM. Mute, pause and leaving flight stop playback immediately; denied playback permission also uses the original adaptive sequence. Kenney sources and CC0 notices are recorded in `assets/audio/sfx/SFX-SOURCES.md`; the supplied songs and their checksums are inventoried separately in `assets/audio/music/MUSIC-SOURCES.md`. The Suno tracks are not described as public-domain assets. Every positional cue runs through its own stereo panner keyed to the event's position (the pilot's own guns stay near the centre). Heavy explosions, novas and beams send to a short procedural room reverb, and large blasts add a sub-bass thump and a debris crackle. The soundtrack is never replaced or re-edited: it passes through a transparent low-pass and a duck, so the heaviest blasts dip it briefly and below 30% hull it muffles while a soft two-tone alarm repeats faster as the hull drops. Shield, hull and kinetic hits, shield collapse and recovery, barrier hits and breaks, broken formations, objectives and checkpoints have their own cues; hostile volleys add a quiet, throttled chirp; the guardian is announced by a two-tone klaxon. Synth layers are capped at 64 voices, and pausing replaces the reverb so its tail stops at once.

Flight batches cached artwork into a visible WebGL2 canvas. Geometry remains at the arena’s full backing resolution. Clouds and their shadows draw directly, because a half-resolution layer’s clear, framebuffer switch and full-screen composite cost more than its quarter-resolution fill saves at their size; only large overlapping explosion light fields (three or more screens of additive coverage) still use the reusable half-resolution layer. The frame is cleared to the sector’s substrate color, so the opaque terrain strips need no blended seam fill; sun shafts submit eight slices hugging their slanted band instead of a mostly transparent quad; and each structure’s service light, lit core, tower halo and fixture halos are baked into two cached composites per building type, so an animated building costs two image draws instead of six. Transparent ship/cloud margins are skipped, opaque terrain avoids unnecessary blending, shockwave rings use bounded analytic geometry, and vertex storage and drawing state are reused. Texture caches are bounded at 256 MiB / 1024 sources, with a separate soft-layer budget of 32 MiB; a typical frame submits one vertex upload and about a hundred draw calls. Source art is still prepared with Canvas 2D before launch. Context loss uses the native renderer while current textures recover in short work slices; permanent GPU failure releases its resources and redraws the current frame natively. Append `?renderer=native` to exercise that fallback directly.

Player damage produces a brief defocus pulse (stronger for hull hits); the canvas softens while HUD and menu text stay sharp. Explosions add a warm fire bloom, with horizontal lens streaks and faint reflections for large blasts and bosses. Shared cached textures and a six-flare limit keep these effects bounded. Effects have high/low settings. Reduced-motion preference disables screen shake, impact blur, lens flares and bright screen flashes, and softens fire bloom. Losing window focus automatically pauses. Failure or denial of local storage and audio does not prevent play.

Ships cast separate ground shadows, with silver armor rims, white-hot engine fire and warm nozzle bloom. Every sector has a reserved high-contrast fleet palette (for example red/yellow over jungle and acid green/yellow over the black asteroid belt), separate from terrain and scenery materials. Fleet source sheets share red armor and gold trim, which the renderer remaps to each sector's complementary colors while preserving neutral metal shading. Every ship uses one fixed hull sprite and shadow: players face up, enemies face down. Steering never rotates or deforms a hull. Restrained exhaust flicker and reactor lighting provide life without alternate sprite frames; reduced-motion mode freezes these decorative effects. Two cached player projectile textures make each weapon readable in motion; hostile projectiles also use cached sprites with size and color tied to the firing ship. Thrust responds to acceleration. Hull and equipment add mass: heavier ships accelerate, coast and reverse more gradually while retaining their cruise speed. Keyboard and touch controls both use this motion model. Ramming a heavy hull, cruiser or guardian shoves the ship clear along the contact normal instead of leaving it inside the hull. Explosions carry the momentum of the hull that died, capped so a diver's burst stays readable, and wing drones hang on an underdamped spring that swings a few units past its slot when the ship stops. Mouse movement and clicks never steer or fire a ship; interface buttons remain clickable.

Each level is generated from a stable hash of its world ID and seed. `WorldRenderer.setWorld(index, seed)` reproduces the same terrain and scenery; changing the seed changes the layout. The campaign uses the default `tyran-v2` seed. The map consists of 100-pixel cells with four elevation materials and shared corner masks that connect water, banks, ground and ridges. Six material variants per elevation add surface detail, with directional relief lighting, fine dune ridges, mineral veins, ice fractures, orbital craters and weathered city panels. Shared fine-grain borders keep neighboring materials connected at high magnification. Irregular contours join at shared tile crossings, with layered cliff shadows, broken rims, erosion fissures, sediment and low relief. Varied crops of the generated materials reduce obvious repeats; dune ripples and Martian strata keep one prevailing heading by varying only zoom, offset and half turns, so their plateaus never read as a checkerboard. Cells are assembled into nearby 800-pixel strips for fast drawing, rather than painted as one landscape. Each strip row receives a soft-light pass of baked light: gentle hillshade from the continuous height field, lit from the same upper-left sun as the material relief and soft-limited on steep channel walls, plus a slow variation that breaks up long runs of repeated cells. A tiny image of corner values is stretched with bilinear filtering so its pixel centres land on cell corners, so rows and strips meet exactly, and the light averages neutral in every biome. Painting skips every layer beneath a cell's highest full material, which is opaque and pixel aligned, roughly halving image draws per strip.

Flight instruments occupy one compact, lightly translucent top bar outside the game canvas. Boss health, challenge hit counts, sector and combat announcements, bonus timers and the aggregated reward readout never cover ships, terrain or incoming fire. Damaged non-boss craft with a radius of at least 24 show a small health bar above their hull. Touch controls use a bottom dock in portrait and side rails on short landscape screens. These regions retain their dimensions when notices appear and while menus or pause screens are open, so launching reuses the prepared terrain and transient feedback never resizes the arena. The canvas follows its measured content size through rotation, browser chrome and viewport changes.

The camera keeps a fixed 900-unit height and derives its width from the exact canvas aspect ratio after space for instruments and controls has been reserved. Wider screens reveal additional 100-unit terrain columns; ships, buildings, circles and scrolling retain the same scale. Terrain and scenery use one logical unit per map unit. Seeded 1,200-unit districts keep existing scenery and supply identities stable as the viewport expands or shrinks.

Three visual planes scroll: **ground**, **atmosphere** and **foreground weather**. Terrain, rocks, vegetation, vehicles and surviving buildings stay on the ground transform, keeping rendered objects and collision aligned. Clouds and their offset ground shadows pass at 1.32× ground speed; larger peripheral wisps and nearby weather pass at 1.85×, with stronger lateral drift. The central firing lane stays readable. Reduced motion removes the extra parallax and freezes decorative drift, lighting and fire animation while retaining normal ground scrolling. Extra offscreen cells cover the lateral camera movement. Terrain, scenery, shadows, glow and projectile sprites are cached. The menu and shop prepare the visible region and one strip ahead; every launch and resume completes any remaining preparation before play. Terrain advances through small row-sized jobs, and the next fused scenery strip is built in six small steps (terrain blit, three neighbouring bands, publish, GPU upload). These jobs run in idle callbacks and in up to two milliseconds of each flight frame’s spare time, so streaming never waits for idle time that a busy render loop may not provide and never lands a whole strip in one frame. A strip that leaves the bottom of the screen is repainted for the next row ahead, so flight allocates no new strip surfaces and reuses their GPU textures instead of allocating and validating new ones. Infinite terrain keeps only nearby strips resident. Pixel caches stay bounded as the level scrolls, while a compact damage ledger preserves damaged and destroyed buildings. Enemy formations enter, attack and depart instead of accumulating at the bottom of the arena.

Sprite memory is shared by appearance, not by actor. Atlas preparation keeps alpha-trimmed cells, skips unused player references in the ten environment fleets and releases full decoded sheets; derived ship caches retain roughly one fleet plus the player ship. Before flight, the active sector prepares all 24 material cells, 252 shoreline/cliff masks, fresh scenery, every building damage stage, foundations and light fixtures. Damage artwork has an 80-entry cap (20.7 MiB), enough for the complete active sector; derived artwork from other sectors is released when switching worlds. Three shared scratch canvases handle scenery grading, shadows and local damage refreshes (6.3 MiB at 1,200-unit width and standard detail; about 57 MiB at 3,200-unit width and 2× detail). Atlases that are only recolored from their pixels — terrain materials, nature and structure scenery, all fleets and projectiles — stay in CPU memory, as do the grading scratches, so preparing a new appearance never waits on a synchronous GPU readback (previously 10–100 ms each, most visibly the first time a structure took damage in flight). Effects, procedural pickup textures and scenery strips keep normal GPU acceleration. Explosions reuse up to 700 particle records, select frames from shared art by age, and cap other transient effects. Every explosion, including boss destruction and delayed detonations, keeps its fire, sparks, shockwaves and screen impact without flying fragments or ground wreckage. Scenery stores each object either as injured HP or a destroyed marker. Campaign menus retain only summary fields; full saved flights are read when resumed.

Seeded clusters of small rocks, scrub, coral and rubble add ground cover around the original scenery. They use an independent random stream, preserving saved destructible IDs and positions. Buildings sit on weathered service aprons and stone foundations. Radar sweeps, facade fixtures, service lights and small rooftop exhaust puffs animate in place. Refineries have small stack flames; damaged standing buildings develop local fire, drifting smoke, embers and warm light spill. Operating lights dim as damage increases, and destroyed structures go dark. The architecture effects share bounded caches rather than allocating particles per building. Static strips remain cached, and reduced-motion mode freezes ambient animation. All thirteen shop cards have cached illustrated previews: firing patterns for guns, ships and nova hardware for support, and armor, shield and reactor systems for upgrades. The previews are composed once from decoded game artwork before launch and reuse inline images without new downloads.

All seven collectible bonuses share a beveled teal case, mint rim, pale emblem and green halo, with the same size and pulse. Distinct symbols identify repair, credits, rapid fire, immortality, power, drones and nova charges; supply-building markers reuse the same badge artwork. Active bonus indicators and collection effects use the same mint palette.

Buildings have 2.5× their earlier health; natural scenery and ground vehicles no longer take damage. Version-three scenery saves retain the percentage of health remaining when migrating either earlier armor model. Buildings appear fresh, lightly damaged or heavily damaged, then disappear completely at zero health. Damage changes their sprite at 70% and 35%, then removes it at depleted health; intermediate hits reuse the cached scenery strip. Durability scales with footprint area and the structure's armor: doubling the width of the same type gives roughly four times the health. Destroying a large structure gives nearby light ships a gentle outward shove without damage. The impulse fades quickly, is weaker on heavier craft, excludes bosses and large ships, and never replaces the player's steering input.

Mission terrain slows as visible enemies accumulate and builds speed again as the screen clears. Four ships set 70% of the clear-screen pace, eight set 60%, and twenty set 50%; even larger crowds cannot stop the map. Dead, departed, offscreen and queued ships do not contribute. Braking eases in with a 0.65-second response and acceleration with a gentler 1.8-second response, so arrivals and kills never snap the scrolling speed. The underlying clear-screen pace still grows toward 1.75× by the final approach; guardians retain their slower battle pace, with escorts adding crowd pressure. Challenge formations also affect their stage’s pace. Pause freezes momentum, saves preserve the current speed, and a new sector resets it. Enemy movement and wave scheduling keep their own timing.

The translucent HUD occupies a single reserved strip: 64–68 px tall on desktop, 100 px on compact screens and 120 px on narrow phones. Aligned labels, tabular values and slim meters group hull, shield and energy; restrained dividers separate mission, armament, combat updates and score/credits. Timed bonuses, boss status and aggregated rewards keep their own space without covering the arena. Core values remain at least 16 px, with pause and touch targets at least 44 px across.

Physics runs at a fixed 60 updates per second. Rendering interpolates between updates for smooth motion on faster displays and catches up through brief slow frames with a bounded budget. Paused and covered title screens stop repainting the arena. High quality renders the available arena at native 4K display resolution within an 8.3-million-pixel budget. Displays with more than 1600 backing pixels across retain terrain and scenery strips at 2× resolution through 32:9, preserving detail from the original tiles and structure artwork. Exceptionally wide viewports use 1× strips when 2× would exceed 8192 backing pixels across a strip. Low quality uses 1× strips and a 2.2-million-pixel budget. Sustained load still reduces backing resolution automatically while reusing the prepared terrain density; resizing or changing quality updates that density explicitly. The screen vignette is a fixed 512-pixel gradient that depends only on the arena’s aspect ratio, so an automatic resolution change repaints and uploads nothing. This never changes the flight area or simulation speed, and changing quality preserves building damage.

Damage refreshes only the affected part of a scenery strip, retaining overlapping objects in their original drawing order. Seeded ground-cover geometry is reused, and ground collision ignores natural scenery and vehicles. Ship and projectile drawing share canvas state where possible; each ship resolves its hull, shadow, exhaust, lighting and their trimmed bounds once per palette, hull class and sector, each live round keeps its texture, layout and heading, and every image draw is allocation free. Offscreen ammunition and effects skip drawing. Autosaves validate the campaign once instead of parsing their own output again, and the soundtrack schedules a gain change only when the target changes rather than every frame. Lens streaks submit their narrow visible strip instead of a mostly transparent square. HUD meters animate with transforms instead of triggering layout on every update. Kill scores, salvage, squadron bonuses and pickups accumulate in one stationary reward indicator in the top instrument strip. It shows the current kill chain, total recent score and credits, and grouped bonus notices. Each gain restores full opacity; after a short hold it fades out four seconds after the last reward, then the next reward starts a fresh total. Pause freezes the fade. Totals come from actual campaign balance changes, so dive multipliers, ground salvage and overflow pickups are counted exactly once. Explosions and ships carry no floating text, and the old duplicate chain readouts are removed.
