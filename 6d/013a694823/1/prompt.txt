AUDIO ANALYSIS — THE SOURCE OF ALL MOTION
Before designing anything, analyze the track and save the results as a timeline data file that drives the entire render:
- Tempo: BPM, plus tempo changes over time if the song has any.
- Beat grid: the timestamp of every beat and downbeat, bar numbers, and phrase boundaries (4/8/16-bar groups).
- Onsets: transient timestamps, separated by type where possible (kick, snare/clap, hi-hats, other percussive hits).
- Per-frame energy: at the video frame rate, energy in the sub, bass, low-mid, high-mid, and high bands, plus overall loudness.
- Structure: timestamps and labels for every section (intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus/drop, bridge, breakdown, outro). Note which sections repeat.
Every animated parameter must be computed from this data. No hardcoded timings, and no free-running loops or animations that move independently of the audio.

HOW THE VISUALS MUST REACT
Tempo: All cyclic motion (rotation, oscillation, pulsing, camera drift, loop cycles) is phase-locked to the beat grid at musically related rates (1/4, 1/2, 1, 2, or 4 beats per cycle). If the tempo changes, the motion follows.
Beats: Every beat produces a visible event. Downbeats hit harder than off-beats. Phrase boundaries (every 4/8/16 bars) trigger a larger change: a new camera angle, a new layer, or a palette shift.
Onsets: Kick → scale punch, camera shake, or shockwave. Snare/clap → flash, cut, or shape reveal. Hi-hats → particle bursts and fine detail. Each type keeps a consistent visual response so the viewer learns the language.
Frequency bands: Sub/bass drives weight and mass (size, pressure, displacement depth). Mids and vocals drive fluid motion, color, and typography. Highs drive sparkle, edges, and particles.
Dynamics: Loudness drives overall intensity, density, and brightness. Quiet passages are sparse and slow. Silence or near-silence means near-stillness.
Structure: Each section type has its own distinct visual state (palette, camera behavior, dominant technique). Transitions between sections land exactly on the section boundary. Repeated sections (e.g. every chorus) are recognizably related, and each repeat escalates. Build-ups visibly accelerate and tighten toward the drop, and the drop lands on its exact first beat.
Timing precision: Visual hits land on the frame of the transient, never after it. Use fast attack and slower release on reactive signals, so smoothing removes jitter without adding lag.
