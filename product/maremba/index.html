Maremba

A concert marimba, a Guatemalan marimba, a West African balafon and a box kalimba, with no samples. Every note is synthesised from carved bars or plucked tines, the mallet or thumb that meets them, their tubes, boxes and gourds, and the room around them.

  • 20 factory patches
  • Reason 14 or later

The instruments

Four instruments. No samples.

Choose an instrument and a striker, then shape the strike, the resonance and the capture. Each note is computed from the bar, the mallet and the air, so soft strokes, hard strokes and repeated notes all sound different.

  • Imperial Rosewood, a concert marimba

    Rosewood bars carved to concert practice over tuned brass tubes. The low fundamental blooms from the tube after the attack, as it does on a recorded concert instrument.

  • Mayan Padauk, a Guatemalan marimba

    Shorter hormigo bars over tapered cedar boxes. Turn up Buzz for the charleo membranes that rasp through the treble, where the melody is played.

  • Balafon Ancestral

    Fire-cured keys over calabash gourds, with spider-silk mirlitons that buzz on firm strokes and fall silent as the note decays.

  • Kalimba Artisan

    Seventeen steel tines on a yielding bridge over one shared box. Flesh, thumb, nail, pick and tap replace the mallets, and the mod wheel covers the sound hole.

  • Five strikers

    Yarn, cord, rubber, wood or the mallet’s shaft. Hardness, Position and Decay shape each stroke, and Variance and Artifacts add a player’s imperfections.

  • Resonance and character

    Carving, Coupling, Bloom and Sympathetic run from the bar outward through the resonator, the frame and the neighbouring keys. Buzz and Tension set the membranes.

Playing

Rolls, damping and dead strokes are part of the model.

A repeated note meets a bar that is still moving. What a player does with the mallets, the hands and the wheel changes the physics, not an effect on top of it.

  • Rolls on the mod wheel

    Raise the wheel to roll every held key with two alternating mallets, from six to twenty strokes a second, with a player’s timing and force scatter.

  • Ring, damp or dead stroke

    Let released keys ring, lay a hand across the bar, or keep the mallet pressed into it from the stroke. Stopping the transport damps everything by hand.

  • Seven tunings

    Equal temperament, Bala 7-equal, Chopi timbila, 5-equal pentatonic and Sambla, plus two measured keyboards: a Birifor gyil and a Sena valimba.

  • Velocity that reaches into the model

    Velocity changes the physical contact, not only the level. Choose one of four response curves, then let it drive up to ten controls, from Hardness to Artifacts.

  • Three captures, three rooms

    Blend close microphones, a far room and a piezo contact pickup, then place the instrument in a wooden room, a chamber or a hall.

  • A finished main mix

    Stereo Width, Warmth and Piezo Drive shape the sound, and an analogue-style limiter with rear Attack and Release keeps the hardest strokes in hand.

In the rack

Built for Reason.

Seven audio outputs put the close microphones, the room and the piezo pickup on their own jacks beside the main pair. Note and Gate CV play the instrument, and five more CV inputs modulate Hardness, Position, Coupling, Sympathetic and Volume.

The rear panel holds Tune, Detune and Scale, 8 to 24 voices, 2× to 8× oversampling and the limiter’s timing. Every front control is automatable and Remote-mappable.
