Physical modelling

Detail starts before the pickup.

The guitar is a continuous physical system. Its strings keep vibrating through a slide, harmonics emerge from finger contact, and the bridge lets neighbouring strings respond to what you play.

  • Two directions of string motion

    Each string runs two coupled transverse waveguides. Scale length, gauge and tension determine stiffness dispersion, while frequency-dependent damping controls the decay of the partials. The two directions decay differently, producing evolving sustain and slow beating.

  • Finite pick contact and release

    The plectrum contacts a patch of string and releases asymmetrically. Pick hardness, width and position shape the attack and its harmonic pattern. A shared wrist introduces bounded variation across a strum, while pick, finger and release noise have independent controls.

  • Frets, slides and touch harmonics

    The fretting hand changes the speaking length and pickup geometry. Slides preserve the string’s vibration while winding friction follows finger speed. Natural and pinch harmonics use contact-dependent losses to select the string’s own modes; hammer-ons and pull-offs continue the existing vibration.

  • Two hands with distinct damping

    Palm pressure acts near the bridge; a dead note uses a separate fretting-hand contact. Their losses follow the current speaking length, so short fretted notes and open bass strings respond differently. Mute Pressure and Tightness let you shape the live bridge hand and its resting damping.

  • A shared bridge and resonant build

    Bridge coupling transfers energy into unplayed strings for sympathetic ringing. Guitar Build moves through walnut-to-ash structural colour informed by paired pickup measurements and six authored scale/gauge setups. Body and Sympathetic controls adjust their contribution independently.

  • Magnetic and electrical pickup response

    Pickup position and finite aperture shape what each coil senses. One- or two-coil sensing, nonlinear magnetic flux, induced voltage and loaded coil resonance then form the electrical signal. Neck/Both/Bridge, Coil Blend and the passive Tone control change this response.

Playing the model

From tight rhythm to ringing lead.

The playing system assigns notes to the instrument’s eight strings and a reachable fretting-hand position. Independent pick and articulation commands let you shape phrases without rebuilding the sound.

  • Seven articulations

    Choose sustain, palm mute, hammer-on/pull-off, natural harmonic, pinch harmonic, slide or dead note through keyswitches. Down, up and alternate strokes remain a separate choice, letting the picking hand work with each articulation.

  • Repicks, tremolo and strums

    String-selection keyswitches constrain the fretting hand. Dedicated repick triggers strike held strings again, while a tremolo keyswitch repeats the picking gesture at the front-panel rate. Strum Spread controls the travel time across simultaneous chord notes.

  • Three output perspectives

    Mono gives a centred DI. Stereo presents the divided-pickup image of one physical guitar. Double uses two independently seeded performances with separate contact detail and small second-player timing differences, one guitar per channel.

In the rack

Your guitar. Your Reason rig.

Electry outputs the direct guitar signal, ready for your choice of amp, cabinet, pedal and effects devices in Reason. The front panel puts all 23 guitar and performance controls within reach, with automation and Remote support, beside pitch and mod wheels and a Sustain switch. The live fretboard shows held notes, release tails and sympathetic strings.

Use the modulation wheel for mute pressure and Breath/CC2 for resonance. Pitch Bend moves the strings together, two semitones by default and up to 24 with Bend Range, while keyswitch vibrato rocks held fretted notes. Switch Sustain off and released notes are damped; switch it on and they ring out. Stereo outputs and Note/Gate CV keep the device connected to the rest of your rack.

Reason Rack Extension

Start with the strings.

Six factory patches provide useful pickup, touch and performance settings for building a rack. Begin with the direct guitar, choose your amplification, and automate the hands and pickups to make each phrase move. Every note is synthesised from the model, without recorded guitar samples.
