Electry

A six-string electric guitar played from your keyboard. Its strings, floating tremolo bridge and three single-coil pickups are modelled, not sampled.

The guitar

Two hands play every note.

A picking hand and a fretting hand play each note you send, on a fretboard that shows where they are. Electry sends the guitar’s direct signal, so the amplifier and effects are yours to choose in the rack.

  • Pickups and wiring

    The five-way switch selects the bridge, middle or neck pickup or one of the in-between pairs. Volume, Neck Tone and Mid Tone work through a model of the guitar’s own wiring, its cable and the amplifier input it feeds.

  • The picking hand

    Position moves the pick between 25 and 250 mm from the saddles, Pick goes from a thin pick to a heavy one, Strum spreads a chord over up to 40 ms, and Release sets how much the fingers sound as they lift.

  • Keyswitched techniques

    Fifteen keyswitches from C0 to D1 give dead notes, legato, slides, harmonics, pinch harmonics, whole- and half-tone bends, slide-ins and vibrato, stroke direction, fingers instead of the pick, and a fretting hand laid across the strings to stop them.

  • Arm, palm and pedal

    The pitch wheel is the tremolo arm, the mod wheel lowers the palm for palm mutes, aftertouch adds vibrato and the sustain pedal lets released notes ring.

In the rack

Plug in your own amp.

With Fretting Lookahead on, Electry plays 50 ms late so the fretting hand reaches each note before it is picked, adding fret ticks and finger glides; Reason compensates for the delay on playback. Turn it off on the back to play live.

Both audio sockets carry the same mono guitar signal, and Note and Gate CV inputs play it one note at a time. Comp is a clean compressor on the direct signal and Level sets the output.

Reason Rack Extension

Seven pickup positions to start.

Seven patches cover all five pickup positions, from Bridge Twang to Neck Blues. Electry has no amplifier or effects of its own: put your choice of amp after it.
