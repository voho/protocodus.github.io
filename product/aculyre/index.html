The lyre

Strings that ring together.

Every string, played or not, ends on one bridge bar standing on the soundbox. Pluck one and the bridge passes its motion to the others, so a phrase leaves a soft halo of strings ringing in sympathy behind it.

  • Three instruments

    A modern 27-string lyre harp in steel, the widest sold, the same frame strung in nylon, and a gut lyre after Anglo-Saxon reconstructions, extended from their six strings to sixteen. Keys past the strings, from A0 to C8, still sound at their own pitch. Switching between them while you play crossfades instead of clicking.

  • The pluck

    Play with a finger, a nail or a pick, and set where along the string and at what angle it is plucked. A pluck near the middle sounds round; one near the bridge sounds bright and thin. No two plucks are quite alike. Pluck a string that is still ringing and the hand catches it first, so the new note grows out of the old one, and a pick tremolo alternates down and up strokes.

  • The hands

    Release sets how long a let-go string rings, from a hand stopping it to letting it die away. Mute rests a palm across the strings, Sympathy sets how freely the unplayed strings answer, and the sustain pedal holds every plucked string.

  • Sharping levers

    With Levers on Auto, a black key raises the lever on the string a semitone below and plucks it, as a lyre harp player sets levers by hand, so a diatonic harp can play in other keys.

  • A pickup under the bridge

    A piezo strip under the bridge bar hears the strings through the wood, with more of their fundamentals and none of the room. The Piezo knob blends from the microphones to the pickup alone, and every patch plays at the same level on Reason’s meter through either.

In the rack

See every string as it sounds.

The six-unit front panel centres on the instrument itself, drawn to scale, with each string lit and widened while it sounds, whether you plucked it or it rings in sympathy. Pluck and lever controls sit on the left; the hands and the output sit on the right. The mod wheel presses the palm mute and the pitch wheel bends every string.

Turn the rack around for oversampling, Note and Gate CV inputs, stereo Width and the stereo output. Automation and Remote mapping connect Aculyre to the rest of your rack.

Reason Rack Extension

Start with Lyre Harp.

Nine factory patches cover the three instruments and ways to play them, from a lyre harp left to ring to a damped steel pick, a picked gut lyre and a stage harp on the piezo alone.

Rack Extension version history

Aculyre version history.

Latest accepted device: 0.7.9d1. Latest development build: 0.13.5d1. The Reason Shop page is not yet public.

0.13.5d1 Development build · 10 November 2026

A soft pluck slips off the finger more slowly, so it sounds darker as well as quieter. Keys above the highest string open at their own pitch, and turning to another instrument during the 20 ms crossfade no longer clicks.

0.12.1d1 Development build · 8 November 2026

Every fundamental decays at the rate its string’s losses give it, each string is tuned as a player tunes it by ear, and notes played with a sharping lever sit level with the open strings.

0.9.0f1 Release candidate · 7 November 2026 build

Re-plucks and tremolo play into the still-ringing string: the hand catches it at the point it plucks, so the partials with a node there ring on into the new note, and a pick re-striking a sounding string alternates down and up strokes. The soundbox’s air now forms standing waves coupled to the top and back, the outside air loads each plate mode, spruce is damped by its grain direction, a resting hand damps idle strings at a point, and the player holding the instrument damps the soundbox. The touch is evened again for the new body.

Not yet accepted by Reason; the Shop page follows acceptance.

0.7.9d1 Accepted device · 3 November 2026 build

Both lyre harps now have a vibrating 4 mm maple back. The touch is balanced string by string across the scale.

0.7.7d1 Development build · 3 November 2026

Tighter equal-tempered tuning, an arched lyre-harp saddle, corrected notes just beyond the strings’ range and more consistent repeated plucks. Factory patches are rebalanced for the updated model.

Reason Shop publication is separate from this development build.
