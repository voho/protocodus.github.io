The crossfader

One curve for every fade a mix needs.

Shape turns the curve from exponential through linear to logarithmic; Skew weights it towards A or B. A fade resting at either end plays plain A or plain B.

  • Shape and Skew

    The logarithmic end, the default, holds the level between two different tracks with no dip in the middle. Linear suits two copies of the same signal; the exponential end lets B in late. Skew lets A hold on longer or brings B in early.

  • CV takes over

    Patch CV into the back and it takes over from the Mix knob; a rear Amount control scales it, and a lamp shows when CV has control.

  • Stereo or mono

    Each input pair also accepts a mono source, and left and right stay separate throughout the crossfade.

  • Smooth under automation

    Slew of up to 50 ms smooths changes in CV, position, Shape or Skew, and slow automated fades glide without steps. Automate Mix, Shape, Skew, Slew and CV Amount to build transitions into an arrangement.

In the rack

See the curve as you shape it.

A live scope on the four-unit panel draws the curve as you turn Shape and Skew, and marks the actual A and B gains at the current position, beside input and output meters.

A CV indicator shows when external control takes over, and a rear-panel amount control attenuates the incoming CV. Remix sits with the Utilities and has no bypass, because a crossfader is always mixing.

Reason Rack Extension

Connect A and B.

Send two sources to A and B, turn Mix, then turn Shape and Skew. Remix has no presets; its five controls are saved in your song.
