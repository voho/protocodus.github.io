Inside the instrument

The sound comes
from the circuit.

A bright attack, a rounded bass and a moving chorus come from different parts of the signal path. YouKnow models those stages together: clock-controlled oscillators feed a nonlinear filter and amplifier, then a high-pass network and clocked stereo delay lines complete the voice.

Circuit documentation and service targets anchor the design. Identified hardware recordings inform specific comparisons, while component tolerances and service drift give each modelled instrument a controllable character.

  • Clock-controlled oscillators

    Stable pitch, living waveforms.

    Timer divisions, a charging ramp, pulse comparison and a divided sub-oscillator form the source. Persistent phase and per-card circuit differences give notes their own history while the DCOs retain a shared master clock.

  • Nonlinear filter and amplifier

    Drive changes the response.

    Four filter stages interact through their resonance feedback. Signal-dependent saturation, self-oscillation and the amplifier’s current-control behaviour shape both harmonics and dynamics as the patch moves.

  • Clocked stereo BBD chorus

    Movement with its own texture.

    Opposing delay modulation, charge-transfer loss, saturation and the surrounding filters create stereo motion with softened highs. A separate noise control sets the modelled chorus hiss.

Simulation detail

More than the shape of a waveform.

The circuit state matters between notes and between stages. Explore the mechanisms behind the response, from oscillator charge to the final chorus return.

01Oscillators, envelopes & voice historyThe source and the shape of each note.

  • A ramp with stored charge

    A shared clock drives integer timers; capacitor charging forms the saw, a comparator forms the pulse and a divider supplies the sub. Pulse-width modulation changes the comparison point, while input coupling preserves the mixer’s changing electrical state.

  • Persistent voices

    The core voice cards retain oscillator phase, sub-divider state and individual filter calibration between notes. Poly 1, Poly 2 and Unison use the voice’s pitch history for glide. The Rack edition responds directly to Reason’s notes, with a one-to-sixteen-voice limit and six voices by default. Its timing differs from the native edition’s six-voice Original performance mode.

  • Control behaviour is audible

    Firmware-derived envelope shaping, delayed LFO onset and the portamento law shape the phrasing. Switch the rear Sync LFO control on to make rate and delay follow Reason’s tempo while preserving the running LFO phase.

02Filter, amplifier & unit characterResonance, compression and controlled variation.

  • Resonance in a nonlinear loop

    The four transconductor stages are calculated with their feedback and input compensation. The resonance-control circuit brings feedback in gradually, with service targets anchoring the nominal self-oscillation level. Raising drive changes the filter’s response.

  • The amplifier contributes

    The voice amplifier includes transistor current control, a control capacitor and a saturating signal path. Gain, compression and harmonics develop together; modelled temperature and circuit noise also affect the quiet parts of the note.

  • Character and service drift

    Unit Character scales component differences, filter offsets, slow cutoff wander and chorus imbalance. Aging adds service drift and noise changes. These controls explore engineering estimates of variation; the model is not a measured duplicate of one particular hardware unit.

03High-pass, chorus & outputBody and stereo colour through the complete path.

  • Interacting high-pass branches

    The four-position high-pass includes the bass-boost setting, interacting capacitors and switch resistance. Unselected branches keep their charge, so a change of setting carries the network’s history forward.

  • Two 256-stage delay lines

    Clocked bucket-brigade lines model held charge, transfer loss and saturation. Nonlinear followers and coupled filters shape their return. Chorus I and II spread the voice into stereo; the dedicated I+II mode gives a narrower colour.

  • Continuous chorus behaviour

    The model retains bucket and control-capacitor state through chorus switching. Its adjustable clock-related hiss continues between notes when enabled, so a cabled instance keeps processing while idle. Switch the chorus off or turn Chorus Noise to zero when an instance should rest between notes. The common amplifier, dry/wet mix and AC coupling complete the path before Reason’s stereo outputs.

Made for the rack

Play the panel. Connect the model.

A readable 8RU panel puts the musical controls in front. Rear connections and processing choices make YouKnow part of your Reason instrument. The controls below describe the current 1.3.2 candidate.

  • 100 authored Rack patches

    Explore 99 Protocodus sounds and a plain Init patch in Reason’s browser. This independently authored Rack bank is separate from the native edition’s 144 presets. Bass, Leads, Keys, Brass, Pads, Strings and Effects categories make starting points easy to find; save your own sounds with Reason’s patch commands.

  • Eight rear CV inputs

    Sequence a monophonic part with Note and Gate, modulate Cutoff and Resonance, or scale Volume, Amp, Sub and Noise from the rack. MIDI playing remains polyphonic up to your selected voice limit.

  • 43 automatable controls

    Record sound and performance changes in Reason or map them through Remote. Velocity response, glide, wheels and sustain support expressive playing, while rear Character and Aging controls shape the model.

  • Quality under your control

    Start with the efficient defaults: 1× oversampling, Poly saturation, Cubic interpolation, Normal filter solver and Normal Circuit Rate. Choose 2× or 4× oversampling, alternative saturation and interpolation, or High/Max solving for more processing detail at higher CPU cost. Circuit Rate Max keeps the reference internal rate; Normal reduces CPU use.

  • Settings that stay with the song

    Processing quality, Circuit Rate, Aging and LFO sync remain with the song while you browse patches. Aging starts at 50% and Sync LFO starts off. Unit Character belongs to the patch. Quality and circuit-rate changes wait for quiet voices and tails before a short transition. Sixteen voices at 4× is the heaviest configuration.

  • Stereo and mono routing

    Use both audio outputs for the stereo chorus. Connecting a single output combines both channels, preserving both chorus lines in a mono rack connection.

  • Levels you can see and save

    VCA Level spans −24 to +24 dB, retaining the modelled amplifier law within its native range and extending it beyond the endpoints. The current bank targets about −7 dBFS sample peak for a single G3/MIDI 67 note, velocity 100, at 48 kHz, default quality, Aging 50% and master Volume 80%. Chords and other notes can peak higher. Earlier songs may need a level adjustment because the Rack calibration and hidden trims changed.

Rack Extension version history

The current release.
The next step.

The Reason Shop currently offers version 1.3.0. Version 1.3.2 is an unreleased Rack candidate prepared on 18 October 2026; its newer panels, guide and features are shown here.

1.3.2 18 October 2026 · Unreleased candidate

Updates the shared engine to 1.3.2, reusing fixed chorus arithmetic and specializing voice-noise calculations. Circuit equations, processing rates and noise chronology stay the same; deterministic comparisons retain identical sample bits. Direct Reason note response and all 100 patch sound settings are preserved.

Fresh front, rear and thumbnail images accompany the candidate. Native engine timing improvements do not establish a Reason CPU-meter improvement. Reason Shop acceptance and publication are still pending.

1.3.1 17 October 2026 · Earlier candidate

Aligned the Rack build with shared engine 1.3.1 and refreshed the matching package and guide. Its dense pitch-bend and modulation queue fix applies to the native Original performance mode; the Rack edition’s direct Reason note response is unchanged.

1.3.0 13 October 2026 · Published in the Reason Shop

The Shop release refines oscillator, filter, amplifier and chorus modelling, voice variation, Unison glide and factory headroom, with smooth Unison level transitions. It includes 100 Rack patches, eight CV inputs and four rear quality controls.

Later candidate work on 16 October added Normal/Max Circuit Rate, the fifth quality control, the extended visible VCA Level range and the current reference calibration. It also regrouped the rear panel and hides the free-rate numeric readout. These later changes are included in the 1.3.2 candidate; earlier songs may need their levels readjusted.

Native edition

A separate host workflow.

The native plug-in and standalone edition also uses engine 1.3.2. New native instances use Original performance mode, following the six-voice firmware and serial timing, with Direct timing available separately. It has its own 144-preset bank, SysEx tools, output-load options and post-circuit Output Trim.

  • Native formats

    VST3, CLAP and standalone on macOS, Windows and Linux, plus Audio Unit on macOS. Package targets are macOS 11 or later on Apple silicon and Intel, Windows x64 and Linux x64. These are native formats; the Rack Extension installs through Reason.

  • Download status

    The source repository links the latest main-build packages. They require GitHub sign-in and expire after 30 days. These are development downloads: macOS bundles are ad-hoc signed and not notarized, and Windows binaries are unsigned. No signed, tagged native release has been published.

  • Choose the matching guide

    The Rack candidate’s patch bank, Reason note response and defaults are described above. Native Original timing, the native preset bank and Output Trim follow their separate guide and release notes.
