The work behind the sound

Built from schematics.
Refined by listening.

We have spent significant time studying schematics, service notes and component datasheets, reading technical discussions in synth forums, and running comparison tests against reference audio.

Our goal was to create the most faithful Juno-106 recreation on the market. That goal means paying attention to the small things: how an envelope opens a voice, how the filter responds to signal level, and what happens inside the chorus.

The model brings together documented circuit behaviour, firmware analysis and listening tests. We keep track of the assumptions where measurements are incomplete, and refine the model as the evidence improves.

What we model

Inside the instrument.

The plug-in follows the original voice architecture from its digital note timers to its analogue output stage. These are some of the details that shape its response.

  • DCO timing and waveforms

    Pitch comes from integer division of a shared clock. We model the charging sawtooth ramp, comparator-based pulse wave, pulse-width control circuit and divide-by-two sub oscillator.

  • Six persistent voices

    The six core voice models keep their oscillators running between notes, preserving phase and sub-oscillator state. Voice assignment follows the original POLY and Solo Unison behaviour, including its handling of a full set of held notes.

  • Scanned control electronics

    A shared 12-bit converter visits 23 control destinations in sequence. Individual sample-and-hold and smoothing circuits shape how those changes reach the sound.

  • Envelopes, modulation and glide

    Firmware-derived envelope curves, sustain mapping, LFO onset and portamento reproduce the digital control logic behind the instrument’s attack, movement and phrasing.

  • The nonlinear four-pole filter

    Four transconductor stages, the resonance feedback loop and input-side compensation are modelled together. Service calibration anchors the filter’s tuning, including the self-oscillation you can play at full resonance.

  • The voice amplifier

    The BA662 amplifier model includes its current-control circuit and signal saturation. The envelope and amplifier work together to shape the level and harmonic character of each note.

  • Mixer and high-pass circuit

    The diode-gated sub oscillator and coupling capacitors retain the circuit’s response to level changes. The four-position high-pass includes the bass-boost setting and preserves capacitor charge when switching.

  • Bucket-brigade stereo chorus

    Two 256-stage MN3009 delay-line models include opposite-phase modulation, saturation, charge-transfer loss, filtering and clocked noise. These details give the chorus its motion, texture and softened upper harmonics.

  • Output and switching behaviour

    The model includes the loaded output mixer, AC coupling and the control circuit that delays chorus muting. Signal history carries through these stages as you change the controls.

The instrument

Made for sitting down and playing.

A clear panel keeps the sound within reach. Start with a familiar patch, move a few controls, and follow what you hear.

  • 144 sounds, plus a blank canvas

    Explore 128 archival factory tones and 16 original YouKnow basses and pads. Brass, strings, keys, plucks and effects are ready to play; INIT gives you a simple starting point for your own patches.

  • The character of an individual synth

    Unit Character adjusts modelled component differences between voices, from calibrated nominal settings to a more varied instrument. Aging adds filter-trim drift and noise, following a documented hardware recalibration.

  • Room beyond six voices

    Modern additions include one to sixteen voices and adjustable velocity response. Play full chords, stack voices in unison, or use portamento for a sliding lead.

  • Chorus to suit the patch

    Choose I, II or I+II to find the right movement and width. A separate Chorus Noise control lets you set how much hiss accompanies the effect.

  • A place for your own sounds

    Import and export hardware-format SysEx tone data. Save a host preset or DAW project to keep the complete YouKnow setup, including its additional performance controls.

  • A panel you can settle into

    Scale the interface to your screen and hover a control for contextual help. Oscillators, filter, envelope and chorus stay together on one panel as you work.
