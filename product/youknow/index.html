Why it feels like an instrument

The character is
in the circuitry.

The way a bass note bites. The way resonance sings as you open the filter. The movement inside a held chord. Faithfulness lives in those details, so we model the stages that create them, and how they work together.

Original schematics, service procedures, component datasheets and firmware analysis guide the design. Circuit tests and comparisons with identified hardware recordings help us refine it. The result is an instrument built for the feel as much as the tone.

  • 6 persistent voice cards

    Character in every note.

    Oscillator phase carries on between notes. Each card has its own filter calibration and modelled component differences. Repeated notes and unison passages inherit that history, giving your playing the texture of an individual instrument.

  • 4 nonlinear filter stages

    A filter you can play.

    Sweep from a rounded bass to a bright, resonant lead. The filter’s stages and feedback loop respond to signal level; the voice amplifier adds its own saturation. Push resonance into self-oscillation and play the filter itself.

  • 2 × 256 BBD stages

    The chorus is part of the voice.

    Two clocked delay lines move in opposite directions, spreading a chord across the stereo field. Modelled charge-transfer loss, saturation and the surrounding filters give the wet sound its softened highs and grain. Dial the hiss to taste.

Inside the model

Small circuits. A big part of the sound.

YouKnow’s depth goes beyond its front panel. Explore the modelling behind the oscillators, the way the controls respond, and the analogue path all the way to the output.

01Oscillators & voice cardsStable pitch. A history behind every note.

  • A clock, a capacitor, a waveform

    Integer timers divide a shared 8 MHz clock. A modelled capacitor charges into a sawtooth; a comparator creates the pulse and a divider creates the sub. Changes in range and charging current preserve the ramp’s stored charge, carrying the circuit’s state into the next sound.

  • Voices with their own history

    The six core cards retain oscillator phase and sub-divider state between notes. Firmware-derived POLY and Solo Unison assignment determines which card plays next, including the original handling of a fully held keyboard. Each card keeps its own component tolerances and filter calibration.

  • Weight, pulse and noise

    Pulse-width modulation follows the scanned comparator-control circuit. The sub-level response uses a diode law checked against hardware recordings, giving the low end its own response as you move the slider. The shared noise source enters gradually through a transistor-junction model. Coupling capacitors retain the mixer’s changing DC state, shaping the transient as waveform levels change.

02Envelopes & control electronicsThe shape of the attack. The feel of the controls.

  • 23 destinations, one converter

    A shared 12-bit converter scans 23 control destinations in the service chart’s write order. Per-destination holds and circuit-specific smoothing shape when and how changes reach the voices. Control response is part of the model, right down to the voltage-distribution buffers.

  • Envelopes and performance timing

    Firmware-derived envelopes retain the original rounding, gate and retrigger behaviour. New instances start in Original timing: the assigner, serial link and voice-board firmware run at nominal clocks, so chord attacks and retriggers follow its scan phase. It uses six voices and hardware tone resolution, with a higher CPU cost. Choose Direct for immediate host response and up to sixteen voices; existing Direct sessions keep that setting.

  • Movement with the right phrasing

    The LFO’s onset and depth arithmetic, its separate PWM path, and the portamento glide law follow the recovered control logic. The portamento knob also follows its loaded potentiometer response, keeping the sweep of the control connected to the phrasing of the notes.

03Filter, amplifier & unit characterResonance, saturation and the differences between voices.

  • Resonance from the circuit

    Four nonlinear transconductor stages are solved with their resonance feedback and input compensation. A transistor-junction model brings resonance in gradually; service procedures anchor full self-oscillation. The current model uses nominal original voice-module tuning and a component-derived resonance offset. These are explicit estimates rather than measurements of original modules.

  • The amplifier has a sound, too

    The BA662 voice amplifier models its transistor current-control circuit, control capacitor and saturating signal path. Service gain sets the drive into the pair, adding compression and harmonics as the input level rises. Its response changes with the modelled card temperature. Resistor noise and current-dependent transistor noise follow the filter and amplifier circuits, including the quiet parts of a note.

  • An individual instrument

    Unit Character adjusts component tolerances, filter offsets, slow cutoff wander and chorus-line imbalance. A shared-clock temperature proxy and analogue card warm-up model let pitch, resonance and amplifier response develop together. Aging adds filter-trim drift and noise inspired by a documented hardware recalibration. Turn added tolerances down to hear the nominal original-module model.

04High-pass, chorus & outputBody and stereo colour, all the way to the jack.

  • The bass boost belongs here

    The four-position high-pass models the bass-boost and cut networks, including their interacting capacitors and switch resistance. Unselected branches retain their charge. Changing a setting carries the circuit’s history forward, with the boost stage’s own saturation under heavy drive.

  • A clocked bucket-brigade chorus

    Two 256-stage MN3009 models move on opposite-phase clocks, with signal holding, saturation and charge-transfer loss. Nonlinear transistor followers and coupled filter networks shape the wet signal’s softened highs. Clocked charge-packet noise supplies a hiss spectrum calibrated against one original chorus board. Switching preserves control-capacitor and bucket state, giving the chorus onset and return their own circuit history.

  • The connection changes the sound

    The common amplifier, dry/wet mixer, AC coupling and volume circuit complete the path. OUTPUT lets you choose the LINE level, receiver resistance and cable capacitance, or the PHONES amplifier with a declared electrical headphone load. Bass, level and treble follow those circuits; PHONES models the electrical connection. LINE mono routing combines both chorus lines.

Research you can look into.

We check circuit responses against independent reference calculations and use identified hardware recordings to test tuning, pulse width, sub level, resonance and chorus hiss. Settings held back from calibration help us check whether a refinement holds up. Our published research distinguishes recovered firmware behaviour, nominal circuit calculations and hardware measurements, and records the listening decisions and remaining measurement questions.

The instrument

Made for sitting down and playing.

A clear panel keeps the sound within reach. Start with a familiar patch, move a few controls, and follow what you hear.

  • The factory sounds, from their original settings

    All 128 original factory tone settings were decoded and cross-checked across three archives. YouKnow adds 16 original basses and pads, plus INIT. Start with familiar brass, strings, keys or plucks, then make them your own.

  • Find your instrument’s character

    Start with nominal original-module tuning, or raise Unit Character for more variation. Use Aging to explore the filter drift and noise of a synth further from its last service.

  • Choose the playing response

    New instances use Original timing for six-voice firmware allocation and serial timing. Select Direct in the TIMING menu for up to sixteen voices, adjustable velocity, unison and portamento. Saved Direct sessions keep their setting; changing modes clears held notes and tails.

  • Chorus to suit the patch

    Choose Chorus I or II for stereo movement, or explore YouKnow’s additional I+II mode for a narrower, coloured sound. A separate Chorus Noise control lets you set how much hiss accompanies the effect.

  • A place for your own sounds

    Import and export hardware-format SysEx tone data. Save a host preset or DAW project to keep the complete YouKnow setup, including its additional performance controls.

  • A panel you can settle into

    Scale the interface to your screen and hover a control or reach it with Tab for contextual help. Oscillators, filter, envelope and chorus stay together, with a voice monitor and session menus for output connections and timing below the keyboard.
