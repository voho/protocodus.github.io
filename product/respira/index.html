Reason Rack Extension · Physically modelled bellows accordion

Respira

Respira is an accordion in which no reed sounds unless air moves. With Hand on Auto, the default, the bellows pull for as long as you hold keys, two keyswitches below the keyboard turn them when you choose, and the wheel swells soft notes and eases stronger notes back. Switch to Wheel and the mod wheel is your hand: rock it back and forth to pull and push air through the reeds, and every phrase swells and turns with your strokes.

  • Automatic or hand bellows

The accordion

Air in, sound out.

Opening the bellows draws air through the pull reeds; closing them presses it through the push reeds. How fast you move sets the pressure, and the pressure sets how strongly the reeds speak.

  • The bellows is the instrument

    Auto pulls with a person’s slightly uneven effort for as long as you play, and turns only when you press C-1 or C♯-1, at the end of a phrase or in rhythm for a bellows shake. How hard you strike sets how hard it plays. On Wheel, Bellows sets how much air one sweep moves. Assist eases the extra air a full chord needs, and Air opens the valve so you can reposition the bellows in silence.

  • Every reed is a steel tongue

    Each sounding reed is a steel tongue in its own chamber, set vibrating by the air flowing past it. Velocity sets how quickly a key opens its reeds, and pitch shifts slightly with pressure, as on a real reed. Aftertouch lets a held key rise part way, so the note softens and bends with the bellows.

  • Eight registers

    Clarinet, Bassoon, Violin, Musette, Bandoneon, Organ, Oboe and Master combine five ranks of reeds. Musette sets how far the tremolo reeds are detuned, from unison to a wide, beating tremolo.

  • Bass and chord buttons

    In Reason’s note names, the keys below C1 play the bass buttons, C1 to B1 the chord buttons, and every key from C2 to C7 plays the treble at its own pitch. Keyboard, on the back, switches to Full to play the whole range as treble, and a map beside it shows which keys do what. The bass and chord reeds sound through the bass box, the space under the bass end. An accordion has no pitch bend, and neither does Respira.

  • The mechanism

    Keys knock as they land on their felts and pallets as their springs seat them, and the bass and chord buttons, register switches and air button knock too, with or without air. Mechanics, on the back, sets how loud they are.

In the rack

Your hand on the left. Registers on the grille.

The four-unit front panel puts the mod wheel on the bellows cloth, between pictures that follow Hand: an open and a closed accordion on Wheel, the grille’s sun on Auto. The nickel grille carries the eight register tabs, with Hand’s Wheel and Auto tabs, Bellows, Assist, Life, Musette, Grille, Width, Bass and Volume below. The sustain pedal opens the air valve too.

Turn the rack around for the Keyboard band: Split or Full, a map of every key that shows the keyswitches and where the bass buttons, chord buttons and treble begin, and Note and Gate CV inputs, which a Matrix or RPG-8 created under Respira plays straight away. Below it sit Touch, Momentum, which sets how smoothly the bellows turn at each change of direction, Mechanics for the level of the keys’ and bellows’ noises, a chart of the registers, oversampling, Room and the stereo output. Life, on the front, starts at 50% and varies the reeds, bellows effort and microphone movement. Touch shapes Auto bellows emphasis from your playing. Init and every factory patch start at a subtle 0.5% Room level, with fine control near dry.

Reason Rack Extension

Start with a stroke.

Load a patch and hold a chord; Auto does the rest until you take the wheel. Eight factory patches, one for each register, sit at matching levels, with Init to build your own.

Rack Extension version history

Respira release history.

Version 0.1.14d1 is the first Rack Extension release in the Reason Shop, from 4 November 2026. The development builds before it are listed below. Buy or try Respira in the Reason Shop.

0.1.14d1 4 November 2026 · First Reason Shop release

Auto’s bellows pull for as long as you play and turn only when you press the C-1 or C♯-1 keyswitch, and the pressure gauge leaves the front panel. Every key up to C7, the top of an 88-key keyboard, plays the treble at its own pitch. Note and Gate CV inputs let a Matrix or RPG-8 play Respira, and a map on the back shows the keyswitches and where the bass buttons, chord buttons and treble begin.

0.1.12d1 Rack Extension candidate · 3 November 2026

In Auto, keyboard velocity and the modulation wheel shape bellows speed. Bass and chord buttons now keep shared reeds sounding until the last button lifts, and Life controls the strength of the reed variations.

0.1.10d1 Development build · 3 November 2026

Refines reed airflow, chamber losses and the way reed ranks share their air supply. Bellows compliance, keybed resonances, opening chiff, player tremor, microphone shading, folds, straps and valves add detail through the existing controls.

Reason Shop publication is separate from this development build.
