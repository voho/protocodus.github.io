The accordion

Air in, sound out.

Opening the bellows draws air through the pull reeds; closing them presses it through the push reeds. How fast you move sets the pressure, and the pressure sets how strongly the reeds speak.

  • The bellows is the instrument

    Auto pulls with a person’s slightly uneven effort for as long as you play, and turns only when you press C-1 or C♯-1, at the end of a phrase or in rhythm for a bellows shake. How hard you strike sets how hard it plays. On Wheel, Bellows sets how much air one sweep moves. Assist eases the extra air a full chord needs, and Air opens the valve so you can reposition the bellows in silence.

  • Every reed is a steel tongue

    Each sounding reed is a steel tongue in its own chamber, set vibrating by the air flowing past it. Velocity sets how quickly a key opens its reeds, and pitch shifts slightly with pressure, as on a real reed. Aftertouch lets a held key rise part way, so the note softens and bends with the bellows.

  • Eight registers

    Clarinet, Bassoon, Violin, Musette, Bandoneon, Organ, Oboe and Master combine five ranks of reeds. Musette sets how far the tremolo reeds are detuned, from unison to a wide, beating tremolo.

  • Bass and chord buttons

    In Reason’s note names, the keys below C1 play the bass buttons, C1 to B1 the chord buttons, and every key from C2 to C7 plays the treble at its own pitch. Keyboard, on the back, switches to Full to play the whole range as treble, and a map beside it shows which keys do what. The bass and chord reeds sound through the bass box, the space under the bass end. An accordion has no pitch bend, and neither does Respira.

  • The mechanism

    Keys knock as they land on their felts and pallets as their springs seat them, and the bass and chord buttons, register switches and air button knock too, with or without air. Mechanics, on the front, sets how loud they are.

In the rack

Your hand on the left. Registers on the grille.

The six-unit front panel puts the mod wheel on the bellows cloth, between pictures that follow Hand: an open and a closed accordion on Wheel, the grille’s sun on Auto. The nickel grille carries the eight register tabs, with Hand’s Wheel and Auto tabs and two rows of knobs below: Touch, Bellows, Momentum, Assist and Life for playing, Musette, Mechanics, Grille, Width, Bass and Volume for the sound. The sustain pedal opens the air valve too. A setup bar along the foot repeats Keyboard, Oversampling and Room from the back, so Remote can reach them.

Turn the rack around for the Keyboard band: Split or Full, a map of every key that shows the keyswitches and where the bass buttons, chord buttons and treble begin, and Note and Gate CV inputs, which a Matrix or RPG-8 created under Respira plays straight away. Below it sit a chart of the registers, oversampling, Room and the stereo output. On the front, Momentum sets how smoothly the bellows turn at each change of direction, Mechanics the level of the keys’ and bellows’ noises, and Life, starting at 50%, varies the reeds, bellows effort and microphone movement. Touch shapes Auto bellows emphasis from your playing. Init and every factory patch start at a subtle 0.5% Room level, with fine control near dry.

Reason Rack Extension

Start with a stroke.

Load a patch and hold a chord; Auto does the rest until you take the wheel. Eight factory patches, one for each register, sit at matching levels, with Init to build your own.

Rack Extension version history

Respira release history.

Version 0.1.27, released on 18 November 2026, is the current Rack Extension in the Reason Shop. The latest development build is 0.7.2f1.

0.7.2f1 Release candidate · 20 November 2026 · built

Type, a new menu on the front, chooses the kind of accordion: Default, Bandoneon, Irish, German, French, Jazz, Balkan, Italian or Finnish, each built in the reed model with its own reeds and tremolo, and for some a tone chamber (0.2 to 0.6). An Accordion Types folder adds 16 patches, two for each type.

Life adds the air inside the bellows and reflections from the player and the instrument (0.7.1). Held reeds keep their tuning when a register, type or quality setting changes, the reed chambers take less CPU, and a Gate CV cable at zero volts leaves the keys at rest (0.7.2).

0.1.27f4 Release candidate · 17 November 2026 · built, created in Reason’s test host

The same sound and patches as 0.1.27f2. The front grows to six units for a setup bar with Keyboard, Oversampling and Room, so Remote can map them, the Note On lamp follows the keys, and Mute and Solo in the sequencer silence the device.

0.1.27 Reason Shop release · 18 November 2026 · device 0.1.27f1

The 0.1.27 accordion, now in the Reason Shop: eight registers over bass and chord buttons, Auto or mod-wheel bellows, and nine patches.

0.1.27f2 Release candidate · 10 November 2026

The final build of 0.1.27f1: the same engine and patch values, with f2 on its patches and rear panel. Reason’s test host created it fresh and loaded its default patch.

0.1.27f1 Release candidate · 10 November 2026

The reeds are computed twice per sample rather than four times, chosen by ear, which roughly halves the heaviest blocks on the development machine.

0.1.20f2 Release candidate · 7 November 2026

A new panel finish: a finer, darker oxblood pearl celluloid with aged polished nickel. The sound is unchanged.

0.1.17f1 Release candidate · 6 November 2026

Answers Reason Studios’ review of 0.1.14d1: the mechanism no longer clicks as one. Each reed valve and key lands at its own moment and speed.

0.1.16d1 Earlier submitted build · 4 November 2026

The modulation wheel reduces the Auto bellows level consistently across playing velocities.

0.1.14d1 4 November 2026 · Earlier submitted build

Auto’s bellows pull for as long as you play and turn only when you press the C-1 or C♯-1 keyswitch, and the pressure gauge leaves the front panel. Every key up to C7, the top of an 88-key keyboard, plays the treble at its own pitch. Note and Gate CV inputs let a Matrix or RPG-8 play Respira, and a map on the back shows the keyswitches and where the bass buttons, chord buttons and treble begin.

0.1.12d1 Rack Extension candidate · 3 November 2026

In Auto, keyboard velocity and the modulation wheel shape bellows speed. Bass and chord buttons now keep shared reeds sounding until the last button lifts, and Life controls the strength of the reed variations.

0.1.10d1 Development build · 3 November 2026

Refines reed airflow, chamber losses and the way reed ranks share their air supply. Bellows compliance, keybed resonances, opening chiff, player tremor, microphone shading, folds, straps and valves add detail through the existing controls.

Reason Shop publication is separate from this development build.
