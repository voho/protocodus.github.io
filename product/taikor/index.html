The modelling foundation

The stick, the skins
and the air between them.

Taikor’s synthesis research follows the physical cause of each sound. A stroke changes the drum’s motion; the ringing drum changes the next contact. The Rack Extension is being developed from that foundation.

  • A contact that responds

    The bachi model considers stick mass, tip size and compliance in a nonlinear collision with the head. A finite contact area senses the moving skin and applies force over the same footprint, connecting impact speed and hardness to the attack.

  • Vibrating hide, mode by mode

    Membrane resonances depend on diameter, tension, thickness, stiffness and material loss. Fixed unevenness splits and turns their vibration patterns. Moving the strike point excites a different mixture of those patterns, changing body, brightness and decay.

  • Two heads, one air cavity

    The front and rear skins have independent physical profiles and interact through the enclosed air. Head motion, air compression and air mass contribute to shared resonances, so body depth and coupling alter the drum’s response.

  • A shell that exchanges energy

    Directional wooden-shell modes represent body stiffness, mass and loss. A reciprocal boundary transfers motion between the resolved heads and shell. A rimshot also strikes the hoop directly, adding another physical excitation path.

  • Stretch and damping

    Head stretching produces the changing pitch of the attack, while a virtual palm removes energy from a ringing skin. The model carries the drum’s displacement and velocity forward, allowing new strokes to interact with motion already present.

  • An ensemble of individuals

    Independent drum and contact states underpin the ensemble research. Differences in hide, tension, shell, damping, timing and strike placement create interacting performances, rather than repeated identical hits.

Coming to Reason

A drum model
for your rack.

The goal is an expressive taiko instrument whose sound follows construction and playing gesture. Four drum-family profiles inform the work: Ō-daiko, nagadō-daiko, okedo-daiko and shime-daiko, each with its own head, shell and stick characteristics.

Taikor uses a reduced physical model informed by construction and acoustics. It is an original instrument design; its parameters are not measurements of a single maker’s drum.
