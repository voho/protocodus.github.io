Physically modelled taiko

Taikor

A physically modelled taiko ensemble inspired by modern kumi-daiko. Play four drum families and four strokes from a sixteen-note grid, with one to eight players per note. Every drum hit is synthesised from stick, head, air and shell interaction, with no drum samples.

  • Four drums
  • Four strokes
  • 1–8 players per note
Ask about Taikor ↗ Explore the features ↓

For macOS, Windows and Linux. VST3, CLAP and standalone, with Audio Unit on macOS.

The instrument

Play the drum. Shape the ensemble.

Open Don hits, cutting edge strokes, muted hits and rimshots are always under your fingers. Each row is a drum, and each column is a way to strike it.

  • Four distinct drum families

    Move from the deep Ō-daiko through nagadō-daiko and okedo-daiko to the tightly tensioned shime-daiko. Each model has its own heads, shell and construction. Change diameter, body depth, tension, head material and shell character.

  • Four strokes, either head

    Play Don, Edge, Muted and Rimshot directly from the 4 × 4 grid or its sixteen MIDI notes. Switch between the front and rear heads with MIDI CC18; the two heads respond through the same coupled drum body.

  • One player or eight

    Trigger up to eight independent drum models per note, each with its own stick contact and ringing state. Ensemble Variation adds companion timing and strike-placement differences, along with variations in hide, tension, shell and damping. The first player stays on your MIDI beat.

  • A responsive strike

    Velocity changes the bachi's impact speed, with Soft, Linear and Hard controller curves. Adjust the hardness of its wooden contact, move the strike point around the head and vary Humanise for repeated hits.

  • Damp and bend a ringing drum

    Lay a virtual hand on the head with the modulation wheel, or bend a sounding stroke with the pitch wheel. Strike Position and Azimuth move the bachi across and around the head, with MIDI control for live gestures.

  • Microphones, reverb and output

    Set distance, spread and stereo width for the close microphone pair. Keep it dry or blend in Hall, Theater or Opera reverb, then add drive, a low cut and output level to fit the part.

Inside the model

The stick, the skins and the air between them.

Construction-informed drum models connect bachi impact, vibrating hide, enclosed air and a wooden shell. Changing a strike or a physical control changes how that system moves and how the microphones hear it.

  • Vibrating hide, mode by mode

    Circular membrane shapes carry the drum's resonances. Diameter, tension, thickness, stiffness and material loss shape the partials and their decay; fixed uneven-head patterns split and orient them. Moving the strike point excites a different mix of those patterns.

  • Two heads and one air cavity

    Front and rear skins have independent physical profiles. Their lowest radial vibration patterns share the enclosed air, so body depth and air coupling change their interaction. Either head can be struck, with the coupled system preserving its ringing state through structural changes.

  • A bachi that loads the head

    Each family has a wooden stick profile with its own mass, tip size and compliance. A nonlinear stick-and-head collision uses a finite contact footprint for both sensing motion and applying force. Impact speed and hardness change the contact; contact-driven upper-spectrum texture extends the resolved membrane sound.

  • The wooden shell responds

    Six shell ring modes represent the body's directional stiffness, mass and loss. A passive, reciprocal boundary transfers motion between the resolved head and shell. Rimshots also strike the hoop directly, with tack rattle on the tacked drum families.

  • Stretch, damping and human variation

    Head stretching produces an adjustable attack pitch glide, while palm damping removes energy from the ringing head. Repeated strokes vary in speed, contact time and texture. Ensemble companions have independent contact and resonance states, with repeatable player and drum differences.

  • A close pair and a shared room

    The microphone model observes head and shell motion with position and distance-dependent response. Its stereo pair captures different views as the strike moves. Stereo convolution reverb applies the selected room's impulse response to the finished ensemble, with a dry/wet blend.

In your setup

One drum, or the whole family.

Choose 1 Drum to play the selected design retuned across the rows, or 4 Drums for the four families at their own sizes. Set Players for an ensemble inside one instance. When layering multiple instances, choose different P1–P4 performer identities for distinct, repeatable gestures. Save the setup in your DAW project or a host preset.
