Inside the drums

A 4 by 4 grid,
and nothing else.

No keyswitches and no articulation menu. Sixteen notes, and every other key on the keyboard is silent.

  • Sixteen notes, no keyswitches. The octave picks the drum and the bottom four semitones pick the stroke: Don, Ka, Tsu and Don Rim. Every other key on the keyboard stays silent.
  • Four distinct drums. Ō-daiko, chū-daiko, okedo-daiko and shime-daiko each carry their own diameter, body depth, hide and shell, from a 150 cm ō-daiko down to a 30 cm shime.
  • Velocity is stick speed. MIDI velocity sets the impact speed of the bachi, so a harder stroke comes out shorter and brighter as well as louder, not just louder.
  • Damping, bend and placement. Hold CC1 to lay a palm on the head and mute the ring, use the pitch wheel to bend a stroke that is already sounding, and CC16/17 to place each hit around and across the head.
  • No samples. Every stroke is solved from a struck circular membrane in real time, never replayed from a recording.
Workflow

Kits and session recall.

The Drum Layout switch chooses what a keyboard row plays: one head retuned across the rows, or the four family members at their own sizes.

Save the kit as a DAW project or host preset to recall drum sizing, layout and every playing control exactly as you left them.
