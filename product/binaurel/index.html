The panner

Move a sound through the space around you.

Every position is rendered with separate impulse responses for each ear, measured on a dummy head. How convincing the result is depends on how closely those cues suit your ears.

  • Point source or stereo pair

    One connected input becomes a single point source; two become separate left and right sources. Width spreads a stereo pair through up to 180 degrees.

  • Distance you can hear

    Distance changes the direct level, the near-field cues, air absorption and the balance against the room.

  • A room around it

    Room moves from free field toward a large hall. Early / Late reduces the reverberant tail or the early reflections independently; its centre keeps both.

  • Vary

    On the rear, Vary Spatial adds smooth movement, with Rate setting its pace and Seed making it repeatable. Zero keeps the source still. The current edition uses the measured KU100 (HQ) head.

In the rack

See where the sound sits.

The four-unit front panel shows the sources from the top, the side and the rear, where the knobs and CV place them. Six knobs in two groups of three each show their value.

The rear carries six trimmed CV inputs, Head Size and the Vary setup. Azimuth CV wraps around the head for continuous orbits. Every knob can be automated. The device delays the sound by a fixed 18 ms.

Reason Rack Extension

Put your headphones on.

Patch a mono or stereo source into Binaurel and turn Azimuth. Binaurel is made for headphones; it has no factory patches, and its settings are saved with your song.
