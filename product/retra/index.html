The machines

Nothing in Retra is a recording.

The clippers and amplifiers are solved from the equations of their diodes, tubes and transistors. The cassette saturates as tape does and loses treble as it wears, and the record distorts the way a stylus tip does in a groove.

  • Eight stages, any order

    A fuzz or clipper, a cassette or a record, the wobble of a cassette deck, reel deck or turntable, dropouts, noise, a tube or transistor amplifier, a small speaker, and a cheap converter or a low-bitrate MP3 or Ogg file. The arrows on a stage swap it with its neighbour.

  • Noise that listens

    Hiss swells around every note, crackle gets denser where the music was loud and bright, hum rides on the signal, and static treats your track as a radio station drifting off tune. Freq moves each noise in pitch; Follow ducks it under the signal or lets it rise with it.

  • One Amount knob

    Every amount control follows one rule: a light colour at 20%, clearly there at 50%, exaggerated at 100%. Amount sets how much of the whole chain you hear.

  • Real codecs and speakers

    MP3 and Ogg are a real encode and decode, down to 8 kbit/s. The speaker is a moving cone that distorts when you push it: a radio, a guitar cabinet, a telephone or a horn.

In the rack

Switch on the stages you want.

Every knob and every stage switch can be automated; the type selectors, the order and the Quality switch are settings, saved with the song. The rear Quality switch runs the analogue stages at 2× or 4× for cleaner high notes at high Drive.

Retra is a stereo effect. The two channels of each machine are not quite the same, so a mono track gains some width from every stage except Stream, MP3 and Ogg. It has no CV inputs.

Reason Rack Extension

Start with a patch.

Twenty patches start you off: ten simple ones, such as Warm Tape, Dusty Record, Pocket Radio and 12-Bit Sampler, and ten whole chains, such as Bedroom Four-Track, Night Drive, Shortwave Broadcast, Answering Machine and Concert Bootleg.

Version history

Retra version history.

Rack Extension in development. The latest build is the 0.7.0f4 release candidate; it is not yet in the Reason Shop.

0.7.0f4 Unreleased · Release candidate · 12 November 2026

Quality’s 1x, 2x and 4x keys are on the front panel as well as the back, as Reason’s validation requires. The sound is unchanged.

0.7.0f3 Unreleased · Release candidate · 12 November 2026

The stage types, Quality and the chain positions can be mapped to a Remote control surface, and the back panel prints its own version. The sound is unchanged.

0.7.0f2 Unreleased · Release candidate · 11 November 2026

Fixes a crash in Reason’s validation on Windows and Intel macOS that stopped 0.7.0f1.

0.7.0f1 Unreleased · Release candidate · 11 November 2026

Seven more details of the machines, each from its part’s own equation or a catalogue figure, among them the cassette’s treble at 1×.

0.6.2f1 Unreleased · Release candidate · 11 November 2026

A few percent less CPU with the same sound. The rear panel shows the signal chain, and the amplifier lamps warm up; Hum runs from 10 to 60 Hz, Crackle grows at high Level and Follow, and five Gentle patches add a light colour.

0.6.0d1 Unreleased · Development build · 11 November 2026

Wobble gets three machines, Cassette, Reel and Turntable, each from its moving parts, and the whole device uses about a third less CPU.

0.5.0d1 Unreleased · Development build · 11 November 2026

Noise Freq moves each noise in pitch, every stage gives a mono track a stereo image, and twenty patches arrive.

0.4.0d1 Unreleased · Development build · 11 November 2026

Each noise is now the mechanism that makes it, acting on the signal as it does in the machine, so what you hear depends on what you play.

0.3.0d2 Unreleased · Development build · 10 November 2026

Radio static: the receiver tuned between stations, with the crackle of distant lightning.
