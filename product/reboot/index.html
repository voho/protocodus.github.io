Reboot

The sound hardware of two 8-bit machines, the Handheld and the Console, emulated chip by chip and played from the keyboard the way a game’s sound driver played them.

  • Expansions A to E
  • Up to eight machines
  • Double

The machines

Two machines, chip by chip.

Each machine keeps its own pitch steps, volume steps and quirks. Reboot writes only what the chip accepts, at the times the chip allows, and pitch is always the chip’s own period setting, so every key plays as close to its pitch as that machine can.

  • The Handheld and the Console

    Model picks the machine’s model, and Cart adds one of the Console’s five cartridge chips, expansions A to E. On the back, Cart channels sets how many channels expansion C runs, and Bias sets the Console’s DAC level, which shifts how its channels mix.

  • Pin or Line

    Path hears the chips and their mixer alone (Pin) or the machine’s own output stage after them (Line).

  • Parts and shapes

    Part picks one of the machine’s channels: pulse, triangle, wave, noise and the cartridge’s. Shape picks its duty, wave or noise mode, and the screen draws one cycle of it as the chip makes it. Every part arrives at one loudness.

  • An envelope the way a driver wrote it

    Four faders set the envelope: Attack, Decay and Release run from 0 to 5 seconds and Sustain sets the chip’s volume step. The chip is written once a frame, as a game’s driver wrote it. The triangle has no volume, so on it the envelope only starts and stops the note.

  • Poly, Mono or Arp

    Mono plays legato with Glide, and Arp steps through the held keys every few frames. Vibrato runs at the machine’s frame rate, and the mod wheel adds up to 50 cents of it.

  • Up to eight machines, and Double

    Boards, on the back, plays up to eight machines at once. Double runs a second set, Detune apart, with the first set on the left and the second on the right. The machines can run at 1x to 8x the session’s rate.

In the rack

The machine’s audio path on the back.

The 8U Broadcast Desk panel, in walnut and brushed aluminium, has six sections: Machine, Envelope, Voice, Output, Vibrato and Performance. A control the chosen machine does not hear is dimmed, not hidden, and still works. The back draws the chosen machine’s signal path, the stage Path switches, then the level trim, Volume and limiter, beside the machine setup: Boards, Cart channels and Bias.

Note and Gate CV inputs on the back let a Matrix or an RPG-8 play Reboot as a second player; the gate’s level sets the velocity.

Reason Rack Extension

Pick a machine and play.

Choose the Handheld or the Console, pick a part and a shape, and play. Set Path to Line to hear the machine’s own output stage, and on the Console try a cartridge from Cart.

Rack Extension version history

Reboot version history.

The Rack Extension is in development and not yet in the Reason Shop.

0.5.2d1 Development build · 20 November 2026 · built

The Broadcast Desk panel, in walnut and brushed aluminium: four vertical faders for Attack, Decay, Sustain and Release, Pulse keys that show the chosen chip’s duties, and a scope of the current waveform. Boards, Cart channels and Bias move to the back (0.5.1d2).

Mono glides reach the lowest notes, Arp follows the last sounding pitch, held envelopes survive cartridge changes, and channels that match share their oversampling work, so the engine costs less (0.5.0 to 0.5.2).

0.4.0d2 In development · 18 November 2026 · not yet built

The panel laid out again with balanced spacing. Path is a row of keys under Cart, and Oversampling has its own section on the back.

0.4.0d1 In development · 18 November 2026 · not yet built

Rebuilt as one voice on two machines, the Handheld and the Console with every model and cartridge; the PSG, the Micro and the second layer are gone, and Double stays. Attack, Decay and Release are now times, every part plays at one loudness, and choosing a machine no longer thumps. A new 8U panel. Patches and songs from earlier versions do not carry over.

0.2.3f1 Release candidate · 17 November 2026 · built, created in Reason’s test host

Four machines on two layers. Remote names written for people, Oversampling on the Remote chart, and a Note On lamp that follows the keys.

0.2.0d2 In development · 17 November 2026 · not yet built

The first panel: four machines drawn in pixel art as the selector, a screen for each layer that draws its shape, and the machine’s audio path on the back.

0.1.0d1 In development · 17 November 2026 · not yet built

The first Rack adapter: the engine’s controls, 16 factory patches, the wheels, the sustain pedal and Note and Gate CV. No panel yet.
