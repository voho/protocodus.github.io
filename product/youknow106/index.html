Circuit-modelled DCO polysynth

YouKnow106

A six-voice DCO polysynth whose voice architecture is modelled block by block on the 1984 Roland Juno-106, from its integer-divided note timers through its four-pole transconductor filter to its uncompanded bucket-brigade chorus.

Inside the instrument

Block by block,
source by source.

The panel keeps the reference instrument’s control inventory and its reading order, with the plug-in additions placed next to the hardware families they belong to.

01

All 128 factory tones

The original 128 tone-memory states are included in the physical instrument’s order, each as the hardware’s complete 18-byte state with no corrective gain, hidden EQ or per-preset rebalancing. The bytes were mechanically decoded and cross-checked with zero mismatches against three public archives.

02

Four chorus states

Two uncompanded 256-stage MN3009 delay lines are driven anti-phase by one triangle, at rates derived from the instrument’s own oscillator circuit. Off, I, II and I+II are all available live and in the session; the original’s owner manual says I and II cannot be used together, so I+II is a plug-in mode and hardware-format patch files save it as II.

03

Calibrated self-oscillating filter

The four-pole filter is solved directly as its continuous nonlinear equations, with cutoff anchored on Roland’s own service calibration point where converter code 6272 self-oscillates at 248 Hz, which the model predicts within a cent rather than fitting.

04

Unit Character and Aging

Unit Character scales every modelled tolerance from calibrated-nominal at 0 percent, through the 100 percent default described as matching real hardware, to exaggerated at 200 percent. Seeds are fixed, so the same patch renders identically every launch. Aging sits beside it and drifts each voice’s filter trim flat by its own share of up to a quarter tone while lifting the noise source by up to 3.5 dB.

What it does not do

Stated plainly.

It models no pitch drift and no inter-voice detune, so six voices on one key are always exactly in tune and unison carries no detune generator, no mains ripple and no velocity from the modelled keybed. It also deliberately leaves out mechanisms whose magnitude the sources cannot fix, including chorus wet-mute click and leakage, high-pass mode-change transients, converter charge injection, and the common output amplifier’s untrimmed even-order distortion.

YouKnow106 is an independent original implementation, not affiliated with or licensed by Roland Corporation, and it contains no firmware, ROM data, samples or captured audio. It does include the original 128 factory tone-memory states as functional 18-byte parameter data, independently decoded and checksum-verified; no Roland Cloud content was extracted. Its panel follows that instrument’s functional geometry and 1980s colour vocabulary while retaining independent branding, typography and project-drawn controls.
