Physically modelled acoustic guitar

Acustra

A guitar that responds from the picking hand to the room. Six physical steel strings exchange energy through a measurement-derived bridge and body. Play fingerstyle, picked lines or voiced strums from your MIDI keyboard, then hear them through microphones, a modelled piezo pickup and preamp, or a blend of both. Every note is synthesised in real time.

  • Six steel strings
  • Three picking styles
  • Five tunings

In development; no build is available yet. Planned for release as VST®3 and standalone for macOS, Windows and Linux, with Audio Unit on macOS.

The instrument

Six strings. One resonant body.

Each pluck starts a physical string model. The six strings share a measured bridge and guitar body, so ringing strings respond to the next note you play. No recorded note samples are used.

  • Finger, pick or thumb

    Choose three distinct ways to play the strings. Move the pluck position, soften or firm up the touch, and use velocity to shape the attack and brightness. Repeated strokes vary within a bounded force range.

  • Voiced chords and strums

    Grouped keyboard chords are voiced using six strings, fret position and a four-finger grip, then strummed across them. A strum within a dotted eighth of the previous one at the host tempo is the hand’s return and alternates direction; after a longer gap the hand starts again with a downstroke. Keyswitches C0, C#0 and D0 latch downstrokes, upstrokes or that automatic rule. Gather Chords combines keys pressed within 30 ms into one strum, adding the same latency.

  • Shape and material

    Explore Parlor, Auditorium, Dreadnought and Jumbo body shapes, with Spruce, Mahogany and Maple material directions. Start from the Dreadnought, Auditorium or Parlor construction preset, then adjust any construction control to make your own guitar.

  • Five tunings

    Play in Standard, Drop D, DADGAD, Open G or Half-step down. Retuning changes each string’s tension and playable range. Acustra plays from the lowest open string to the twentieth fret of the highest, MIDI notes 40 to 84 in Standard, and ignores notes outside that range. The C0, C#0 and D0 keyswitches, notes 24 to 26, sit below it and never sound.

  • Expression under your hands

    Use the modulation wheel for vibrato and CC2 for bridge-hand damping. The hand keeps a consistent decay rate through slides, bends and pressure changes. The pitch wheel slides the fretting hand; lower-zone MPE adds per-string bends, pluck position and pressure-scaled vibrato. The Sustain switch lets released notes ring on every channel, beside the CC64 pedal. Explicit CC65/CC84 transitions connect notes on a ringing string, with brief fret friction; key-up velocity shapes damping and release noise.

  • Microphones, piezo and room

    Choose stereo microphones, a mono microphone or an under-saddle piezo through its modelled preamp. Blend the piezo beneath the microphones or send it to a separate output. Room adds a small studio’s reflections to the microphones, leaving the piezo dry.

Physical modelling

The attack, the sustain, the space.

The tone comes from interacting models of the strings, the hands, the guitar and its captures. Change how you play and those same models change the attack, the ringing partials and the decay.

  • Strings with stiffness and loss

    Each steel string carries travelling waves in two planes of vibration. Stiffness spreads its upper partials, frequency-dependent loss lets them fade at different rates, and the tension increase after a pluck adds a brief pitch glide. String Age increases the losses, especially in the wound bass strings.

  • The hand releasing the string

    The attack models contact width, pluck position and the string slipping around the picking edge. Harder strokes release faster and brighter; a pick, finger and thumb set different contact and release behaviours. Moving the hand changes the harmonics at their source.

  • A bridge that connects the strings

    A passive bridge model transfers energy between the strings and body, including motion that rocks the saddle. Ringing strings can respond sympathetically to new notes. The top also springs back when the picking hand lets go, adding weight from its low resonances to the attack.

  • Measured resonances, adjustable construction

    Bridge and microphone responses start from guitar measurements. Body Shape re-couples the measured body’s air and top resonances for four published body sizes. Body Material changes the top’s density and stiffness, moving those resonances, the radiation and the bridge loading together; the measured damping is retained.

  • A pickup and its electronics

    The piezo starts with force at the saddle, then models saddle loading, the piezo element, cable capacitance and a battery-powered preamp circuit, including its filters, protection diodes and op-amp limits. It gives you a separate direct capture to blend or process.

  • Microphones in a small studio

    Measured radiation paths provide stereo and mono microphone views; their voicing is informed by real flat-top recordings. Room adds modelled early reflections and a diffuse decay around those microphones. Adjust Stereo Width for space while the separate piezo stays dry.

Playing it

What each MIDI message plays.

Conventional MIDI is the default: all sixteen channels are independent. A lower MPE zone starts when channel 1 receives the MPE configuration message (RPN 6).

Acustra 1.3.2 MIDI implementation
MessageWhat it plays
Note On and OffPlucks within the tuning’s range, from the lowest open string to the twentieth fret of the highest; other notes are ignored. Velocity shapes the attack and the strum’s pick speed. Key-up velocity shapes damping and release noise.
C0, C#0, D0 (notes 24–26)Keyswitches on any channel: every strum a downstroke, every strum an upstroke, or the automatic rule. Each holds until the next keyswitch or Panic and never sounds.
Pitch bendSlides the fretting hand. RPN 0 sets the range, 2 semitones by default and up to 96.
CC1 ModulationFretting-hand vibrato through string tension, across the instrument. Open strings stay unbent.
CC2 BreathBridge-hand damping: the picking hand’s heel resting by the saddle.
CC64 SustainHolds notes released on its channel until pedal-up. The panel’s Sustain switch does the same on every channel.
CC65 and CC84A connected finger transition: switch CC65 on, send the held source pitch on CC84, then the destination Note On, up to an octave away.
MPE lower zoneEach member channel bends its own string by tension, 48 semitones by default. CC74 sets its pluck position; channel pressure scales its vibrato depth.
CC126 and CC127Mono Mode On with a value of 6 on channel 1 gives channels 1–6 one string each; Poly Mode On returns to shared string allocation.

In your setup

Choose the guitar. Find your touch.

Start with Guitar, Picking and Capture, then adjust the body, string age and playing controls. Release Noise and Room sit on the panel with the other sound controls; a new instance starts with Release Noise at 10%, Piezo Mix at 20% and Room at 50%. Keep the microphone sound, blend in the piezo, or process the two captures separately in your DAW. Save the complete setup with your project or a host preset.

Reason edition

The same guitar in the rack.

The Acustra Rack Extension plays the same engine and playing rules through a thin Reason adapter. It is planned for Reason 14 or later and is not available yet. Its 1.2.11f1 source carries the 1.3.2 instrument; 1.2.8f1 is the last candidate built with the Jukebox SDK and created in Reason Recon, and 1.2.9f1 to 1.2.11f1 await an SDK build and Reason validation.

  • Four tunings, two woods

    The panel offers Standard, Drop D, DADGAD and Open G, with Spruce and Mahogany. Songs that stored Maple or Half-step down still load them.

  • No MIDI channels

    Reason sends no MIDI channels, MPE or release velocity, so the rack edition has no MPE, string-per-channel mode, CC65/CC84 transitions, or per-channel bend and sustain. Gather Chords is absent because a Rack Extension declares its latency once.

  • Added for Reason

    Monophonic Note and Gate CV inputs, a Bridge Hand knob working with CC2, a Bend Range knob from 1 to 12 semitones, and 1×, 2× or 4× oversampling on the rear panel, at a fixed 49-sample latency that Reason compensates.
