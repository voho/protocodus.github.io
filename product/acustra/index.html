Acustra

Acustra models the contact of finger, pick or thumb, the energy passing between strings and body, and the microphones and piezo that capture it. Physical modelling

The whole instrument responds.

The sound emerges from interacting physical models. Plucking, damping and changing the construction affect the vibrating strings and resonant body that generate each note.

  • Steel strings with stiffness and loss

    Six stiff-string waveguides follow vibration in two transverse directions. Material-dependent stiffness spreads the upper partials, while frequency-dependent losses give each string its own evolving decay. String Age changes the loss of brightness and sustain.

  • A hand behind every attack

    Finger, Pick and Thumb use different contact widths, release behaviour and playing positions. The model considers string compliance, wrist posture and the contact left by repeated notes, and every contact lands a few cents off, as a fretting finger does. Pluck Position and Touch change the excitation itself, including the balance of string motion.

  • Energy shared through the bridge

    All six strings connect to one passive bridge, including the rocking motion of the saddle. The body loads the strings while the strings drive the body, so unplayed strings can ring sympathetically and new notes interact with an existing tail.

  • A measured foundation for the body

    Bridge and radiation modes start from published guitar measurements. Parlor, Auditorium, Dreadnought and Jumbo reshape the coupled air and soundboard resonances. Spruce and Mahogany use representative density and directional stiffness to change modal mass and response. The player’s torso and forearm damp the body gently, as on a held guitar.

  • An under-saddle pickup circuit

    The piezo starts with the force arriving at the saddle. Saddle loading, the piezo element, cable capacitance and preamp input form its electrical response, followed by modelled filtering and nonlinear circuit limits. This creates a distinct direct signal for blending or separate processing.

  • Microphones and room acoustics

    Stereo and mono captures combine measurement-derived radiation with projections of microphone position. Room adds early reflections and a diffuse studio decay to the microphone signal. The direct piezo remains dry, preserving a separate capture for your rack.

Playing the model

A guitar performance from your keyboard.

The playing system assigns notes to six physical strings and a reachable fret position. Your performance determines the contact, the damping and the movement of the fretting hand.

  • Chords that become strums

    Notes that start together are voiced as a hand shape across up to six strings. Three or more simultaneous notes become a strum, with velocity setting pick speed. Keyswitches select downstrokes, upstrokes or tempo-aware automatic alternation.

  • Four tunings, one physical system

    Choose Standard, Drop D, DADGAD or Open G on the panel. Retuning changes string tension at a fixed scale length. The six-string display shows held notes, release tails and sympathetic activity across the twenty-fret range.

  • Damping, vibrato and sustain

    Use the modulation wheel for fretting-hand vibrato, Breath/CC2 for palm pressure, and the pitch wheel for bends with an adjustable range. A sustain pedal, the front Sustain switch or its keyswitch can let released strings ring. Release Noise follows the damping contact.

In the rack

One guitar. Two capture paths.

Send stereo or mono microphones to the main outputs, blend in the piezo, or select the pickup alone. Piezo Blend Invert changes the pickup’s polarity against the microphones, while a dedicated Piezo output lets you process it independently with Reason devices. Note and Gate CV add a monophonic playing path alongside sequencer notes.

Eight factory patches cover fingerstyle, picked and thumbed playing, alternate tunings and stage-ready piezo capture, each with a subtle 10% Room setting. Save your construction, playing controls and capture settings as a Reason patch. Rear-panel Detune adds up to ±20 cents of per-note pitch variation. The 1×, 2× and 4× processing choices apply when the instrument is silent; factory patches use 2×.

Rack Extension version history

Acustra release history.

Version 1.9.0 is the current Rack Extension in the Reason Shop. Acustra was first released there as 1.3.2 on 20 October 2026. Get the release or download a trial through the Reason Shop.

1.9.0 Current Reason Shop version

A gentler velocity response, more consistent strums at a fixed velocity, and subtle damping of the body by the player’s torso and forearm. Each contact now lands with its own small pitch variation, and hammer-ons, pull-offs, releases and the microphones’ room response are refined.

Retuning is faster to calculate, and the eight factory patches and panel artwork are updated to match.

1.3.2 20 October 2026 · First Reason Shop release

The first Reason Shop release of Acustra: six physically modelled steel strings with Finger, Pick and Thumb playing, four tunings, four body shapes with spruce or mahogany tops, microphone and piezo capture with a dedicated Piezo output, and eight factory patches.

Includes per-note Detune, Piezo Blend Invert, sustain and strum-direction controls, Note/Gate CV, and selectable 1×, 2× or 4× processing. Factory patches start at 2× with 10% Room.
