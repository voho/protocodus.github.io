Acustra

A guitar that responds from the picking hand to the room. Six physical steel strings exchange energy through a measurement-derived bridge and body. Play fingerstyle, picked lines or voiced strums, then hear them through microphones, a modelled piezo pickup and preamp, or a blend of both. Every note is synthesised in real time.

The instrument

Six strings. One resonant body.

Each pluck starts a physical string model. The six strings share a measured bridge and guitar body, so ringing strings respond to the next note you play. No recorded note samples are used.

  • Finger, pick or thumb

    Choose three distinct ways to play the strings. Move the pluck position, soften or firm up the touch, and use velocity to shape the attack and brightness. Picking contact, wrist posture and the memory of recent strokes shape each new note.

  • Chords that follow the hand

    Play notes together to form a six-string hand shape. Finger chords of three or four strings use coordinated plucks; Pick pairs sweep across two strings and larger chords strum. Alternate strokes bring lighter edges, timing variation and contact with skipped strings.

  • Four bodies, two woods

    Explore Parlor, Auditorium, Dreadnought and Jumbo, with Spruce or Mahogany soundboard character. These constructions reshape one shared physical guitar, changing the bridge loading, body resonances and radiation together.

  • Four tunings

    Play in Standard, Drop D, DADGAD or Open G. Retuning changes each string’s tension and playable range, from its lowest open string to the twentieth fret of the highest. In Standard tuning, that is MIDI notes 40 to 84.

  • Expression under your hands

    Use the modulation wheel for vibrato, the pitch wheel for bends and Mute or breath control for bridge-hand damping. Sustain from the front switch, pedal or keyswitch lets released strings ring. Fretted releases follow the lifting fingertip with a gentle fall in pitch.

  • Microphones, piezo and room

    Choose stereo microphones, a mono microphone or an under-saddle piezo through its modelled preamp. Blend the piezo beneath the microphones or route it to a separate output. Room adds a small studio’s reflections to the microphones while the pickup stays dry.

Physical modelling

The attack, the sustain, the space.

The tone comes from interacting models of the strings, the hands, the guitar and its captures. Change how you play and those same models change the attack, the ringing partials and the decay.

  • Strings with stiffness and loss

    Each steel string carries travelling waves in two planes of vibration. Stiffness spreads its upper partials, frequency-dependent loss lets them fade at different rates, and the tension increase after a pluck adds a brief pitch glide. String Age increases the losses, especially in the wound bass strings.

  • The hand releasing the string

    The attack models contact width, pluck position and the string slipping around the picking edge. Harder strokes release faster and brighter; a pick, finger and thumb set different contact and release behaviours. Moving the hand changes the harmonics at their source.

  • A bridge that connects the strings

    A passive bridge model transfers energy between the strings and body, including motion that rocks the saddle. Ringing strings can respond sympathetically to new notes. The top also springs back when the picking hand lets go, adding weight from its low resonances to the attack.

  • Measured resonances, adjustable construction

    Bridge and microphone responses start from guitar measurements. Guitar changes the coupled air and top resonances; Wood changes modal mass and directional stiffness. The four body shapes are modelled variations of that measured foundation.

  • A pickup and its electronics

    The piezo starts with force at the saddle, then models saddle loading, the piezo element, cable capacitance and a battery-powered preamp circuit, including its filters, protection diodes and op-amp limits. It gives you a direct capture to blend or process separately.

  • Microphones in a small studio

    Measurement-informed radiation paths provide stereo and mono microphone views. Room adds modelled early reflections and a diffuse decay around those microphones. Adjust Stereo Width for space while the separate piezo stays dry.

Playing it

A fretting hand and a picking hand.

Play notes together from Reason’s sequencer for voiced chords, use keyswitches to guide the stroke, and shape the phrase with the wheels and pedal. The six-string display follows held, released and sympathetic strings.

Acustra Rack Extension performance controls
ControlWhat it plays
Notes and velocityPlucks within the selected tuning’s range. Velocity shapes the attack and stroke speed; simultaneous notes form a playable hand shape.
C0, C#0, D0 · MIDI 24–26Latch downstrokes, latch upstrokes, or return to automatic direction. In automatic mode, nearby strums alternate at the song tempo and restart with a downstroke after a pause.
D#0 · MIDI 27Toggle sustain independently of the front Sustain switch and CC64 pedal. Released strings remain held while any of these sources is active.
Pitch and modulation wheelsBend with the selected 1–12 semitone range, or add fretting-hand vibrato.
Mute and CC2 BreathPress the picking hand at the bridge to damp the strings. The stronger of the knob and breath-controller values sets the pressure.
Note and Gate CVPlay monophonically from a CV source. Note sets pitch; Gate triggers a pluck and its level sets velocity.

Octave labels vary between keyboards; the MIDI note numbers identify the keyswitches. The Rack Extension uses Reason’s note and performance controls; the plug-in’s MPE, per-channel controls and chord-gathering switch are not part of this edition.

In the rack

Microphones together. Pickup on its own.

Route the main stereo capture and the independent mono Piezo output to separate effects or mixer channels. Rear Detune adds gentle pitch variation per played tone. Piezo Blend Invert changes only the pickup blended under the microphones.

Find your touch

Start with Dreadnought Steel.

Eight factory patches cover fingerstyle, picked and thumbed playing, alternate tunings, a muted guitar and piezo capture. Dreadnought Steel starts with Finger, Spruce and Standard tuning, 20% Piezo Mix and 10% Room. Adjust the construction and playing controls, then save your sound as a Reason patch.

Rear Oversampling offers 1×, 2× and 4×, with 2× selected by default. Higher settings use more CPU; choose 1× when your session needs more headroom. A change applies after the instrument becomes silent or after Reset Audio.
