The ukulele

Four short strings on a small body.

Each string is modelled as a stiff, vibrating string that loses energy and drives the bridge and the body. Change the body, the strings or the way you play, and the attack, the brightness and the length of each note change with it.

  • Re-entrant tuning

    Standard tuning puts a high G on the fourth string, so chords sit close and bright. Low G swaps in a lower fourth string, and D tuning raises every string a whole tone.

  • Strings and bodies

    Nylon and fluorocarbon sets each have their own gauges, tension and damping. Soprano, concert and tenor bodies follow published dimensions and scale lengths, in spruce, mahogany, koa or cedar. Their upper resonances are spread as densely as those counted on a real ukulele top, so high notes no longer catch on a single harsh resonance.

  • Strum, pick, thumb or finger

    Strum sweeps a finger over the neck, where a ukulele is strummed: down with the nail, back up with the pad. Finger and thumb pluck with soft skin, and the thumb strums back up on its nail; the pick gives melodies a sharper edge. Keyswitches latch downstrokes, upstrokes or automatic strumming, and holding F0 turns each stroke into a muted chuck that also stops strings left ringing by the sustain pedal.

  • Mic, piezo and room

    Hear it through a stereo mic pair, a mono mic or an under-saddle piezo, then set the body, room and width. A separate piezo output carries the pickup on its own for its own processing.

  • Ready for the mix

    Every patch plays at the same level on Reason’s meter, and the bodies, woods and captures stay within about 1.5 dB of it, so you can compare sounds without riding the fader. A limiter on the main and piezo outputs keeps the hardest chords out of the red.

In the rack

Choose the ukulele. Then play it.

The front panel chooses the instrument on the left (body, wood, strings and tuning) and plays it on the right: technique, pluck position, touch, palm mute, string age and capture. A fretboard shows where each string is playing and how strongly it rings.

Turn the rack around for oversampling, bend range, detune, release noise and the piezo’s blend polarity. A stereo main output, a direct piezo output, Note and Gate CV inputs, automation and Remote mapping connect Aculele to the rest of your rack.

Reason Rack Extension

Start with a strum.

Seven factory patches cover the three bodies and tunings, from a concert strum and a soprano in D tuning to tenor fingerstyle and a stage piezo sound. Play chords from any keyboard: Aculele chooses a string and fret for each note, in the shapes of a ukulele chord book, and a three-note strum still sounds all four strings.

Rack Extension version history

Aculele release history.

Version 0.4.64, released on 17 November 2026, is the current Rack Extension in the Reason Shop. The latest development build is 0.4.70f3.

0.4.70f3 Release candidate · 17 November 2026 · built, created in Reason’s test host

Soft plucks lose the tick at their start (0.4.68), and the engine takes about an eighth less CPU on the development machine (0.4.69 and 0.4.70); Reason’s own CPU figure is not measured.

Quality can be mapped with Remote, every control has a readable Remote name, the Note On lamp follows the keys, and Mute and Solo in the sequencer silence the device.

0.4.67d1 Development build · 15 November 2026 · built, created in Reason’s test host, not yet played there

Upstrokes now tilt their level about the middle of the ukulele’s four strings rather than six, and the strings’ stretch is computed more economically, both chosen by ear. The engine takes about a fifth less CPU on the development machine; Reason’s own CPU figure is not measured.

0.4.64 Reason Shop release · 17 November 2026 · device 0.4.64f2

Now in the Reason Shop. Reason’s package check refused 0.4.64d1 because it dropped the Oversampling control’s MIDI and Remote mapping. In 0.4.64f2 that property stays on its old mapping and does nothing, and the rear 1x, 2x and 4x buttons set a new Quality setting; a song or patch saved at 2x or 4x opens at 1x. The sound is unchanged.

0.4.64d1 Development build · 10 November 2026

When a pluck lets go, the top gives back the whole force the saddle held, and open strings ring on longer; both were chosen by ear. The body sounds the same at every sample rate, and notes play straight away after a body, string, tuning or string-age change.

0.4.47d1 Development build · 8 November 2026

Engine refinements from the canonical Aculele 0.4.47. Patches are unchanged.

0.4.45f1 Release candidate · 7 November 2026

Spruce, mahogany, koa and cedar tops, nail plucks under Finger, no two plucks quite alike, and fret shifts that slide between notes.

0.4.39d1 Earlier accepted device · 3 November 2026 build

Picked and nail-plucked notes now start with a consistent polarity across neighbouring velocities. Gradually increasing the velocity gives a smoother change in attack.

0.4.38d1 Development build · 3 November 2026

Refines each string’s nut-to-saddle length compensation and the way the bridge drives the body. Corrects state handling so resets and repeated playing stay consistent.

Reason Shop publication is separate from this development build.
