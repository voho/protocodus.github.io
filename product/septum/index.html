Two-tone virtual analog synthesizer

Septum

Two tones. Seven saws.

Wide supersaw leads, moving pads and a second sound under your hands. Septum models the SH-201’s synthesis architecture with two complete tones and ten voices. Play one tone, layer two, or split them across the keyboard, then bring a phrase to life with the arpeggiator, modulation delay and reverb.

  • Ten voices
  • Upper & Lower tones
  • Seven-saw oscillator
Ask about Septum ↗ Explore the features ↓

In development. VST3 and standalone for macOS, Windows and Linux, with Audio Unit on macOS.

The instrument

A whole voice, twice.

Each tone has two oscillators, a mixer, a multimode filter, three envelopes and two LFOs. Their shared effects bring the two sounds together.

  • Seven saws, one broad sound

    The Super Saw spreads seven detuned sawtooth oscillators. Mix it with the second oscillator, explore oscillator sync and ring modulation, or turn to the feedback oscillator for a different edge.

  • Layer or split

    Single mode gives one tone all ten voices. Dual layers Upper and Lower with five voices each; Split places them on separate sides of the keyboard. Build a pad beneath a lead or give each hand its own sound.

  • Movement and space

    Independent envelopes and LFOs shape each tone. The modulation-delay and reverb chain adds width and a tail, while the arpeggiator offers a 32 × 16 pattern grid.

  • A filter for incoming audio

    A separate four-type audio filter processes the audio input. Bring an external signal into the instrument alongside the tones and explore its filter response.

In your setup

Build the patch around your playing.

Choose Single, Dual or Split, shape Upper and Lower, then save the complete sound in your DAW project or host preset. Septum is an independent implementation; it contains no Roland firmware, ROM data, recorded samples or factory patch data.
