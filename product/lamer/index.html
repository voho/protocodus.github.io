From subtle to spectacularly lossy

Two codecs. Your kind of character.

Both codecs transform audio into frequency components, allocate a finite bit budget and quantize the result before decoding it back to sound. Masking and bandwidth decisions depend on the signal, so a transient, a sustained chord and a noisy texture erode differently. Lower the bitrate to expose that behavior.

  • A changing spectral budget.

    Bitrate sets the coding target from 8 to 256 kbps. Move from a lighter touch to pronounced codec texture, with blurred transients, softened highs and metallic edges. Smoothed changes let the character evolve across a phrase.

  • Generation loss, one pass at a time.

    Each round sends the audio through another genuine encode/decode cycle. Start with one pass, then stack up to four generations to deepen the transformation and uncover new movement in the details.

  • Shape what goes in.

    Low-pass and high-pass filters sit before the first codec round. Both span 20 Hz to 20 kHz with 12 dB/octave slopes and an Off position. Isolate a narrow band or leave the whole spectrum for the encoder to work with.

  • Keep the impact.

    Mix blends the processed sound with the unfiltered, latency-aligned original. Keep the attack and weight of your source while adding texture underneath, or go fully wet for a complete transformation.

Three places to start

Give a familiar sound a new past.

A little worn, a little distant, or thoroughly transformed. Start with a source you know and follow the details that emerge.

  1. 01

    Texture behind a beat

    Try one round at 64 kbps with a modest wet blend. Lower Bitrate until hats and room sound gain character, then use Mix to keep the kick and snare attack in focus.

  2. 02

    A distant vocal or pad

    Narrow the input with LP and HP, add a second or third round, and compare the encoders. Blend the result beneath the original for a hazy layer.

  3. 03

    A moving transition

    Automate Bitrate downward and add rounds across a build. Bring it back at the next section, using filter sweeps and Mix to control how far the sound travels.

A few useful details

Optional Bitrate Crossfade

For both MP3 and OGG, this rear switch blends the old and new bitrate results during changes. Try it on a held chord or an exposed sweep. It starts Off and uses more CPU when enabled during bitrate changes. Your choice is saved with the song; the switch is manual and cannot be automated.

Changing encoders

MP3/OGG selection is manual, saved with your song and available through Remote. Switching formats fades out while the new codec gets ready, then fades back in. A brief gap is expected; encoder selection cannot be sequencer-automated.

Delay and dry/wet alignment

Real codec processing introduces delay. Lamer reports it to Reason and aligns its internal dry/wet paths. At 48 kHz, one round is approximately 46.7 ms for MP3 or 22.7 ms for OGG. More rounds increase delay and processing work. The Bitrate control uses a short 1 ms ramp; codec buffering determines when the resulting sound reaches the output.

What the bitrate number means

Bitrate values are coding targets. At very low settings, valid frames and packets can require more bits than the selected target; the control still reduces the useful coding budget and changes the sound.
