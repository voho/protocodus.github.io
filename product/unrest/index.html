Unrest

Finds the narrow peaks that make a sound harsh, whistling or boxy and turns down only the excess, for as long as it is there, leaving the rest of the sound alone.

The suppressor

Cut the resonance, keep the note.

A fixed notch keeps cutting after the note has moved on. Unrest listens for peaks that stand above the smooth shape of the sound around them, such as a ringing snare, a whistling guitar, a sharp vocal or a boxy room, and follows the music, so the notes around a resonance keep their body.

  • Strength and Max cut

    Strength sets how readily it cuts and Max cut how deep, from 1 to 36 dB. Sharpness narrows the cuts from broad taming to thin notches, and Resolution sets how finely it looks.

  • Attack, Release and Guard

    Set how fast it cuts and recovers. Guard eases off on drum hits, plucks and consonants so their attack stays intact.

  • Soft or Hard, and where it cuts

    Soft cuts gently for a mix bus or a vocal; Hard cuts deeper for harsh cymbals and cleanup. Low cut and High cut keep the kick’s weight or the air out of reach, and Tilt leans the cutting toward boxy low mids or toward sibilance.

  • Stereo and sidechain

    Work in L/R or M/S, with Ch indep from fully linked to free, so the centre can be cleaned without pumping the sides. Ext sidechain lets another track decide where it cuts, and Delta listen plays only what Unrest removes.

  • Linear or Low-lat

    Linear makes zero-phase cuts with 2048 samples of latency at 1x, which Reason compensates; Low-lat uses a filter bank that adds none at 1x. Oversampling at 2x or 4x reduces aliasing.

  • Colour after the cleanup

    Iron, Tube or Tape adds transformer weight, triode warmth or tape saturation, matched in level, with Drive setting how much. Mix blends the delay-compensated dry sound with the processed one, and Output trims the level.

In the rack

Key it from another track.

The display shows the input spectrum and the cut across frequency beside gain reduction and level meters. On the back, a stereo sidechain input sits beside the main pair: cable a lead vocal into a backing bus’s Unrest and switch on Ext sidechain, so the bus is cut where the vocal needs room.

In tests Unrest uses about 0.7 % of one core in Low-lat at 1x and up to about 2.5 % in Linear at 4x with Tape, at 48 kHz, on the development machine; Reason’s own CPU figure is not measured.

Reason Rack Extension

Insert it and turn up Strength.

Find Unrest among the effects in Reason’s browser and insert it on a track, a bus or the master; it opens on Init. Turn Strength up until the harshness goes, switch on Delta listen to hear exactly what is being cut, then narrow it with Sharpness, Low cut and High cut.

Rack Extension version history

Unrest version history.

The Rack Extension is in development and not yet in the Reason Shop.

0.1.0f2 Release candidate · 16 November 2026 · built, created in Reason’s test host

Filed under EQ and Dynamics in Reason’s browser. The sound, patches and controls are 0.1.0f1’s; the back prints the new number.

0.1.0f1 Release candidate · 16 November 2026

The release revision of 0.1.0d4, with the same sound, patches and controls.

0.1.0d4 Development build · 15 November 2026

More even detection: smooth, broadband material passes almost untouched, the low end and fundamentals are protected, and Soft cuts more gently in quiet passages. The factory patches were refreshed.

0.1.0d3 Development build · 15 November 2026

A new 6U panel with the display, the main controls and the saturation in separate sections, and on the back a signal flow over the jacks.

0.1.0d1 Development build · 15 November 2026

The first Rack Extension: the Linear and Low-lat engines, oversampling, Off, Iron, Tube and Tape, Guard, Tilt, Low and High cut, L/R and M/S, Delta, the external sidechain and 16 patches.
