Circuit-modelled monophonic synthesizer

Ghostar

A monophonic synthesizer modelled block by block from the documentation of the Crumar Spirit, a 1983 instrument designed by Jim Scott, Tom Rhea and Bob Moog, built around its series dual filter.

Inside the synth

A white-box model,
with its sources named.

Every law in the documentation is tagged by where it came from: the manuals and datasheets, a calculation from them, or a choice made inside a range the sources do not fix. The parts still uncertain are listed openly.

01

The series dual filter

A lower multimode section, which can be out, overdrive, a parametric boost or a highpass, slides against a 12 or 24 dB upper lowpass, with the overdrive sitting between the two so the upper filter re-filters its distortion. FORMANT tracking disconnects the lower filter from keyboard CV, X and Y modulation, the filter envelope and the pedal, freezing its peak as a fixed formant.

02

Two parallel audio paths

One path runs the mixer through both filters into a VCA driven by the loudness envelope; the other runs its own mixer, including the ring modulator, through a VCA controlled by Shaper Y and a passive BRIGHTNESS tone control. The seventh demo splits them left and right, with an enveloped bass against a free-running ring-and-noise drone.

03

MOD X and Shaper Y

MOD X offers six sources: LFO triangle, LFO square, sample-and-hold random, sample-and-hold Y, continuous red noise, and the selected Osc B waveform, with RIPPLE, ARPEGGIO and LEAP arpeggiator modes on the same clock. Shaper Y is a variable-rate integrator whose SHAPE control moves the split between rise and fall without changing the total time.

04

Eleven Sound Charts

The modelled hardware shipped no presets, so the bank opens with the eleven drawn panel settings from its manual, from the deliberately silent Preparatory Pattern to the Inverted Guitar, with seventeen level-matched Ghostar Programs behind them. Selecting any program writes the whole panel, so each one can be read as well as played.

What it does not do

Stated plainly.

No calibration-grade hardware data set exists, since no filter, envelope, noise-level or control-depth captures of a Spirit have been published, so a number of constants remain voiced or open and are listed under Known gaps with the measurement that would close each. No oscillator drift is applied, the plug-in contains no firmware, ROM data, samples or captured audio, and coincident keyboard and arpeggiator LFO-reset arbitration is not implemented.

Ghostar is an independent original implementation, not affiliated with, endorsed by, or licensed by Crumar or its successors. Its name and livery are its own.
