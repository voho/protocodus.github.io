Drumalor

A thirteen-voice drum instrument that generates every hit as you play it, from oscillators, noise, filters, envelopes, resonators and circuit-inspired nonlinear shaping, rather than playing back recorded samples.

Inside the kit

No samples.
No ROM images.

Every hit is generated in the audio callback. What you change is the drum, not a playback rate.

01

Thirteen synthesised voices

Kick, snare, clap, closed and open hats, ride, crash, three toms, shaker and two percussion voices, each generated in the audio callback from oscillators, noise, filters, envelopes, resonators and circuit-inspired nonlinear shaping.

02

Velocity plays the drum

MIDI velocity is a timbre control across the struck voices, not only a level control, so a soft stroke is duller as well as quieter. The snare's wires only rattle while the resonant head lifts them off their resting contact, so hard strikes buzz and soft ones stay dry.

03

Articulations, not extra voices

The snare answers notes 38, 40 and 37 for head, rimshot and cross-stick, and the metallic voices answer notes 44, 53, 52 and 55 for hi-hat foot chick, ride bell, china and splash. Hi-hat pedal position follows CC 4 continuously, CC 88 carries high-resolution velocity, and aftertouch chokes ringing cymbals progressively with pressure.

04

Kit mixer and bus

Per-voice Level and Pan, choke groups A, B and C, Kit Humanise, Kit Bleed, Bus Drive, Bus Compression and an Output control sit alongside each voice's character, pitch and decay controls: seven automatable controls for each of the thirteen voices, or 91, plus five kit controls, for 96 host parameters in total.

What it does not do

Stated plainly.

No room or ambience is modelled, and no recorded cymbal is used: the digital cymbal channel's tables are generated rather than embedded. There is no calibrated correspondence to any particular unit of the machines whose published circuit analyses the cymbal channels follow.

Drumalor's voices are circuit-inspired behavioral models, not a claim of component-for-component emulation of a TR-808, TR-909 or any other specific machine. It emulates no particular branded machine, embeds no sample, recording or ROM image, and is a modern behavioral design rather than a calibrated hardware replica.
